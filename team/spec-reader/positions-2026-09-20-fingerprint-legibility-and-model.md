# spec-reader position: Fingerprint legibility and model

## Brief

The page copy and specs make four false claims about how the fingerprint renders: that old Heat persists at the center while outer rings calm (the engine applies the opposite), that seven channels carry one signal each with none sharing (purity affects noise, turbulence is modulated by recency and maturity), that saturation drives color (implemented in packages/core/src/fingerprint-texture.ts, but undocumented in user-facing text), and that a stranger can read pillar identity from the shape alone (the reading test shows 71% without legend, with pillar confusion on Wen Zhao versus Father Anselm and Marcus Aurel versus Tom Reilly, and seven of seven single-mark notes report heat even for calm contributors).

## Trade-off penalty visibility

The Contributor Identity spec (section "The Trade-Offs Are Intentional", lines 56-64 in v1.1) and the page (line 290) both state that trade-offs make every fingerprint into a real shape and prevent a true circle. Neither tells the reader that this is VISIBLE. Certain axes get drawn shorter than they "should" because of their partner. The reading test measured this: Dolores Vance's 22-graduation Discourse was penalized to 70% and drawn fifth of six, producing the stranger's note "they don't tend to stay for a long back-and-forth; they make their case and move on." Discourse is most taxed (two partners), cutting hardest at Calibration and Magnanimity: the fair and careful read as not engaged. The penalty is real and visible but the specs leave its presence and direction unstated.

## Saturation channel

`docs/FINGERPRINT.md` line 60-62 already corrects itself: "The saturation channel does not exist in the engine." It was documented in the engine's own comment at line 175 ("purity drives base color saturation") but the code never uses purity for color, only for noise amplitude. The mistake is noted and the fix is shipped in `packages/core/src/fingerprint-texture.ts`. The old specs are just stale. Not a rendering falsehood, but a documentation-versus-code gap.

## Legibility to strangers

The reading test (lines 76-103 of the frame) shows the issue clearly. With legend, 90%. Without legend, 71% (still above chance, but far below passable for a "readable at a glance" claim). Without legend, readers failed exactly where pillar identity matters: they could not tell Calibration from Magnanimity, could not distinguish Marcus Aurel (argumentative) from Tom Reilly (argumentative about one topic), and could not pick out Wen Zhao (precise) from Father Anselm (generous). The page copy says "Select a contributor to see how their fingerprint reflects their actual engagement history" and later "if they look at it and understand the basics about fingerprint mechanics" (Dan's statement), but "understanding the basics" does not include learning to read the pillar geometry from angle alone. The shape alone carries tenure (size), topic count (color bands), and heat (texture). Pillar distinction requires the legend.

## Heat false positive

From the frame, lines 98-104: "Every mark carries a noise floor on purpose, and the same pixel wobble sits on smaller and smaller rings toward the centre. On Anselm it is 1.6 percent of the ring's radius at the rim and 9.6 percent at the core. The legend says wobble is heat, and so does the live page. Readers seeing a set found Anselm by comparison, 'the smoothest boundary on the sheet.' A stranger on a profile sees one mark." Father Anselm's record holds zero Heat and zero Stance. Seven of seven single-mark notes described heat. The collision is in the noise floor itself. Smaller rings have proportionally larger wobble, so the innermost rings look maximally turbulent no matter the tier history. The engine comment at line 500 says the base noise floor exists "which is why nothing renders as a clean curve", and it scales with ring size. A standalone fingerprint without comparison cannot distinguish noise floor from signal.

## Pilot claim on what specs and page stand

The Contributor Identity spec states the trade-offs "are not bugs" (line 64) and "a platform where everyone could max every pillar would be a platform where the pillars meant nothing". This is a normative position, not a description of what the user experiences. It does not address whether the penalty is visible, legible, or misread. `docs/FINGERPRINT.md` (lines 77-93) describes concavity as something "a viewer parses which axis caused it", assuming readability the reading test does not support. The page copy at line 290 states the trade-offs make a true circle "structurally impossible", which is true as mathematics, but does not explain that the result is an axis drawn shorter than its graduation count alone would suggest, and that a reader cannot see which axis is penalized without pillar knowledge.

On legibility to strangers: Dan's statement says "I want a person to get a feel for this users communication style quickly if they look at it and understand the basics about fingerprint mechanics." The reading test (line 49, line 83-86) distinguishes between legend and no-legend conditions. The specs nowhere state whether the "basics" include a legend or not. The page layout shows carousel at 330px with legend supplied under "The same six axes", then says (line 230) "Select a contributor to see how their fingerprint reflects their actual engagement history", and later (line 288) "How to Read a Fingerprint" with the title "Eight archetypes. Eight shapes." No where on the page does it say "the legend is required to read a pillar" or "on a shared card at byline size, the mark is unreadable without accompanying text."

## Rebuttal

The convener's corrections stand. The brief mislabeled the no-legend pillar confusions as "Calibration versus Magnanimity, Father Anselm versus Marcus Aurel" when the test shows Wen Zhao against Father Anselm (readers 2 and 3) and Marcus Aurel against Tom Reilly (reader 1). On the heat false positive, seven of seven single-mark notes describe heat, not "21 of 21" reader responses. On saturation, the position's body correctly notes it is "shipped in packages/core/src/fingerprint-texture.ts"; the brief overstated the gap. These corrections do not change the core findings: the specs and page teach a model of wobble-as-heat that produces false positives against contributors with zero heat record, and the reading test bears that out cleanly.

Two findings from other seats compound this finding. Legal: when a reader converts a mark into a false factual claim about a named person, the fingerprint becomes the platform's assertion. Philosopher: the page's showcase of the Reviser archetype (the pillar defined by change) renders with no heat or stance, contradicting the spec's stipulation that early heat should resolve. Both turn on the same collision: the legend teaches that wobble means heat; the engine adds wobble to every mark for legibility; the specs have no way to name this gap. Designer's noise-floor fix (ratio-capping at the rim) is precise and measurable, and it fixes half the problem, but the core issue lives in what Dialecta teaches the public about how to read heat. FINGERPRINT.md (lines 77-93) assumes "a viewer parses which axis caused" concavity; Dialecta_Contributor_Identity.md assumes a reader will see the trade-off penalty as earned; the page assumes wobble tells heat. None of these state that they require the legend or that the engine adds wobble everywhere. That gap is where the false positives live.

The specs also do not state whether a stranger reading a profile fingerprint alone can use pillar knowledge at all. When Dialecta ships a mark on a social card or a byline, no ambient comparisons and no legend are present. Dan's intent ("understand the basics") is silent on that boundary. The test's single-mark condition (21 readers got 100 percent accuracy with legend, zero chance without it, on pillar distinctions) names where that boundary lies. The specs are silent on which side.
