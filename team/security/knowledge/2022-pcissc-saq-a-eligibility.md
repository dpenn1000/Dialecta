# PCI DSS SAQ A: what keeps a merchant in the smallest questionnaire

**Source:** PCI Security Standards Council, "Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard: Self-Assessment Questionnaire A and Attestation of Compliance, for use with PCI DSS Version 4.0", publication date April 2022, section "Merchant Eligibility Criteria for Self-Assessment Questionnaire A"; and PCI Security Standards Council Blog, "FAQ Clarifies New SAQ A Eligibility Criteria for E-Commerce Merchants", read 2026-09-20. https://listings.pcisecuritystandards.org/documents/PCI-DSS-v4-0-SAQ-A.pdf ; https://blog.pcisecuritystandards.org/faq-clarifies-new-saq-a-eligibility-criteria-for-e-commerce-merchants

## Summary

SAQ A is the shortest PCI DSS self-assessment questionnaire, available only to a merchant that has pushed nearly all cardholder data handling to someone else. The Council's own eligibility criteria, quoted directly from the SAQ A document: the merchant "accepts only card-not-present (e-commerce or mail/telephone-order) transactions"; "all processing of account data is entirely outsourced to a PCI DSS compliant third-party service provider (TPSP)/payment processor"; the merchant "does not electronically store, process, or transmit any account data on merchant systems or premises"; the merchant "has reviewed the PCI DSS Attestation of Compliance form(s) for its TPSP(s) and confirmed" their compliance; and any account data the merchant retains at all is on paper, "not received electronically." For e-commerce specifically, one more condition applies: "all elements of the payment page(s)/form(s) delivered to the customer's browser originate only and directly from a PCI DSS compliant TPSP/payment processor." That last clause governs how a checkout gets built: a hosted payment page, or a TPSP-hosted iframe the merchant's page only embeds without touching card fields, keeps the merchant in SAQ A; a self-built form that collects card data before sending it to a processor generally does not, even if the merchant never stores what it collects.

A newer condition came from the Council's own blog: effective April 1, 2025, an e-commerce merchant using SAQ A must also confirm "their site is not susceptible to attacks from scripts that could affect the merchant's e-commerce system(s)," satisfied either by applying the script and tamper-detection controls referenced in Requirements 6.4.3 and 11.6.1, or by getting written confirmation from the TPSP that its embedded payment form already carries that protection. This targets a specific attack: a compromised script on the merchant's own page skimming card data out of what is otherwise a third-party payment form, a gap the outsourcing criteria above do not by themselves close. The SAQ A document read for this note is dated April 2022 for PCI DSS v4.0; the 2025 script-protection addition was confirmed through the Council's own blog rather than a re-read of the current v4.0.1 document itself.

## Implies for Dialecta

- If Dialecta's paid membership checkout uses a fully hosted redirect or a vendor-hosted iframe, such as Stripe Checkout or Stripe Elements in hosted mode, and Dialecta's own pages never receive card numbers, SAQ A is the right target, and most of the remaining burden is the checkout page's own script hygiene rather than card data handling.
- A custom-styled card form that collects card fields directly, even if the values are tokenized client-side before reaching a Dialecta server, is a different and heavier SAQ tier. That is a build decision to make deliberately, before the checkout is built, not one to discover afterward.
- The April 2025 script-protection addition means the page Dialecta controls, the one that loads the processor's hosted form, needs a script inventory or subresource-integrity practice, since a compromised third-party script on that page is explicitly in scope even though Dialecta never touches a card number.
- Not legal advice, and PCI DSS is a contractual standard rather than a statute, but a payment processor's merchant agreement can make compliance a condition of keeping the account. What would settle it: confirming with whichever processor Dialecta signs with exactly which SAQ type its specific checkout integration qualifies for, since the processor makes that call, not a general reading of the PCI SSC document.
