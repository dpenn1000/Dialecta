## The register question

The sentences mostly earn "not rules imposed from above": direct address, and "that pull isn't your weakness" indicts the industry, not the reader. What undercuts it is structural. "X, not Y" recurs nine times in the snapshot, the gate's count, often for real distinctions, but the repetition turns a chosen move into a reflex. The scaffolding is contract form regardless: numbered "§" sections in Roman numerals, a section titled The Commitment, sign at the bottom. The words deny being terms and conditions. The shape doesn't.

## § VIII

Mostly works. "I welcome the mirror" is built to be repeated back, concrete enough to survive being said by someone else. The soft spot: "not to punish me, but to take me seriously" names punish only to deny it, the one line answering an accusation nobody made.

## The scope cut

Three jobs, not mechanics, is right, and § III is the honest hard case: it is where § I's promise, that the system describes rather than silences, stops being asserted and becomes checkable, seven times, in its best writing ("more flag-planting than thinking"). Self-selection runs on that proof; a link defers it. One tier kept in full, the rest linked, keeps the evidence cheaply.

What has to survive any cut to a third: direct address, the one self-implicating line, and one mechanism made concrete rather than named. Lose all three and the register slides from agreement to notice in warmer words.

ADR-004 now has less room to earn a new clause, and grows heavier as the document shrinks around it. It cannot arrive as its own paragraph. It has to ride the existing sentence, "I welcome the mirror," naming who else may look as one more clause, not a new one beside it. Needing its own scaffolding is a failed compression test before anyone judges the wording.
