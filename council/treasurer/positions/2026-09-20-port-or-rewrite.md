## Brief

Port most of it: on forward cost alone, writing what already exists runs 5 to 7 weeks against a 5 to 8 day first slice, and weeks are the real currency here, not dollars, since Dan has no other budget. But the same cost test the port relies on also finds two things the plan hasn't priced. `_recovered/api/article/classify-stream.js` runs `claude-opus-4-7` with adaptive thinking and an 8,192 token ceiling, and `dialecta-admin-resetup-maps.jsx` fires it for what its own docblock calls smoke-testing, behind an admin gate `security` has already shown is forgeable. `dialecta-dev-admin.jsx`, 2,367 lines, is a worse trade than Supabase Studio, which is already paid for and already safe.

## Sunk cost against carrying cost

The months Dan named are real, and they are gone regardless of what this bench decides today. That is what makes "months of effort went into it" a sunk cost by definition: true, and not an argument for either path on its own. The argument that does work is forward cost, and this bench already has both numbers, filed by `builder` and cross-checked twice against the same source: full rebuild, 5 to 7 weeks. First working slice, porting instead, 5 to 8 working days. That is 5 to 8 days against 25 to 35, a compression of roughly 3 to 7 times to the same first working path: article, comment, classify, a rendered card.

No hourly rate for Dan's time exists anywhere in this repository, and I will not invent one. The infrastructure floor this seat already prices is denominated in dollars because vendors bill in dollars. Dan's own labor is not for sale to this project, so weeks are not a stand-in for a dollar figure, they are the actual currency, the one resource here that cannot be financed, discounted, or found more of by cutting an infrastructure line. Nineteen to twenty-eight members solve a $78-to-$110-a-month floor. Nothing solves a 3-to-7-times swing in weeks except not spending the weeks.

Carrying cost is the piece "port everything" leaves unpriced, and it is real but concentrated, not spread evenly across the tree. `builder` and `security`, working independently by different methods, landed on the same shape: 21 files carrying 55 identity call sites need their argument re-sourced, a mechanical edit each. Nine files reading `window.__DIALECTA_MEMBER_UUID__` or a `data-member-*` attribute need an outright rewrite of how they learn who is asking. Inside the 28 files that merely receive identity as a prop, 19 use it to gate a mutation or a privileged view with no session proof behind it, a pattern security's read finds recurring independently in at least six unrelated places, not one bug repeated but a shape nobody who built this tree ever fixed at the root. Against that, 9 files (6,280 lines) plus 12 more (5,310 lines), about 37 percent of the 31,354 lines of code in `lib/theme`, need no change for identity reasons at all. The review burden a port carries is not "read 38,300 lines carefully." It is "read the 19 trust-decision files and the 9 injection points with real care, and the rest at the speed of a mechanical diff." `dialecta-dev-admin.jsx`, below, is the one place that care is not cheap at all.

## The editor

`dialecta-editor.jsx` is 4,727 lines, the single largest file in the recovered tree and, by itself, more than three times the size of everything in `apps/web/src` today (1,439 lines across 28 files). `builder`'s read, confirmed independently by `security`, is that about 250 of those lines, the `ProseEditor` contenteditable box, get rewritten regardless of this debate's outcome: ADR-003 already commits to TipTap writing `body_json` and `body_html`, and porting a contenteditable box would contradict a decision already made. That leaves about 4,477 lines, the stage flow, the tag picker, the opinion-map inputs, the polish panel, the publish flow, Stage 2.5, that port rather than get authored again.

Price the difference at builder's own numbers, the way the brief asks. The full-rebuild figure and the slice figure are not two estimates of the same thing; the slice is what a port makes reachable in days that a from-scratch build would not reach for weeks, and the editor is why. It sits on the slice's own critical path, and it is large enough by itself that writing it from nothing would plausibly consume a meaningful share of a full rebuild's runway on one file. Porting turns "write a 4,727-line editor" into "write 250 lines against a schema decision already made, and carry the other 4,477 forward." That is the one thing the port side of this debate buys that no rewrite can: not speed across the whole tree, speed on the single file large enough to gate everything downstream of it.

## The Underwriter price

ROADMAP moved this decision into Phase 2 because deciding costs nothing and not deciding already blocks two things. Vercel's Hobby plan is licensed for non-commercial use only and stops being legal the moment a membership exists, not the moment one is billed. The peer-gifting rebuild, the Stripe leg and the `is_gifted` / `gift_expires_at` columns survive Ghost's departure, the comp mechanism does not, cannot be sized without knowing what a gift is worth. Both are blocked on a number, not on a design. I am naming it.

**Fifty dollars a year.** Annual, never monthly by default: at $50 a year, Stripe's fixed 30 cents plus 2.9 percent costs about $2.10, an effective rate of 4.2 percent, against 9.6 percent for the same money collected as $5 a month, a table already filed in `positions/monetization.md`. Nineteen to twenty-eight members at that price cover the $936-to-$1,320 annual floor, within reach of what reactivation plus a season of writing can plausibly produce, per `positions/acquisition-cost.md`. A hundred a year would need only nine to thirteen members and is more fee-efficient still, and I considered it. I am not recommending it: this repository has no price-elasticity data for either number, and concentrating the whole floor on nine to thirteen cold conversions is a worse bet than spreading it across nineteen to twenty-eight, with nothing on file to say the higher price converts well enough to make up the difference. Fifty is also a legible gift amount, one year, which is what the gifting rebuild needs to scope against.

What this number is not: validated by demand. `positions/acquisition-cost.md` confirms zero Stripe customers have ever existed for Dialecta; this is a supply-side number, built from the fee curve and the floor, not measured willingness to pay. `dialecta-tier-capabilities.js`, read in full, is the evidence for what it buys: `max_candidates` of 2 against `null` (the pro default of 3), `polish_runs` of 2 against `Infinity`. A one-candidate difference and a run-count cap is a thin feature case for fifty dollars on its own, and it should be sold as what it is designed to be: patronage that keeps Dialecta free to read, with the Charter Underwriter badge, the first roughly hundred paid members, already migrated into the schema, as the actual reward, not a bigger opinion-map picker.

`philosopher`'s constraint still holds and this number does not touch it: a price switches norms by existing, not by its size, so setting it at fifty rather than five or five hundred does not reopen that question.

## Running cost

Sort what the recovered tree adds by what fires a priced model call and what only touches Supabase, reading the actual endpoint each file calls rather than assuming from the surface name.

**Free once ported, no material recurring cost.** `dialecta-notifications-data.js`, `-bell.jsx`, `-page.jsx`, `-settings.jsx` (1,166 lines combined) read and write a notifications table. `dialecta-nomination-panel.jsx` (658 lines) posts a nomination. `dialecta-opinion-map-placement.jsx` (670 lines) posts a reader's placement among candidates generated elsewhere. The profile and community families and `dialecta-fingerprint-engine.jsx` (client-side geometry and color, confirmed by security's read) are Supabase reads, writes, and storage, the kind of cost already inside the $78-to-$110 floor this seat prices every month. None of these should be dropped on cost grounds; none of them cost anything to drop.

**Priced, and cheap at today's volume.** `dialecta-editor.jsx`'s `suggest-topics` call (`claude-haiku-4-5-20251001`, `max_tokens: 512`) is the same order as the $0.002 comment classifier already filed. Its `aesthetic-suggest` calls and `dialecta-admin-repolish.jsx`'s repolish path (the same Haiku model, `max_tokens: 8192`, a full article as input rather than a comment) run higher, an estimate of roughly a cent to two cents a call by the same input-times-rate, output-times-rate method that produced the $0.002 figure. I have not measured a real run; this is a rate calculation, not a measurement. `dialecta-quotes-data.js`'s `ai-suggest` (Haiku, `max_tokens: 3000`, admin only) sits in the same range.

**Priced, and the one place this debate should stop and look.** `dialecta-editor.jsx`'s own `classify` call and `dialecta-admin-resetup-maps.jsx`'s `classify-stream` call both run `claude-opus-4-7`, not Haiku, with `max_tokens: 8192` and adaptive thinking, which bills its reasoning tokens at the output rate. Against the pricing this seat already fetched (the Opus tier on file: $5 input, $25 output per million, the closest rate available for a model string with none published under its own name), a typical run is roughly $0.03 to $0.15, a nickel give or take a dime, against $0.002 to $0.004 for a comment: one order of magnitude at the low end, close to two at the high end. That gap is not a reason to drop either file. At the handful of runs a working editor or an occasional admin re-map would generate in a month, it costs a few dollars, immaterial next to the floor. That is why the spend cap ROADMAP already ordered ("a spend cap on `/api/classify` before comments open, since every write fires a priced call") has to be read as covering the comment-level Haiku endpoint only, and extended by name to the article-level Opus endpoint before either file ships: nothing in the recovered client code caps it today.

**What turns that into a dollar figure instead of a hypothetical.** Security's read of `dialecta-admin-resetup-maps.jsx` and `dialecta-admin-repolish.jsx` finds the identical forgeable shape already confirmed in `dialecta-dev-admin.jsx`: a client-side fetch, a boolean read off unauthenticated JSON, a privileged button gated on it, no session proof behind the mutation that follows. If that shape ships as written, the Opus-class call behind Re-setup Maps is not gated by who is an admin, it is gated by who can produce a string that reads as one, the same forgery already confirmed for comments. A hundred scripted calls against that path costs three to fifteen dollars. Ten thousand, a trivial loop against an endpoint with no cap, costs three hundred to fifteen hundred. That is a real number attached to a security finding that was filed without one, and it is the strongest reason in this whole position that porting cannot mean porting the auth pattern too.

## dialecta-dev-admin.jsx against Supabase Studio

`dialecta-dev-admin.jsx` is 2,367 lines and already sits outside builder's estimate: "admin can wait or run through Supabase Studio" was already the call before this position existed. I am pricing that call, not relitigating it.

What Studio gives for zero marginal cost, since the $32 Supabase line is already inside the floor this seat prices every month regardless of this file: the member directory `MembersSection` downloads whole and filters client side (Studio's table editor does the same thing against the real table, under Dan's own authenticated session, rather than a client fetch with no `Authorization` header, no `credentials`, no session of any kind, confirmed by security's read). The role grant `GrantRoleModal` performs client side (a direct row edit in Studio is the same change, made by someone Supabase has already authenticated, instead of a `POST` to `/api/admin/team` that trusts whatever `member_id` string the browser hands it, the shape security calls the single worst finding in the whole recovered tree). Feedback triage, if the feedback table is a table.

What Studio does not give, and what a rebuild would owe if this file were rebuilt at all: the repolish trigger, an action rather than a table edit, though one call to an authenticated endpoint or a short script covers it, not 2,367 lines of React, and the `pulse` analytics view, where a saved SQL query in Studio substitutes for a bespoke dashboard.

The trade is not port against nothing: 2,367 lines carrying a confirmed, unauthenticated role-grant path, plus whatever it costs to fix that path properly, against a surface already paid for, already authenticated correctly, and already running. Drop `dialecta-dev-admin.jsx` and `dev-admin-mount.jsx` (33 lines, the ninth DOM-read injection point, with nothing left to feed once its parent is dropped) as port targets. If Dan's actual admin workflow later proves Studio and a terminal insufficient, most likely on the repolish button, that is new, scoped, small work with a server-side auth check from the start, not a port of 2,367 lines that would carry the hole forward by default.

## Rebuttal

Migrator and security both land on my own number, and not the same way, so I take them separately.

Migrator's two migrations are real and I hadn't priced them: the article-content DDL and a public read policy on comments. But migrator's own word for both is small, days not weeks, additive only, against five rows and one policy. Add them honestly and 5 to 8 days becomes roughly 7 to 10. The compression against a 25 to 35 day rewrite softens from 3 to 7 times to nearer 2.5 to 5 times. Still real, still the load-bearing fact. I priced the front end and skipped the two migrations gating it. That was the gap, not the multiple.

Security's dozen missing doors is a different claim than it first reads. The table names nine surfaces behind eighteen files, and exactly one, comment write, ships live today. That one door carries the whole first slice: the compose path and the discourse layer's mutations. The other eight, article submit, follow, own-profile edit, opinion-map placement, nomination, notifications, quotes admin, admin grant, gate the rest of the port, the 4,477 lines of editor and everything behind it, not the slice. My 5 to 8 day figure was never a claim that the full port clears security's authorization gap. It was a claim about reaching one working demo fastest. That claim survives migrator's correction and doesn't touch security's finding, because the slice and the full port were never the same number, and my brief should have said so.

What I owe next: price the eight unbuilt doors into the full-port estimate before anyone reads 5 to 7 weeks as covering them. It probably doesn't.
