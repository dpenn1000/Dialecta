# INN Index 2025: how small editorial nonprofits get funded

**Source:** Jesse Holcomb, Michele McLellan and Ha Ta, *The 2025 INN Index*, Institute for Nonprofit News, covering 2024 data. Read via Dan Kennedy, "Nonprofit local news is growing, but the revenue mix remains unbalanced", Media Nation, 9 October 2025, fetched 2026-09-19. https://dankennedy.net/2025/10/09/nonprofit-local-news-is-growing-but-the-revenue-mix-remains-unbalanced-according-to-a-new-inn-report/ . INN's own pages at inn.org and news.inn.org returned HTTP 403 to automated fetches on the same date, so the primary report could not be read directly.

## Summary

Revenue mix across INN member newsrooms in 2024: **foundations 49 percent, individual giving 32 percent, earned revenue 18 percent.** Median member revenue was $532,000, up from $477,000 in 2023. The median local newsroom ran on just under $360,000 with four full-time employees. Local news organisations were 51 percent of INN's membership, up from 48 percent. Search summaries of INN's own reporting add that the roughly 400 digital-first nonprofit newsrooms took in a combined $650 to $700 million, up 14 percent on 2023, and that earned revenue has nearly doubled as a share since 2017, with 58 percent of members earning at least 10 percent of revenue from earned income.

Kennedy's framing, which is the reason to file this rather than a press release, is that the mix "remains unbalanced". Half the sector's money comes from foundations. That is a structural dependency on a small number of institutions whose priorities change on their own schedule.

## Implies for Dialecta

- **The charter's veto on single-grant dependency is the right call and this is the sector-scale evidence for it.** Forty-nine percent foundation funding is the failure mode, not the benchmark. Dialecta should treat grant money as fundable for a discrete build with a start and an end, never as the line that pays the monthly floor.
- The median INN newsroom runs on $532,000. Dialecta's floor is around $564 a year. These are not the same kind of organisation and Dialecta should not import this sector's cost structure, staffing assumptions or fundraising apparatus. The relevance is the shape of the risk, not the size of the budget.
- Individual giving at 32 percent is the channel available to a solo founder with a small readership, and it is the same channel Wikimedia, MetaFilter and Kelly all land on from different directions. Four independent sources converging is the strongest thing in this research tree.
- Earned revenue at 18 percent and rising is the part worth watching for later. For Dialecta the plausible earned lines are things the platform already makes: the classified corpus, the opinion-map aggregates, and the classification engine itself. The Project Brief's Tier 3 already names the corpus as a moat. Selling access to any of it touches editorial independence directly and is a council question, not a treasurer decision.
