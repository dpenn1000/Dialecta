# Wikimedia Foundation: what reader funding looks like at the top of the curve

**Source:** Wikimedia Foundation audited financial statements FY2024-2025, summarised at meta.wikimedia.org, "Wikimedia Foundation reports/Financial/Audits/2024-2025 frequently asked questions", fetched 2026-09-19. https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Wikimedia_Foundation_reports/Financial/Audits/2024-2025_-_frequently_asked_questions . Average-donation figure from https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Fundraising , same date.

## Summary

FY2024-2025: total revenue $208.6 million, up 13 percent or $23.2 million. Of that, $189.5 million came from donations including Endowment grants, up from $174.7 million. Expenses were $190.9 million, up 7 percent. Net assets at 30 June 2025 were $296.6 million, or 17.1 months of operating expenses.

Revenue by channel: banners 30 percent (about $62.4 million, down from 32 percent the year before), recurring giving 21 percent, email 15 percent, Wikimedia Enterprise 4 percent in its first materially contributing year. The Foundation states that most funding comes from individual readers "giving an average of $11".

Expense mix: 77.4 percent programmatic, 11.4 percent fundraising, 11.2 percent general and administrative.

Three things in that are transferable and one is a warning.

Transferable: no single channel exceeds a third of revenue; recurring giving at 21 percent is larger than email and is the channel that compounds; and 17.1 months of reserve is treated as a target rather than a surplus to spend.

The warning is the 11.4 percent fundraising expense. **Raising money from many small donors costs about 11 cents on the dollar even at the largest reader-funded operation in the world.** Small-donor funding is not free money; it is a channel with a cost of goods.

## Implies for Dialecta

- The "average of $11" is the number that makes the small-donor model legible at Dialecta's scale. Against an infrastructure floor near $564 a year (see `../positions/monetization.md`), the platform needs about 51 gifts of $11, or 12 annual memberships at $50. Both are small enough to be reached by a family blog's readership, which is what Dialecta currently is.
- The 11.4 percent fundraising cost argues against banner-style appeals on a site whose founding thesis is about what environments reward. Dialecta cannot run Wikipedia's banner without becoming a site that asks for money in the reading experience, which is the same attentional intrusion the thesis objects to in advertising. Take the recurring-giving lesson and leave the banner.
- Recurring giving at 21 percent, growing, supports the standing position that a recurring annual membership beats one-off appeals. It is also the channel with the lowest per-dollar solicitation cost, since it is solicited once.
- Diversification is a target, not a virtue in the abstract. At Dialecta's size, two channels is diversification: memberships and patronage. Grants are the third and the charter already vetoes depending on one. See `2025-inn-index-revenue-mix.md` for what over-reliance on foundations does to small editorial organisations.
