# Kelly (2008), "1,000 True Fans"

**Source:** Kevin Kelly, "1,000 True Fans", The Technium, originally published March 2008; the version fetched is the edited 2014 update, later reprinted in Tim Ferriss, *Tools of Titans*. Fetched 2026-09-19. https://kk.org/thetechnium/1000-true-fans/

## Summary

Kelly's argument: a creator does not need mass success, only a direct relationship with about a thousand people who "will purchase anything and everything you produce". The arithmetic is deliberately simple. 1,000 true fans multiplied by $100 of annual spending each is $100,000 a year, and the essay assumes the creator keeps the whole amount by avoiding intermediaries.

The caveats Kelly states are the part that gets dropped when the essay is quoted. The required number varies by medium and geography. A collaborative group needs proportionally more fans, because the income divides. Not every creator has the temperament for the fan-tending the model demands. And indirect support, meaning money that reaches the creator through advertisers or publishers rather than from the fan, requires far more than a thousand people, because each one is worth a fraction as much.

The essay is a 2008 argument, verified as such. It predates Substack, Patreon and the current creator-platform economy, which is a point in its favour rather than against it: the platforms were built on this arithmetic.

## Implies for Dialecta

- Run the arithmetic backwards and it is the most encouraging number in this research tree. Dialecta does not need $100,000. It needs roughly $564 a year to stay switched on. At Kelly's $100 a fan that is **six true fans**. At a $50 annual membership it is twelve. At Wikimedia's average $11 gift it is 51.
- That reframes the monetization question entirely. It is not "how does this become a business". It is "how does a platform cover a bill smaller than one streaming subscription a month without changing what it rewards". The second question has answers the first does not.
- Kelly's caveat about collaborative groups is the one that bites. Dialecta is multi-author by design, and if contributor payouts ever enter the model the fan count required multiplies by the number of people being paid. That is the same linear-cost failure the treasurer flags in `2026-medium-partner-program.md`, arriving from the revenue side.
- The caveat about indirect support is the cleanest statement of why advertising fails at this scale. Ad-funded attention is worth a fraction of a cent per reader per view; direct support is worth dollars per reader per year. A site with Dialecta's readership cannot reach its floor on indirect support and can reach it on direct support with a dozen people. `2014-metafilter-ad-collapse.md` is the case study.
- Temperament is a real cost and it is Dan's, not the platform's. A membership model that needs monthly cultivation of a donor base is a recurring demand on the founder's attention. Annual billing and a patronage line that asks once are the cheap versions on that axis too.
