# The funnel everyone is quoting was measured on a site with nothing to join

**Source:** Dialecta's own data. `council/treasurer/research/2026-ghost-native-analytics-all-time.md`
(Ghost 6 native analytics, all-time range 6 April to 20 September 2026) and
`council/circulation/brief.md`, both read 2026-09-20. No new external source: this note is a
re-reading of measured internal numbers already filed, asking the question this seat exists to
ask, what can honestly be concluded about arrival from them.

## Summary

The number in circulation: 269 visitors produced 6 real people, so roughly 17,000 visitors would
produce the 19 memberships that cover the $78 monthly floor. That figure is arithmetically sound
as a projection of a measured rate. The rate itself is not sound as a projection of Dialecta, for
two separate reasons pointing in different directions, and the Council should stop quoting a
single number until both are resolved.

**First, the numerator is not cold traffic converting.** Of the 269 visitors, direct traffic is
194, 72 percent, and treasurer's note already flags that as mostly Dan's own address book plus
dev-time visits, not discovery. Of the 6 real people who created member records, 3 are arm's
length and 3 are not, which reads as Dan's own network again rather than strangers deciding to
join on the strength of the site alone. A rate built mostly from people who were personally asked
does not predict what a stranger arriving from Facebook or a search result will do, because
nobody personally asked them. The honest statement is that Dialecta has almost no measured data
on how a true stranger converts, not that the stranger-conversion rate is 2.2 percent.

**Second, and this is the part that is specifically mine to say: every one of those 269 visitors
landed on a site with zero published comments and zero published discourse.** Three comments
exist and all three sit at `pending_review`, unseen by any visitor, per `brief.md`. Whatever the
269 were reacting to, it was five articles and an empty comments section, not Dialecta's actual
proposition: a place where people argue in public and the arguing is worth reading. A visitor
today cannot evaluate the thing Dan is building, because the thing Dan is building has never been
visible to a visitor. That is not a small caveat on the 2.2 percent figure. It means the figure
describes a different, simpler product: a five-post blog with a dead comments widget.

## What can honestly be concluded

Not a corrected rate. There isn't one to correct to yet. The two effects above pull in opposite
directions and neither is sized. A rate contaminated by personal asks is an overestimate of cold
conversion. A rate measured on a site with no visible discourse is very possibly an underestimate
of what the finished product converts at, since an empty room is a worse pitch than a room with
something happening in it, and right now there is a specific, measurable reason for that: nobody
visiting Dialecta has ever been able to read a single comment on it.

What can be said plainly: **the 17,000-visitor figure is not a forecast. It is one point, computed
from a rate that was never measuring the product being forecast for.** It should not appear in a
plan, a pitch, or another advisor's arithmetic as if it were a stable constant. The correct next
step is not a better estimate, it is a re-measurement once real discourse is live and once at
least one channel has sent a batch of genuinely cold visitors. Until then, treat arrival-to-member
conversion as unmeasured, not as measured-and-low.

## Implies for Dialecta

- Every forecast in the Council that leans on "17,000 visitors," or the 14,500 figure in
  treasurer's own note, which is the same rate carried one step further into paying members,
  should carry this note's caveat or be retired until re-measured. This does not dispute
  treasurer's arithmetic on the rate as given. It disputes that the rate is safe to project
  forward.
- The single most useful thing this seat can do before the next Council debate is get real
  discourse in front of real cold traffic and measure what happens, once, honestly. That is a
  sequencing question, not a research question, and it sits inside Dan's own "finished before
  anyone is invited" decision rather than against it: the correction happens after that finish
  line, not before it.
- The 6-real-people number is worth more as a list of 6 people to ask directly than as the
  numerator of a rate. Direct, personal outreach is the one channel already proven to work on this
  data, at n=6, and it does not require solving arrival at all. See `positions.md`.
- Facebook's 54 visitors, 20 percent of all traffic and the only channel that is not Dan's own
  address book, is a firmer planning fact than any rate derived from the 6, because it is a
  channel behaviour, repeatable and free, not a one-time personal ask. It is where cold-traffic
  measurement should start.
