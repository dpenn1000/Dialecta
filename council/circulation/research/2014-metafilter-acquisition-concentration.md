# MetaFilter's ad collapse, read for acquisition concentration rather than revenue

**Source:** Same primary source as `council/treasurer/research/2014-metafilter-ad-collapse.md`
(David Auerbach, "MetaFilter layoffs: Why has Google forsaken the legendary Internet forum?",
Slate, May 2014). Read there in full per the reading list's own instruction, rather than
re-fetched. This note adds only the angle treasurer's charter does not cover: what the same
mechanism implies for where visitors come from, as opposed to how the site is paid for.

## Summary

Treasurer's note has the facts: a single Google ranking change cut MetaFilter's traffic 40 percent
and its ad revenue by nearly half within weeks, after a decade of the arrangement working. Nothing
about the site changed. The dependency was invisible until the day it was not.

**This seat is not reopening whether Dialecta runs ads for revenue. That veto stands and is not
mine to reopen.** What is mine: the same failure shape applies to acquisition, not just to
revenue, and Dialecta is already exposed to it. Facebook is currently 100 percent of Dialecta's
non-direct, non-personal-network traffic, 54 of 269 visitors all time, see
`2026-dialecta-funnel-correction.md`. That is one platform, one algorithm, one account.
MetaFilter's lesson is not "do not use ads." It is "do not let one channel you do not control
become the whole channel," and on the acquisition side of the ledger, Dialecta already has exactly
one.

## Implies for Dialecta

- A second arrival channel is worth establishing before Facebook is trusted as "the plan," not
  because Facebook is failing (it is the only channel with a measured return) but because it is
  the only one, and MetaFilter shows what happens to a site that never had to ask that question
  until the day it mattered.
- This is a concentration argument, not an advertising argument. Keeping it that way is the point
  of filing this note separately from treasurer's rather than folding it in.
