# The social login conversion lift traces to a 2011 preference survey, not a measurement

**Source:** Reviewed together because no single source settles the question. Janrain/Blue Research
survey, 2010-2011 (Janrain sold social login; the original report is no longer online, read via
Wikipedia's "Social login" article and corroborating secondary write-ups, dates and n disagree
across sources: 657 consumers in December 2010 per one citation, 619 in October 2011 per another,
both plausibly real Janrain-commissioned waves rather than one study misreported). Gafni & Nissim,
"To Social Login or not Login? Exploring Factors Affecting the Decision," Interdisciplinary Journal
of Information, Knowledge, and Management, 2016, iisit.org, peer reviewed, primary. Corbado,
"Social Login Conversion Rate: Benchmarks & Pitfalls," corbado.com, fetched 2026-09-20 in full
(vendor: Corbado sells passkeys, a competing authentication product, which cuts against rather than
toward its own skepticism of social login). CXL, "Social Login: 9 Reasons Why You Need It,"
cxl.com/blog/social-login/, independent CRO consultancy, direct fetch returned HTTP 403, the claims
below are search-index snippets of that page rather than a primary read and are marked as such.

## Summary

No independent, causally clean evidence shows that adding social login increases signup
completion. What circulates as settled fact traces to a small number of weak sources, and each one
is weaker once traced to its origin rather than to the blog post repeating it.

**The number behind most "social login increases conversion" claims is a stated preference survey,
not a measurement of behavior.** Janrain, a company that sold social login, commissioned Blue
Research to survey several hundred self-selected online shoppers around 2010-2011. The headline
finding, still quoted today, is that roughly three in four consumers said they preferred social
login to registering with a password, and a similar share said registration forms annoyed them
enough to change their behavior. That is what people told a survey company they preferred, fifteen
years ago, commissioned by a business that sold the alternative. It is not two versions of a signup
form measured side by side, and it predates the current provider landscape, Facebook's News Feed
algorithm changes, and every privacy scandal since.

**The one peer-reviewed study found is the same shape.** Gafni and Nissim surveyed 101 internet
users on attitude toward social login. Five factors emerged: familiarity and convenience as
encouragers, privacy and security as inhibitors. That is a model of intention, not a measurement of
completed signups. No conversion rate appears anywhere in it.

**Where real company results surface, they are thin and they cut both ways.** Via CXL's
search-indexed page (not independently fetched, see caveat above): MailChimp measured a 3.4 percent
lift in conversion from landing to login over a one month test, then removed the feature over brand
and design concerns. Magnetiq got a higher raw signup rate through social login but a lower
signup-to-paid rate than through its own form. The second result is the one that matters for a
membership platform: the button won the vanity metric and lost the metric that pays a bill.

**Even a company built to sell the alternative admits there is no portable number.** Corbado, which
sells passkeys, reviewed this space in 2026 and concluded plainly that "social login conversion
rate is not a single number you can copy from a case study," that published figures mostly trace to
"interested parties," meaning social login vendors, and that provider performance is "a tendency,
not a guarantee." Its own recommendation is to measure your own funnel rather than borrow anyone
else's number.

**A reflexive note.** Even the more independent-looking source in this review, CXL, is itself
repeating named-company anecdotes without a visible primary citation, which this note could not
verify past the search index. The entire public information supply on this question is thin
secondary reporting citing other thin secondary reporting back to one 2011 vendor survey. That is
itself a finding.

## Implies for Dialecta

- Any recommendation to add Facebook, X, or another provider "because it increases signups" is
  resting on this evidence base, not on a finding. Name that plainly if it is invoked in council.
- The Magnetiq result is the closest analogue to Dialecta's own situation: a platform whose revenue
  depends on paid conversion, not raw signups, got a worse outcome on the metric that pays the
  bills from the same feature that improved the vanity metric. Worth weighing before shipping,
  independent of whether it generalizes.
- This does not mean social login has no effect. It means the effect, if any, is unmeasured for any
  product resembling Dialecta, exactly the standard this seat's charter already holds every other
  recommendation to.
