# Godbehere v. Phoenix Newspapers: Arizona's false light tort, and the true record that misleads

**Source:** Godbehere v. Phoenix Newspapers, Inc., 162 Ariz. 335, 783 P.2d 781 (Ariz. 1989), and
Hart v. Seven Resorts, Inc., 947 P.2d 846 (1997), read 2026-09-20 through two routes, neither of
them the slip opinion. The quoted holdings come from the Digital Media Law Project's Arizona false
light guide (Berkman Klein Center, archived), https://www.dmlp.org/legal-guide/arizona-false-light.
The Restatement Section 652E text as the court quoted it comes from vLex's visible portion of the
opinion, https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/godbehere-v-phoenix-newspapers-887714510. Justia and
Casemine returned 403 and CourtListener an empty body on this attempt. **The sentence that false
light arises when "publication of true information creates a false implication" is stated by DMLP
as the general rule without a pin cite, and by search summaries of the opinion; it has not been
read in the court's own words.** Upgrade to a primary read before quoting it as the court's.

## Summary

Arizona recognises false light invasion of privacy on the Restatement's terms. As the court quoted
Section 652E: "One who gives publicity to a matter concerning another that places the other before
the public in a false light is subject to liability to the other for invasion of his privacy, if
(a) the false light in which the other was placed would be highly offensive to a reasonable person,
and (b) the actor had knowledge of or acted in reckless disregard as to the falsity of the
publicized matter and the false light in which the other would be placed."

Five features separate it from defamation, each from DMLP's quotation of Godbehere unless noted.

- **It protects feelings rather than standing.** "Privacy, [unlike defamation], does not protect
  reputation but protects mental and emotional interests," and "a plaintiff may recover even in
  the absence of reputational damage."
- **Truth is not a complete answer.** "a plaintiff may bring a false light invasion of privacy
  action ... even though the actual facts stated are true." DMLP states the rule as arising "when
  something untrue has been published about an individual, or when the publication of true
  information creates a false implication."
- **The bar is high.** The false light must be "highly offensive to a reasonable person" and the
  publication must involve "a major misrepresentation of [the plaintiff's] character, history,
  activities or beliefs," not "minor or unimportant inaccuracies."
- **"Publicity" for false light "differs from 'publication'" for defamation.** The Restatement's
  definition of publicity was not read here.
- **Public officials cannot sue on official conduct.** "there can be no false light invasion of
  privacy action for matters involving official acts or duties of public officers," which is the
  holding that disposed of the sheriff's claim.

Hart v. Seven Resorts restates the fault element: the defendant must have "knowledge of or acted
in reckless disregard as to the falsity of the publicized matter and the false light in which the
[plaintiff] would be placed."

## Implies for Dialecta

- **It is the tort shaped like the fingerprint's defects.** Defamation asks whether a statement is
  false. False light also asks whether a true record was presented so that it implies something
  false. The 2026-09-20 reading test found both: a mark implying heat in a profile with none, and a
  mark drawing real, early heat as recent and a real 22 graduations on Discourse as a person who
  does not engage (`council/log/2026-09-20-fingerprint-legibility-and-model.md`).
- **Its high bar is what protects the platform on Heat.** "Heated" is unlikely to be a major
  misrepresentation of character, or highly offensive to a reasonable person. A false Breach
  reading, that a person targets people rather than ideas, plausibly clears both. The Breach
  residual is where this tort would bite.
- **Its fault element turns on what the operator knew and when.** A render defect documented in the
  platform's own dated log is knowledge, or close to it, for every mark published afterwards. The
  date of a fix matters as much as the fix.
- **"Publicity" is probably a larger audience than "publication".** If so, a connections-only mark
  is exposed to defamation, where one third party is enough, and less to false light, while the
  public level carries both. Unverified until the Restatement's definition is read.
- Not legal advice. What would settle it: a primary read of Godbehere at 162 Ariz. 335, and an
  Arizona lawyer's view of whether a rendered image, read through a legend the platform published,
  can be a "major misrepresentation" of a person's activities.
