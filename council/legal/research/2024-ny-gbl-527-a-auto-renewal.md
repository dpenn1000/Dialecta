# New York GBL 527-a: the statute that already knows what a price ladder is

**Source:** N.Y. General Business Law Section 527-a, primary text read 2026-09-20 at
nysenate.gov. Quotations below are from the statute.
https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/527-A

## Summary

Applies to "any business making an automatic renewal or continuous service offer to a consumer"
in New York. No revenue threshold and no small-business exemption. The exempt categories in
subsection 4 are financial institutions, alarm operators, franchises and similar, none of which
reach a membership site.

**Disclosure, before consent or billing information is requested.** The material terms must be
clear and conspicuous and must include the product, "the amount of the costs that will be
charged", the frequency of charges, the deadline to act to stop further charges, the cancellation
mechanism, and, where applicable, a "clear and conspicuous explanation of how and when the price
will change".

**Affirmative consent** is required before the initial charge.

**Price increases, the provision that matters here.** A business may not charge "following an
increase in price, or a price higher than what was disclosed ... without either: (i) first
obtaining the consumer's affirmative consent to such increased price; or (ii) allowing the
consumer to cancel ... anytime within, at least, fourteen days after such charge and refund the
consumer in the amount equivalent to the price of the remaining term of the service ... on a pro
rata basis."

**Cancellation** must be "as easy to use as the mechanism that the consumer used to provide
consent and ... through the same medium", and available through every medium by which the business
accepts consent.

**Reminder.** A paid term of one year or longer that renews for six months or longer requires
notice "at least fifteen days before, but not more than forty-five days before, the cancellation
deadline", with cancellation instructions.

**Enforcement.** Attorney General only. No private right of action. Civil penalties run from $100
for a single unknowing violation to $1,000 for multiple knowing ones, with a bona fide error
defence the business must prove by a preponderance.

## Implies for Dialecta

- **A published, dated price ladder is the disclosure this statute asks for, not a risk it
  creates.** "How and when the price will change" is an enumerated material term. Announcing $50
  now and $100 on a stated date, on the screen before the card field, satisfies a requirement that
  a silent price rise would breach.
- **The grandfather is what keeps the first hundred out of the price-increase provision
  entirely.** If their price never rises, clause (d) never fires for them. If it ever does rise,
  each of them needs fresh affirmative consent or a fourteen-day cancel-and-pro-rata-refund
  window, per person, which is a support burden a one-operator site should not accept by accident.
- **An annual membership needs a renewal reminder window of fifteen to forty-five days before
  renewal.** That is a scheduled email, and it is the single cheapest item on this list.
- **The penalties are small and the AG is the only enforcer, so the honest severity is low.** The
  reason to comply is not the fine. It is that the same facts feed a state consumer-protection
  claim, a processor dispute and a public complaint, and the compliant version costs a screen and
  a cron job.
- Not legal advice. Whether a New York resident paying an Arizona operator brings 527-a into play
  is the ordinary long-arm question and was not researched; the statute is drafted to reach offers
  made to New York consumers rather than businesses located there.
