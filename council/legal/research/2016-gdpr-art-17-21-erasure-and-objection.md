# GDPR Articles 17 and 21: erasure, and the right to object to profiling

**Source:** Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation), Articles 17 and 21,
read 2026-09-20 via gdpr-info.eu, https://gdpr-info.eu/art-17-gdpr/ and
https://gdpr-info.eu/art-21-gdpr/. gdpr-info.eu is a private publisher's reproduction of the
Official Journal text, the same route `council/security/research/2016-eu-gdpr-territorial-scope.md`
used. The Official Journal itself was not read. Article 17(3) was read in the fetch tool's
abridgement, with points (b) to (d) shortened; (a) and (e) are quoted as given.

## Summary

**Article 17(1):** "The data subject shall have the right to obtain from the controller the erasure
of personal data concerning him or her without undue delay", where one of six grounds applies,
among them that "(a) the personal data are no longer necessary in relation to the purposes for which
they were collected or otherwise processed", that "(b) the data subject withdraws consent ... and
where there is no other legal ground for the processing", and that "(c) the data subject objects to
the processing ... and there are no overriding legitimate grounds for the processing."

**Article 17(2):** "Where the controller has made the personal data public and is obliged pursuant
to paragraph 1 to erase the personal data, the controller ... shall take reasonable steps,
including technical measures, to inform controllers which are processing the personal data."

**Article 17(3)** disapplies the right to the extent processing is necessary "(a) for exercising the
right of freedom of expression and information" or "(e) for the establishment, exercise or defence
of legal claims", among three other grounds.

**Article 21(1):** "The data subject shall have the right to object, on grounds relating to his or
her particular situation, at any time to processing of personal data concerning him or her which is
based on point (e) or (f) of Article 6(1), including profiling based on those provisions. The
controller shall no longer process the personal data unless the controller demonstrates compelling
legitimate grounds for the processing which override the interests, rights and freedoms of the data
subject or for the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims."

**Article 21(4):** "At the latest at the time of the first communication with the data subject, the
right referred to in paragraphs 1 and 2 shall be explicitly brought to the attention of the data
subject and shall be presented clearly and separately from any other information."

## Implies for Dialecta

- **None of this applies unless the GDPR does.** `security`'s territorial scope note finds the
  fingerprint itself a plausible trigger under Article 3(2)(b), monitoring of behaviour, for even
  one EU-resident member, independent of any marketing to Europe.
- **Article 21 is aimed at the fingerprint rather than at the comment.** It names profiling. A
  member who objects puts the burden on Dialecta to show "compelling legitimate grounds" that
  override their interests, a high bar for a public characterisation of how a person argues.
- **Article 17(3)(a) is the case for keeping a hardened comment in a public thread.** This seat
  reads it as a weaker case for keeping a profile the platform composed about the person, because
  the expression it would protect there is the platform's, about the person, rather than the
  person's own. (This seat's reading; no source says it.)
- **Article 17(2) reaches share cards.** A card that Facebook or X has cached is personal data the
  controller made public, and an erasure would carry a duty of reasonable steps to tell them.
- **Article 21(4) costs one sentence in the Pact.** If an EU-resident member ever joins, the right
  to object has to be stated at the first communication, clearly and separately. The Pact is the
  first communication.
- **Article 21(1) reaches only processing based on Article 6(1)(e) or (f).** Processing based on
  consent or on contract is reached by other routes, consent withdrawal among them, none read here.
  Which basis would carry the public display is a lawyer's question.
- Not legal advice. What would settle it: whether Article 3(2)(b) reaches Dialecta for an
  EU-resident member, which is `security`'s open question, and which Article 6 basis the public
  display rests on.
