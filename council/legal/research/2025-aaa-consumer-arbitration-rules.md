# AAA Consumer Arbitration Rules, and what an arbitration clause would cost Dialecta

**Source:** American Arbitration Association, Consumer Arbitration Rules, amended and effective May
1, 2025, read 2026-09-21 in full text from a copy hosted by McGlinchey Stafford, a named firm, and
extracted from the PDF. The fee figures are **not** from the rules: they are from search-result
summaries of the AAA's 2025 Consumer Fee Schedule and the AAA's own consumer rules page, read at
second hand, and must be checked against the schedule before anyone relies on them.
https://www.mcglinchey.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/2025_Consumer_Arbitration_Rules-1.pdf
https://www.adr.org/rules-forms-and-fees/consumer/

## Summary

From the rules, read directly:

- **R-1(f):** where no claim exceeds $25,000, the dispute is decided on documents only, unless the
  parties agree or the arbitrator decides otherwise.
- **R-9:** "Either party may choose to take their own claims directly to small claims court
  without first filing with the AAA."
- **R-12:** a business should register its consumer clause on the AAA's public Consumer Clause
  Registry, for a nonrefundable review fee and an annual fee; an unregistered clause gets an
  expedited review, at a one-time fee, when the first case is filed.
- **R-46(c):** the arbitrator may award fees or arbitrator compensation to a business only where the
  law requires it or a claim was brought to harass or is "patently frivolous."

From the AAA's consumer page: consumers pay "only a capped administrative filing fee", and "the
company pays the remaining administrative fees and all arbitrator compensation in consumer cases."

At second hand, from summaries of the 2025 schedule: $600 to register a clause and $600 a year to
keep it registered; $300 for an expedited review of an unregistered clause; a $375 business filing
fee when an individual files before a single arbitrator; an arbitrator appointment fee of $450 for
the business; merits arbitrators at $300 an hour.

## Implies for Dialecta

- **The business carries the whole cost of every consumer arbitration**, including one that fails,
  except the frivolous. At fourteen to a hundred members, that is a standing fee for protection
  against a claim nobody expects.
- **R-9 keeps small claims open either way**, so an arbitration clause buys nothing on the claims a
  member is most likely to bring, a refund or a renewal charge.
- **R-1(f) is the strongest point for arbitration.** A claim under $25,000 is decided on paper,
  which is cheaper than any court defence. A defamation or false light claim will usually plead
  more than that, which is where the argument turns; see `drafts/README.md`, Arbitration.
