# Public Act 25-113: the amended CTDPA text, read for whether a fingerprint is sensitive data

**Source:** Connecticut General Assembly, Substitute Senate Bill No. 1295, Public Act No. 25-113,
"An Act Concerning Data Privacy", enrolled text, 80 pages, sections effective July 1, 2026 and
August 1, 2026. Read 2026-09-20 from the General Assembly's own PDF.
https://www.cga.ct.gov/2025/act/pa/pdf/2025PA-00113-R00SB-01295-PA.pdf

This is primary text, not a summary. `council/security/research/2023-ct-data-privacy-act.md`
reached the applicability question from secondary sources and left the sensitive data question to
a lawyer. This note answers it from the statute, and it comes out differently from the guess.

## Summary

**Applicability, Section 6, amending Conn. Gen. Stat. Section 42-516, effective July 1, 2026.**
The act applies to persons that "(1) Conduct business in this state, or produce products or
services that are targeted to residents of this state, and during the preceding calendar year
controlled or processed the personal data of not fewer than thirty-five thousand consumers,
excluding personal data controlled or processed solely for the purpose of completing a payment
transaction; (2) control or process consumers' sensitive data, excluding personal data controlled
or processed solely for the purposes of completing a payment transaction; or (3) offer consumers'
personal data for sale in trade or commerce." The old 100,000 threshold and the old 25,000 plus
25 percent of revenue test are struck. Clauses (2) and (3) carry no consumer count at all. The
renumbering leaves the business nexus language inside clause (1), which reads oddly against
clauses (2) and (3); the natural reading is that the nexus applies throughout, and it is met here
either way because the operator is in Connecticut.

**Sensitive data, Section 42-515(39) as amended.** A closed list of nine lettered categories:
"(A) data revealing (i) racial or ethnic origin, (ii) religious beliefs, (iii) a mental or
physical health condition, diagnosis, disability or treatment, (iv) sex life, sexual orientation
or status as nonbinary or transgender, or (v) citizenship or immigration status, (B) consumer
health data, (C) genetic or biometric data or information derived therefrom, (D) personal data
collected from an individual the controller has actual knowledge, or wilfully disregards, is a
child, (E) data concerning an individual's status as a victim of crime, (F) precise geolocation
data, (G) neural data, (H) a consumer's financial account number, financial account log-in
information or credit card or debit card number ... or (I) government-issued identification
number".

**Biometric data, Section 42-515(4).** "data generated by automatic measurements of an
individual's biological characteristics, such as a fingerprint, a voiceprint, eye retinas, irises
or other unique biological patterns or characteristics that are used to identify a specific
individual."

**Neural data, Section 42-515(24).** "any information that is generated by measuring the activity
of an individual's central nervous system."

**Personal data, Section 42-515(27).** "any information that is linked or reasonably linkable to
an identified or identifiable individual." It excludes de-identified data and publicly available
information.

**Publicly available information, Section 42-515(34).** Information lawfully available from
government records, or that "a controller has a reasonable basis to believe (I) a consumer has
lawfully made available to the general public, or (II) has been lawfully made available to the
general public from widely distributed media".

**Profiling, Section 42-515(31).** "any form of automated processing performed on personal data
to evaluate, analyze or predict personal aspects related to an identified or identifiable
individual's economic situation, health, personal preferences, interests, reliability, behavior,
location or movements."

**Decision that produces any legal or similarly significant effect, Section 42-515(15).** Narrowed
by this act to "any decision made by the controller, or on behalf of the controller, that results
in the provision or denial by the controller of any financial or lending service, any housing,
any insurance, any education enrollment or opportunity, any criminal justice, any employment
opportunity or any health care service." The act strikes the former closing words "or access to
essential goods or services".

**Profiling rights, Section 8 amending Section 42-518(a)(6).** Where personal data "were
processed for the purposes of profiling in furtherance of any automated decision that produced
any legal or similarly significant effect", the consumer may "(A) question the result of such
profiling, (B) be informed of the reason that such profiling resulted in such decision, (C)
review the consumer's personal data that were processed for the purposes of such profiling", plus
a correction right limited to housing decisions.

**Impact assessment, Section 42-522(c).** Required of "Each controller that engages in any
profiling for the purposes of making a decision that produces any legal or similarly significant
effect", with seven prescribed contents including inputs, outputs, metrics, known limitations,
transparency measures and post-deployment monitoring.

## Implies for Dialecta

- **The six pillars and the archetype are not sensitive data.** The list at Section 42-515(39) is
  closed and lettered, and Acuity, Reach, Calibration, Magnanimity, Discourse and Consistency
  appear nowhere in it. `docs/Dialecta_Contributor_Identity.md` line 30 says the pillars "do not
  measure what someone believes", and Naming Principle 3 at line 123 forbids tribal coding. Taken
  at its word, the spec has designed the fingerprint out of category (A).
- **The Thinking Fingerprint is not biometric data.** Section 42-515(4) requires measurement of a
  biological characteristic. A petal shape computed from comment history measures nothing
  biological. The name is a metaphor and the statute is not fooled by it, but the name will be
  the first thing quoted in any demand letter, and `docs/Dialecta_Contributor_Identity.md` line
  77 saying the shape "is unfakeable" reads at a glance like a claim of unique identification.
  Worth knowing before it is read back to us.
- **Not neural data either.** Section 42-515(24) requires measuring central nervous system
  activity. This one is not close.
- **The door into CTDPA is the comment text, not the fingerprint.** Dialecta's editorial subject
  is contested public questions. The moment a Connecticut contributor writes "as a Catholic" or
  "as an immigrant" and `api/comment.js` sends it to Claude and stores the structured
  `claim_text` in `classifications`, Dialecta is processing data revealing religious belief or
  immigration status. That triggers clause (2) at any volume, including one. The live
  `opinion_map_positions` table is the same exposure with a schema around it.
- **The publicly available information carve-out is the best defence and it has a hole.** A
  published comment is plausibly information the consumer "lawfully made available to the general
  public". But classification happens before publication, on text submitted privately; a Breach
  comment is never published in its original form at all; and Private Draft Mode is by definition
  not public. At the moment of processing the text is not yet public, which is the moment the
  statute cares about.
- **Dialecta profiles, on the statute's own words.** Section 42-515(31) covers automated
  processing that evaluates "reliability" and "behavior" and "personal preferences, interests".
  Calibration evaluates reliability by name; Discourse and Consistency evaluate behaviour by
  name. There is no argument to be had about this one.
- **The profiling obligations do not attach, and that is the finding worth acting on.** Both the
  Section 42-518(a)(6) rights and the Section 42-522(c) impact assessment fire only on profiling
  in furtherance of a decision producing a legal or similarly significant effect, and this act
  narrowed that phrase to seven enumerated denials: lending, housing, insurance, education,
  criminal justice, employment, health care. A tier badge denies none of them. The striking of
  "access to essential goods or services" removed the only clause a tier could have been argued
  into. No impact assessment is owed for the classification engine under Connecticut law.
- **Enforcement is the Attorney General alone.** There is no private right of action in the act,
  which means the realistic failure mode is an AG inquiry rather than a plaintiff, and the
  realistic trigger for an AG inquiry is a complaint from a contributor who is angry about a
  label.
- Not legal advice. What would need counsel: whether a comment published on Dialecta is "publicly
  available information" at the moment of processing rather than at the moment of display. That
  single question decides whether the platform is inside CTDPA at fourteen users or outside it,
  and a non-lawyer should not be the last word on it.
