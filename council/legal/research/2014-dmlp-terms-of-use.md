# DMLP on terms of use for a site that hosts user content (archived)

**Source:** Digital Media Law Project, Berkman Klein Center, "Terms of Use", read 2026-09-21
through the fetch tool's extraction. **Archived:** the project stopped updating in 2014, though the
page now shows a September 2026 last-updated stamp from the host.
http://www.dmlp.org/legal-guide/terms-use

## Summary

For a site hosting user content it recommends: account terms; "a disclaimer of affiliation and/or
responsibility for material posted or linked to the website"; content standards (not "illegal,
obscene, defamatory, threatening, infringing of intellectual property rights, invasive of privacy
or otherwise injurious or objectionable"); a reservation of the site's own copyright; a licence
from users, for example "a non-exclusive, royalty-free, perpetual, and worldwide license to use
your content" including rights "to copy, distribute, transmit, publicly display, publicly
perform, reproduce, edit, translate and reformat your content"; "a provision or provisions
reserving your right to terminate or restrict access to a user's account, and to delete any
content posted through it"; a ban on "misleading others about their identity"; clauses
"prohibiting on-site and offline harassment"; and a DMCA policy. Its reason for the removal
right: without it, an operator "could be held liable for copyright infringement for editing or
changing the comment." It does not discuss browsewrap, liability limits, indemnity, forum or
modification.

## Implies for Dialecta

- **The licence in the draft is narrower than the DMLP model on purpose.** The model licence is
  perpetual for everything and lets the site edit and translate. Dialecta's licence is limited to
  running Dialecta, lists what it covers, and becomes permanent only for a comment once it is
  permanent. Its one editing right is formatting for display, which the sanitiser in `apps/web`
  already exercises, and which the DMLP's infringement point says has to be licensed.
- **The content standards list is the one place the draft departs from the model on the platform's
  own grounds.** "Defamatory", "threatening" and "harassment" are words with legal contours, and a
  Breach reading must never read as a finding of any of them (`2023-scotus-counterman-v-colorado.md`).
  The draft's Conduct list describes acts instead: private information, a statement that someone
  will be harmed, impersonation.
- **"Delete any content posted through it" conflicts with Permanence, so the draft does not take
  it.** The terms reserve removal only on stated grounds, and hide rather than delete where the law
  allows.
