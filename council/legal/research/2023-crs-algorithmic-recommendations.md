# CRS on algorithmic recommendations: the courts that went the other way

**Source:** Eric N. Holmes, "Liability for Algorithmic Recommendations", Congressional Research
Service Report R47753, 2023-10-12, read 2026-09-20.
https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R47753.html

## Summary

Written ten months before Moody v. NetChoice and Anderson v. TikTok, this report describes a
settled landscape that those two decisions unsettled. Its headline finding: "Social media
providers facing claims for recommending content have thus far successfully relied on Section 230
to avoid liability for their recommendations."

It walks Force v. Facebook and Gonzalez v. Google, both Anti-Terrorism Act claims, and reports
that "the courts in both Gonzalez and Force held that Section 230(c)(1) protects a social media
platform's use of algorithms to make recommendations." It quotes Dyroff v. Ultimate Software for
the proposition that recommendations "are tools meant to facilitate the communication and content
of others. They are not content in and of themselves." The report's summary of the reasoning is
that claims based on algorithmic recommendations "turn not on the creation of algorithms, but on
the defendants' curation of [content]", and curation is a publisher function 230 was written to
protect.

## Implies for Dialecta

- This is the case for the other side of the tier label question, and it should be argued at full
  strength rather than waved at. If a recommendation is a tool facilitating others' content and
  not content in itself, a platform could argue a tier badge is a sorting mechanism rather than a
  statement, and that Dialecta is doing what every forum that pins a top answer does.
- The argument is weaker for a badge than for a ranking, and the reason is in the Dyroff sentence
  itself. A recommendation surfaces someone else's words. A badge reading Heat, and a commenter
  message written by the model, are words nobody but Dialecta wrote. "Not content in and of
  themselves" is a claim that cannot be made about a sentence.
- The currency problem is the reason this note exists as a counterweight and not as a
  reassurance. R47753 predates NetChoice (July 2024) and Anderson (August 2024). It describes
  what courts had held, not what they hold. Any use of it has to carry that date.
- Practice for this seat: a CRS report is excellent on the law as of its update date and silent
  on everything after. Check the date before quoting one on a question where a Supreme Court term
  has intervened.
