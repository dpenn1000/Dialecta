# ROSCA: the three things a recurring charge owes, and they are in force

**Source:** Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act, 15 U.S.C. Sections 8401 to 8405, primary
text read 2026-09-20 at Cornell LII. Section 8403 and Section 8404 fetched and quoted; Section
8405 (state attorney general enforcement) was referenced in the navigation and not read, and is
marked as a gap below.
https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/8403
https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/8404

## Summary

Section 8403 prohibits charging a consumer for anything sold on the internet through a negative
option feature unless the seller does three things:

1. "provides text that clearly and conspicuously discloses all material terms of the transaction
   before obtaining the consumer's billing information"
2. "obtains a consumer's express informed consent before charging the consumer's credit card,
   debit card, bank account, or other financial account"
3. "provides simple mechanisms for a consumer to stop recurring charges from being placed on the
   consumer's credit card, debit card, bank account, or other financial account"

A negative option feature is any offer where the seller treats a consumer's silence or failure to
cancel as acceptance of a charge. An annually auto-renewing membership is one.

Section 8404(a): "Violation of this chapter or any regulation prescribed under this chapter shall
be treated as a violation of a rule under section 18 of the Federal Trade Commission Act ...
regarding unfair or deceptive acts or practices." That routing matters, because it converts a
ROSCA breach into an FTC Act rule violation with civil penalty exposure rather than leaving it as
a bare statutory duty.

Nothing in 8403 or 8404 creates a private right of action. Enforcement is the FTC, and state
attorneys general under 8405.

## Implies for Dialecta

- **Sequence, not content, is the binding part of requirement one.** The material terms have to be
  on screen *before* the billing field appears. A pricing page that states $50 a year, followed by
  a Stripe checkout that first collects the card and then shows the renewal terms on the receipt,
  fails a statute that is in force today.
- **"All material terms" is where the ladder lives.** If the founding price is locked for the
  first hundred and rises to $100 for everyone after, that is a material term of the transaction
  for both cohorts, and it belongs on the screen before the card field.
- **Requirement two is the reason the Pact cannot carry the payment consent.** The Pact is express
  informed consent to being classified. It says nothing about money, and consent to one thing is
  not consent to another.
- **Requirement three is a build item, not a paragraph.** A cancel control in the member's own
  account, reachable without email, satisfies it. Routing cancellation to a mailto link from a
  flow that enrolled by card is the classic failure.
- No private right of action means the realistic enforcement path is not a member suing on ROSCA.
  It is a state attorney general, a state consumer-protection claim borrowing the same facts, or a
  card network chargeback. At a hundred members the third is the only one that will actually
  happen, and it is still worth building for, because a processor reads the same three
  requirements.
- Not legal advice. Section 8405 is unread and the note does not characterise state AG powers
  beyond saying they exist.
