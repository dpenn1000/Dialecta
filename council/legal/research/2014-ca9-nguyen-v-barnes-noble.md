# Nguyen v. Barnes and Noble: a link to the terms is not agreement to the terms

**Source:** Nguyen v. Barnes and Noble, Inc., 763 F.3d 1171 (9th Cir. Aug. 18, 2014), read
2026-09-20 via an encyclopaedia summary rather than the slip opinion. The holding is quoted from
that summary and should be checked against the reporter before anything is built on the exact
wording.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguyen_v._Barnes_%26_Noble,_Inc.

## Summary

The Ninth Circuit held an online retailer's browsewrap terms unenforceable against a consumer.
The rule it stated: "where a website makes its terms of use available via a conspicuous hyperlink
of every page of the website but otherwise provides no notice to users nor prompts them to take
any affirmative action to demonstrate assent, even close proximity of the hyperlink to relevant
buttons users must click on, without more, is insufficient to give rise to constructive notice."
The court set that against clickwrap, where the user takes an affirmative act, and noted courts'
traditional reluctance to enforce browsewrap against individual consumers.

## Implies for Dialecta

- P0-D2 decides the sign-up flow, and this decides one thing inside it. If Dialecta ever wants to
  rely on its terms, whether to disclaim liability, to take an arbitration position, or to say
  what the platform may publish about a contributor, a footer link will not carry it. The sign-up
  screen needs an affirmative act and a record of it.
- The cheapest form that satisfies this is a sentence above the button saying that creating an
  account accepts the terms and the privacy policy, with both linked, plus a stored timestamp of
  the account creation. A separate checkbox is stronger still. Either is a single field on a
  screen that P0-4 is building anyway, which makes it the cheapest control this advisor will
  propose on any item.
- The Pact is the surface that already does this well and is worth borrowing from. A contributor
  who signs the Pact has taken an affirmative act with a recorded name, which is closer to what
  the Ninth Circuit asked for than any link.
- The reach is imperfect and should not be overstated. Nguyen is Ninth Circuit and Dialecta is in
  the Second. It is cited here as the canonical statement of a rule most courts apply, not as
  binding law in Connecticut.
- Not legal advice. What would need counsel: what the terms should actually say. Enforceable
  terms that say the wrong thing are worse than none.
