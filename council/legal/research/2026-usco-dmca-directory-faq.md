# The DMCA designated agent directory: six dollars, three years, and a street address

**Source:** US Copyright Office, DMCA Designated Agent Directory FAQs, read 2026-09-21 through the
fetch tool's extraction, with the quotations below as it returned them. No last-updated date is
shown on the page. A regulator, primary for its own procedure.
https://www.copyright.gov/dmca-directory/faq.html

## Summary

- **Fee:** "The current fee is $6 per designation, amendment, or resubmission."
- **What a designation needs:** the service provider's "full legal name, physical street address
  (not a post office box), any alternate names used by the service provider, and the name,
  organization, physical mail address (street address or post office box), telephone number, and
  email address of its designated agent."
- **The provider's own address cannot be a post office box**, except "only with prior approval of
  the Copyright Office" in exceptional circumstances involving documented threats to personal
  safety. The agent's address may be a post office box.
- **The agent can be an individual**, a title, a department, or a third-party entity.
- **Expiry:** a designation "will expire and become invalid three years after it is registered
  with the Office, unless the service provider renews such designation", and a lapsed one risks the
  section 512 safe harbours.
- **Each legal entity needs its own designation.** Related providers that are "separate legal
  entities ... each must have its own separate designation."
- **Alternate names** include "website names and addresses (i.e., URLs)", so dialecta.org belongs on
  the designation.
- The FAQs do not say whether the agent's details must also be posted on the provider's own site;
  the Copyright Office's section 512 page says they must (`2020-usco-section-512-resources.md`).

## Implies for Dialecta

- **The cheapest item in the drafts, and it has one trap.** The service provider's street address
  goes into a public directory. If Dan registers as an individual, that is his home address. An
  entity with a business address avoids it; the post office box exception needs a documented
  threat and the Office's approval first.
- **Decide the entity before registering, or register twice.** A designation filed in Dan's name
  does not carry over to an LLC formed later, because each legal entity needs its own. At six
  dollars the second filing costs nothing; the point is the address, not the fee.
- **A three-year renewal belongs on a calendar.** A lapsed designation is a lost safe harbour
  discovered on the day a notice arrives.
- Not legal advice. Whether a sole proprietor's trade name is enough, or the designation must be
  in Dan's own name, is a question for the lawyer review in `drafts/README.md`.
