# FTC privacy and security guidance: a privacy promise is enforceable as written

**Source:** Federal Trade Commission, "Privacy and Security" business guidance hub, read
2026-09-21 through the fetch tool's extraction. No date shown. A regulator.
https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security

## Summary

"If your company makes privacy promises, either expressly or by implication, the FTC Act
requires you to live up to those claims." And: "Think your company doesn't make any privacy
claims? Think again, and reread your privacy policy to make sure you're honoring the promises
you've pledged." (The FTC's dashes are rendered here as commas.) The hub links the COPPA Rule and FAQs, data security guidance, and sector rules
(Health Breach Notification, FCRA, GLBA, Red Flags) that do not reach Dialecta.

## Implies for Dialecta

- **The privacy notice is a list of promises, and so is every privacy statement in the Pact.** The
  Pact's § VI says the pattern profile is private, "not as a score, not as a judgment", on a site
  where the fingerprint renders publicly. Under this standard, and under A.R.S. 44-1522(C), which
  imports it (`2026-az-consumer-fraud-act-44-1521.md`), a reader's reasonable understanding of
  that line is what the platform has promised.
- **"By implication" is the part that decides the drafting.** A notice that says "only us" beside
  a column the public key can read is an implied promise already broken. That is why
  `drafts/privacy-notice.md` brackets every "only us" that depends on a grant not yet closed (B4).
- **Promise less, and keep all of it.** The notice offers the same short set of rights to everyone
  because each one can be honoured by hand at fourteen members, and nothing it offers depends on a
  law deciding to reach Dialecta.
