# COPPA for a general-audience site: actual knowledge, and a terms clause is not enough

**Source:** Federal Trade Commission, "Complying with COPPA: Frequently Asked Questions", page
reflecting the Rule as amended, last updated April 22, 2025, read 2026-09-21 through the fetch
tool's extraction. A regulator.
https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions

## Summary

COPPA reaches a general-audience site when its operator has "actual knowledge that they are
collecting, using, or disclosing personal information from children under 13", and it does not
require an operator to investigate ages. On learning it has collected a child's information, the
operator must either comply with notice and parental consent or immediately delete it. Age screens
are allowed and should be neutral, letting users "freely to enter the month and year of birth",
without encouraging a false answer. A general-audience site may block children under 13 entirely.
A ban in the terms is not enough on its own: a site "may be deemed directed to children even if
its Terms of Service prohibits children from using the site."

## Implies for Dialecta

- **The draft's under-13 sentence is the whole COPPA duty at this size:** don't knowingly collect,
  and delete on learning. The draft meets it today, at no cost.
- **The age line in the terms is not a COPPA defence, and the draft does not treat it as one.**
  What keeps Dialecta outside COPPA's directed-to-children test is that nothing about it is aimed
  at children, which is a fact about the site rather than about the terms.
- **A neutral screen matters only below 13.** For an 18 line, the draft uses a self-attestation at
  sign-up (B15). If Dan ever wants to screen for 13, the FAQ's month-and-year design is the one to
  build.
