# EFF on Section 230: it covers the smallest comment section, and it has edges

**Source:** Electronic Frontier Foundation, "Section 230" issue page, read 2026-09-20. An advocacy
organisation with a longstanding position in favour of broad Section 230 protection; read as
argument rather than as a neutral statement of law. https://www.eff.org/issues/cda230

## Summary

EFF's case is that the shield is for everyone, not only for large platforms: Section 230 "has
protected us all: small blogs and websites, big platforms, and individual users", and it lets
people speak "from vast conversational forums like Twitter and Discord, to the comment sections
of the smallest newspapers and blogs." On moderation it says the law "allows for web operators,
large and small, to moderate user speech and content as they see fit", and that varied moderation
is what lets users find the places they like.

EFF states the limits itself: the protections "are not absolute. It does not protect companies
that violate federal criminal law. It does not protect companies that create illegal or harmful
content. Nor does Section 230 protect companies from intellectual property claims."

## Implies for Dialecta

- The best argument that Dialecta's whole moderation apparatus is ordinary is here: a small
  comment section choosing what to show, in what order, with what prominence, is the paradigm
  case 230 was written for. Everything in the tier system that is only a sorting or suppression
  decision sits squarely inside that.
- EFF's own list of limits contains the concession that matters. Section 230 "does not protect
  companies that create ... content". Dialecta creates content: the commenter message is written
  by a model Dialecta runs, to a prompt Dialecta wrote, and published under Dialecta's name.
  Even the source most committed to a broad shield does not claim the shield covers that.
- Read this as the platform's strongest friendly brief, not as authority. It cites no case for
  the propositions above and is not trying to.
