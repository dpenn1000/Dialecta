# Wiley Rein on the 2026 CTDPA amendments: the thresholds, and a profiling duty with a date on it

**Source:** Kathleen E. Scott, Joan Stewart and Ania Trichet, "Major Changes to Connecticut
Consumer Privacy Law Will Take Effect July 1, 2026", Wiley Rein LLP client alert, 2026-04-27,
read 2026-09-20. A named practising firm writing for clients, so read as competent summary rather
than as authority.
https://www.wiley.law/alert-Major-Changes-to-Connecticut-Consumer-Privacy-Law-Will-Take-Effect-July-1-2026

## Summary

The alert sets out the three new applicability triggers: controlled or processed personal data of
at least 35,000 consumers, lowered from 100,000; processing sensitive data, with no volume
threshold; and offering consumers' personal data for sale, with no volume threshold. It reports
the sensitive data definition expanding to take in government identifiers such as driver's
licence and passport numbers, financial account elements, and Social Security numbers, with
consent required before processing or selling them.

On automated processing it reports two new obligations. Controllers must grant consumers rights
"to question outcomes, be informed of reasoning, review data used". And controllers must conduct
impact assessments for "any profiling for the purposes of making a decision that produces any
legal or similarly significant effect", which the alert dates to August 1, 2026, a month after
the rest. It also notes a flat prohibition on targeted advertising and sale of personal data for
people aged 13 to 17 "regardless of consent".

## Implies for Dialecta

- The alert is right on the three triggers and right that the sensitive data trigger has no
  floor. Read against the enrolled Public Act filed at
  `council/legal/research/2025-ct-public-act-25-113.md`, it is a faithful summary.
- The alert's phrasing of the profiling rights, stated without the qualifier, is the trap in this
  source. Read alone it sounds as though any controller that profiles owes a right to question
  the outcome. The statute attaches both the rights and the impact assessment only to profiling
  in furtherance of a decision producing a legal or similarly significant effect, and that phrase
  was narrowed by the same act to seven enumerated denials. A client alert compresses; the
  compression is where a non-lawyer gets it wrong.
- The 13 to 17 prohibition is worth carrying into P0-D2 even though Dialecta sells no data and
  runs no targeted advertising today. If a future revenue model touches either, the age band is a
  hard stop and not a consent question, which makes it a constraint on `treasurer` rather than a
  control on `security`.
- The August 1, 2026 date matters for sequencing. Both dates are already past as of this filing,
  so nothing here is a deadline to plan for; it is present law.
