# DMLP on publishing others' content: your commentary is yours

**Source:** Digital Media Law Project, "Publishing the Statements and Content of Others", Berkman
Klein Center for Internet and Society, Harvard University, read 2026-09-20. Archived; the project
stopped updating in 2014. https://www.dmlp.org/legal-guide/publishing-statements-and-content-others

## Summary

Written for exactly the structure Dialecta has: an operator who hosts other people's words and
adds some of their own. Its statement of the rule is plain: "if you add your own commentary along
with the user-submitted content, you will only be shielded from liability for the material
created by your user, not for your own statements."

On editing, the page is permissive and specific about the limit: "You will not lose this immunity
even if you edit the content, whether for accuracy or civility, and you will be entitled to
immunity so long as your edits do not substantially alter the meaning of the original
statements." Its example of crossing the line is an edit that turns "Bob is not a murderer" into
"Bob is a murderer".

## Implies for Dialecta

- The rule splits Dialecta's comment card into two publications on one surface. The comment body
  is the contributor's and is shielded. The badge, the AI Classification Card, the commenter
  message and the Breach notice are Dialecta's own commentary alongside it, and this source says
  plainly that the shield does not follow them.
- The edit rule is the cleanest available statement of why the platform's other moves are safe.
  Suppressing a Breach comment, holding a comment for Stage 2, sorting by tier, filtering by tier
  in the topology bar: none of those alter the meaning of a contributor's statement, so none of
  them cost the shield.
- This source is eleven years old and its author stopped updating in 2014. It is filed for the
  mechanism, which has not moved, and not for currency. Nothing load bearing should rest on it
  alone; it agrees with CRS R46751, which is current, and that agreement is the reason to trust
  it.
- Not legal advice.
