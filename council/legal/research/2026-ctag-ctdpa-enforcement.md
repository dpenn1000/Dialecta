# Connecticut Attorney General on the CTDPA: who enforces it, and for how much

**Source:** Office of the Attorney General, State of Connecticut, "The Connecticut Data Privacy
Act", read 2026-09-20. The enforcing regulator's own public page.
https://portal.ct.gov/ag/sections/privacy/the-connecticut-data-privacy-act

## Summary

The page states the current coverage test in the same three parts as the amended statute: 35,000
or more consumers, processing sensitive data, or selling consumer data. It adds that all consumer
health data controllers are covered regardless of size, and lists the exempt categories: state
and local government, nonprofits except as to health data, financial institutions, HIPAA covered
entities, and institutions of higher education.

On consumer rights it lists access, correction, deletion, portability, the right to learn who
received the data, and opt out of targeted advertising and sale, with universal opt out signals
honoured since January 1, 2025.

Two sentences carry the practical weight. "The Attorney General has exclusive authority to
enforce violations of the Act", and there is no private right of action. Penalties run through
the Connecticut Unfair Trade Practices Act at up to 5,000 dollars per violation, alongside
injunctive relief, restitution and disgorgement.

The page as read on 2026-09-20 carries the 35,000 figure but says nothing about the July 2026
amendments as such, and nothing about the new profiling rights or the impact assessment duty. It
is current on thresholds and behind on the rest.

## Implies for Dialecta

- No private right of action is the single most useful fact about Connecticut exposure for a
  fourteen user platform. Nobody can sue Dialecta under the CTDPA. The realistic worst case is an
  Attorney General inquiry, and the realistic trigger for one is a contributor complaint about a
  published label, which ties this file to the tier label position rather than to any schema
  decision.
- The nonprofit exemption is not a route out. It is limited to 501(c) organisations and does not
  reach health data even for them, and Dialecta is not one.
- 5,000 dollars per violation is a number that scales with the number of consumers affected, so
  at fourteen members the whole theoretical exposure is small. That is a reason to spend on the
  cheapest real controls and not on a compliance programme, which is what the charter's
  constraint section asks this seat to say out loud.
- The page's silence on the 2026 amendments is a caution about regulator pages generally: they
  are authoritative on what they say and not a complete statement of what the statute says. The
  enrolled act is the source to quote.
