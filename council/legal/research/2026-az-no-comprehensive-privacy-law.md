# Arizona has no comprehensive consumer privacy statute in force

**Source:** Cross-checked across four 2026 state-privacy trackers (Security.org, Termly, Securiti,
Recording Law) against the Arizona Legislature's own bill tracker, read 2026-09-20. Trackers are
compiled by vendors with a compliance-tooling interest and are marked as such; the fact itself, an
absence, is confirmed by the absence of any enrolled act and by the pending-bill status below.

## Summary

Arizona has not enacted a CCPA/VCDPA-style comprehensive consumer data privacy act as of
2026-09-20. A bill, SB 1815, was introduced in the Arizona Senate on 2026-02-09; per LegiScan's
bill history, read 2026-09-20, it died in committee without a hearing and is not on a path to
passage this session. `positions/2026-09-20-consent-waiver-and-the-pact.md`'s companion research
describes "two 2026 bills" dying; this note independently confirms only SB 1815 and has not found
the second. Treat "two bills" as unverified by this note specifically until it is. Arizona
residents currently have no state-law right to access, correct,
delete, or port personal data held by a private business, and no state-law right to opt out of
sale or targeted advertising, of the kind Connecticut, California, Virginia and roughly twenty
other states now provide.

This is an absence, not a silence this seat is filling in. Do not read it as "Arizona has weaker
privacy law than Connecticut" in general; Arizona's breach notification statute and its
constitutional privacy clause are separate and are filed separately
(`2026-az-breach-notification-18-552.md`). It is specific to the CTDPA-shaped question this tree
has been asking: is there a second state, alongside Connecticut, whose comprehensive privacy act
now reaches Dialecta because Dan lives there. There is not.

Arizona does carry narrower, sector-specific statutes noted by the trackers and not independently
verified here because they are outside what this correction needs: genetic testing privacy,
student records, and insurance information. None of the four trackers describe anything resembling
the CTDPA's sensitive-data trigger or its profiling-rights regime.

## Implies for Dialecta

- The relocation itself does not add a second comprehensive-privacy regime on top of whatever
  CTDPA exposure already exists through Connecticut-resident readers. That is the good-news half
  of the jurisdiction correction and it is worth stating plainly rather than only stating the risk.
- SB 1815 is dead for this session, not merely pending. The conclusion is the same either way,
  no comprehensive law reaches Dialecta, but "died in committee" is the more precise fact and the
  cheaper one to have to walk back if a future sprint assumes it was still live.
- This does not touch GDPR, which reaches Dialecta through reader location in the EU regardless of
  where Dan or Dialecta sit (`council/security/research/2016-eu-gdpr-territorial-scope.md`), and
  does not touch any other US state's comprehensive act reaching Dialecta through a resident
  reader the way Connecticut's does. That question, which state's residents Dialecta actually has,
  is unresearched in this tree and unresearched here.
- Not legal advice. What would settle it beyond today's reading: a docket check on SB 1815 at the
  point Dialecta next revisits this, since a bill's status is the one part of this note with a
  shelf life.
