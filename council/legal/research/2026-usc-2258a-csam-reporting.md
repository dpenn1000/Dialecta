# 18 U.S.C. Section 2258A: the one mandatory report, and what it does not require

**Source:** 18 U.S.C. Section 2258A, "Reporting requirements of providers", Cornell Legal
Information Institute, read 2026-09-20.
https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2258A

## Summary

A provider of an electronic communication service or remote computing service that obtains
"actual knowledge of any facts or circumstances" indicating an apparent violation of the federal
child sexual abuse material statutes must report to the CyberTipline of the National Center for
Missing and Exploited Children "as soon as reasonably possible after obtaining actual knowledge".
The duty is mandatory for apparent violations and discretionary for imminent ones. Subsection (b)
sets out what a report may contain: user identity, email and IP addresses, payment data,
timestamps, geographic information, the visual depictions themselves, and the complete
communication containing them.

Penalties in subsection (e) are graded by size, not waived by it. A first knowing and wilful
failure to report costs up to 600,000 dollars for a provider under 100 million monthly users and
850,000 dollars for a larger one; subsequent failures are 850,000 and 1,000,000 dollars.

Subsection (f) is the counterweight. Nothing in the section requires a provider to "monitor any
user", to monitor content, or to "affirmatively search, screen, or scan" for violations. The duty
attaches to knowledge, not to diligence.

## Implies for Dialecta

- This is the one reporting duty that does not care that Dialecta has fourteen members, and the
  penalty figures do not shrink to match the platform. It is also the one duty triggered by
  something Dialecta does not currently accept: comments are text, and the section is about
  visual depictions.
- The trigger is therefore a product decision, not a legal one. The day a comment, a profile, or
  an article accepts an uploaded image, this duty attaches and a CyberTipline path has to exist
  before the first upload, not after the first report. That belongs on the backlog as a
  precondition of any image feature rather than as work of its own.
- Subsection (f) is the reason no scanning obligation follows. Dialecta is not required to build
  detection, and building detection is not the cheap control here. Knowing where to send a report
  is.
- The classification pipeline changes the knowledge analysis in a way worth noticing. Every
  comment passes through a model that reads it. If images ever pass through the same path, the
  platform will have looked at everything, which makes "actual knowledge" easier to establish
  than it would be for a service that never looks.
- Not legal advice. What would need counsel: whether registering with the CyberTipline before
  accepting images is required or merely sensible, and what a one person operator's report
  handling looks like in practice.
