# What the silent round left open

*`legal` position, 2026-09-20, path-to-launch. The convener planned a rebuttal-round launch for

## Brief

Three answers to what my silence left open. One: the Archetype is materially more exposed than a
per-comment badge, under Arizona and Ninth Circuit law, on three independent grounds, consent
scope, Roommates.com's material-contribution test, and Milkovich's disclosed-basis defense losing
its anchor once the artifact outlives one comment. Two: terms taken by an affirmative act have to
exist the moment `upgrade_url` stops being null, not at Phase 6 billing, so write it once, now,
into the build already underway. Three: media liability insurance has never been priced against
this plan. My own standing position recommended pricing it and nobody has called a broker yet.

## Position

### 1. The Archetype against a per-comment badge (Ariz., 9th Cir.)

Materially different, not the same exposure at larger size.

**Consent does not reach it.** Restatement Section 583 runs consent to a specific publication, not
a subject in the abstract. Stage 2 covers a comment's badge and Contrast Strip; nothing on the
consent ladder in `consent-at-the-moment.md` reaches an aggregate visible to anyone but its
subject.

**Section 230 protects it less, not more.** Roommates.com, 521 F.3d 1157 (9th Cir. 2008) (en
banc), binds here since the move: a platform that requires answers into categories it chose, and
builds its own output from them, is a co-developer, not a host. Eight archetypes, built from a
history the contributor never wrote comment by comment, fit that test more squarely than one tier
word beside its disclosed basis.

**Milkovich has nothing to hold onto.** The badge survives on a disclosed basis a reader can check
against the comment on screen. The Archetype characterizes a history no reader sees, with no fact
to check it against, the same finding as this seat's own on the Contrast Strip and Breach notice,
now with no contest path either.

Yetman v. English, 168 Ariz. 71 (1991), keeps this a jury question either way, not a pleadings
dismissal: architecture before publication, or exposure carried on purpose. Where an Arizona jury
draws it needs a licensed Arizona attorney reading a rendered Archetype card.

**Whether the gate is legal too.** Yes. `security` found the visibility default unenforceable today, a
migration against a session that does not exist. The same migration is where the missing consent
event belongs: nobody consents into a control that does not exist. One build item, not two.

### 2. Underwriter, terms at signup

Before a person pays: a privacy notice (FTC Act Section 5 binds regardless of state) and terms
taken by an affirmative act, Nguyen v. Barnes & Noble, 763 F.3d 1171 (9th Cir. 2014), binding here,
covering the right to classify and publish, cancellation, and what it gates.

None of it predates treasurer's move to price the number now, privately, in a config file. It has
to exist the moment `upgrade_url` stops being null. Treasurer's own finding, that Vercel's Hobby
plan turns non-compliant "the moment a membership exists, not the moment one is billed," applies
here too: affirmative-act terms are owed at that same moment, not at Phase 6 charging. The Pact is
already the mechanism; extend it once, in the shared build.

### 3. Media liability insurance, priced against this plan

Not priced. My own standing position recommended pricing it before launch and flagged whether a
policy covers statements the insured's own model generated as wording for a broker to settle
before binding. No carrier, quote, or call is on record since. Ask specifically about AI-assigned
characterization of a named person, run from Arizona by one operator with no revenue; a generic
media E&O quote may not contemplate that product. This needs a licensed broker, not another source
read.
