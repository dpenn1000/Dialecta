# P0-D2: open sign-up or invite-only, argued from exposure

*`legal` position on the council question framed at
`council/log/2026-09-19-p0-d2-login-methods.md`. Written 2026-09-20, after the other seats had
argued cost and completion rates and before rebuttals. Not counsel. Nothing here is legal advice.*

## The position

**Open sign-up, conditional on three things existing before the first stranger can create an
account.** The gate that matters is not the sign-up form. It is whether the platform has decided,
in writing, what it publishes about people and where a threat goes.

The three:

1. Terms and a privacy policy, accepted by an affirmative act on the sign-up screen.
2. The basis shipping beside every published label, which is backlog A-5.
3. A written rule for what happens to a Breach comment that describes a real threat.

If those exist, open sign-up adds very little unshielded exposure per additional user. If they do
not, invite-only is worth choosing purely to buy the weeks it takes to build them, and that is
the only argument for it this seat finds persuasive.

## Why the safety case for invite-only is weaker than it looks

`treasurer` argued P0-D2 on cost and `designer` on completion rates. The council log records that
nobody argued what happens when the first bad actor arrives. Having now argued it, the conclusion
runs against the instinct.

**Section 230 protects the thing open sign-up multiplies, and does not protect the thing it does
not.** A stranger's comment is information provided by another information content provider and
is shielded on the face of 230(c)(1) (`2026-usc-section-230-text.md`). The exposure that is
unshielded is what Dialecta publishes about that stranger, and that exposure exists identically
for the fourteen members already here. Opening sign-up scales the protected risk, not the
unprotected one. The invite gate does not touch the part that is actually exposed.

**Invite-only moves the exposure rather than reducing it.** Every person who gets in was vouched
for by Dan personally. A bad outcome from an invited contributor is a choice with a name on it in
a way that a stranger's conduct is not. That is worse, not better.

**An invite list is a list of people, held for no purpose except the gate.** The charter would
veto collecting a category of data because it is interesting rather than because a feature needs
it, and this seat should apply that to a control it might otherwise like. An invite table holds
email addresses of people who never became members, with no retention rule and no notice
covering them.

**Where invite-only genuinely helps is Connecticut, and the help is temporary.** The only door
the CTDPA has into Dialecta is processing a Connecticut resident's sensitive data, which has no
volume floor (`2025-ct-public-act-25-113.md`, and the analysis at
`council/legal/positions/2026-09-20-ctdpa-sensitive-data.md`). At fourteen known people it is
possible to know who is a Connecticut resident. With open sign-up it is not. That is a real
difference and it is worth exactly as much as the answer to the one open question in that
position, which is whether a published comment is publicly available information at the moment of
processing. If it is, this advantage disappears.

## What has to exist before the gate opens, and why

**Terms accepted by an affirmative act.** A conspicuous footer link does not create constructive
notice; the user has to be prompted to act (`2014-ca9-nguyen-v-barnes-noble.md`). This is one
sentence above the button on a screen P0-4 is building anyway, plus the timestamp the account
creation already writes. It is the cheapest control in this whole tree and it is the difference
between having terms and having a document nobody is bound by. The Pact is the surface that
already does this well and is worth borrowing from.

**The basis beside the label.** Open sign-up means the platform will publish an AI-authored
characterisation of a person it has never met, who has no reason to give it the benefit of the
doubt, and who can send a demand letter as cheaply as a member can send an email. Section 230
does not shield that label (`2024-crs-section-230-overview.md`). What does defend it is that the
AI Classification Card publishes the reasoning next to the badge, so a reader can check the
platform's work. That is backlog A-5 and it is the item this seat would veto shipping without.

**A routing rule for Breach.** No US law requires a platform to report a credible threat to
anyone (`2026-vandort-reporting-gap.md`). The Stored Communications Act permits voluntary
disclosure where the provider in good faith believes an emergency involving danger of death or
serious physical injury requires disclosure without delay, and that phrase is the standard a rule
can be written to. It is much narrower than what Breach detects: most Breach comments are
insults, and a rule that treats an insult like an emergency will either over-report or be
ignored. Open sign-up is what makes a real one arrive. Deciding during the incident is the
failure mode the charter exists to prevent.

## One thing that changes the answer outright

**Images.** Dialecta is text today, so 18 U.S.C. Section 2258A does not bite. The day a comment,
profile or article accepts an uploaded image, a mandatory federal reporting duty attaches to a
one person operator, with penalties that do not shrink for a small provider
(`2026-usc-2258a-csam-reporting.md`). Open sign-up plus image upload is the combination that
creates it. If any image feature is in scope for the same phase as opening sign-up, this position
flips to invite-only until a CyberTipline path exists.

## What this seat does not claim

`designer` owns whether an invite gate depresses the funnel, and `treasurer` owns what each
branch costs to build, which the council log notes cannot be costed until the live schema
question resolves. This position does not dispute either. It says only that the liability
argument, which is the one nobody had made, does not favour invite-only, and that the safety
argument people expect it to make is mostly about documents rather than about doors.

Nothing here is a reason to delay P0-4. Two of the three conditions are single fields on screens
being built anyway. The third is a paragraph.
