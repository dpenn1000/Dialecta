# The fingerprint, read by a stranger

*`legal` position, 2026-09-20, full council debate. Frame:
`council/log/2026-09-20-fingerprint-legibility-and-model.md`. Not counsel. Nothing here is legal
advice. Every claim names its state or circuit and cites a filed note, and a claim with no note
behind it is marked (unsourced). Three notes were filed for this position; they are listed at the
end.*

## Brief

Legibility turns the fingerprint from an impression into the platform's assertion about a named
person, and tonight's test shows it can be false. Three test profiles hold almost no Heat, and all
three strangers' notes say they ran hot; Father Anselm, with none, got "lately their arguments have
been getting heated", because the page teaches that a rippling line is heat and the render ripples
every line on purpose. Milkovich does not shelter opinion that implies a fact no stranger can
check, and tonight's log makes this a known defect. The contest path on the table would not have
helped him: every classification he has is right. Nothing beyond self-visible ships until every
taught channel matches the record.

## The strangers' notes

Nobody has been harmed yet. The seven test profiles are synthetic (`scripts/profile_gallery.py`),
and the three fingerprints the live page renders belong to seed fixtures: Maya Reiss, Wen Zhao and
Father Anselm Okafor. The live `archetypes` table, readable by the anonymous key, holds exactly
three rows, and they are those three
(`exchange/open/2026-09-20-004-blindspot-p0-6-would-import-test-accounts.md`). Defamation and false
light both need a real person. The exposure starts the first time a stranger can read a real
member's mark, and tonight measured what that mark will say.

In the condition closest to a stranger on a profile, one mark and a legend with nothing to compare
it against:

| Profile | Heat on record | The stranger's note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Father Anselm | None, and no Stance | "lately their arguments have been getting heated" |
| Wen Zhao | A trace, turbulence 0.025 | "Lately they've been running hotter than they used to" |
| Priya Raman | A trace, turbulence 0.011 | "They ran hot early on and have mostly cooled off since" |
| Dolores Vance | Real, and all of it early | "They ran hot early on", right by coincidence, since the render draws her heat at the rim where recent work sits |

All seven notes report heat (`answers.json`). The mechanism is the platform's twice over. The
render gives every mark a noise floor on purpose, and on Anselm it is 9.6 percent of the ring's
radius at the core. The live page teaches that "turbulence generates visible wave patterns in the
rings" (`_theme/page-fingerprint.hbs` line 365). A stranger who reads the mark the way the page
says reads heat into a man who has none.

## 1. The fingerprint as the platform's statement

**Section 230 stays where it was, because it never covered the mark.** Dialecta wrote the engine,
chose the channels and publishes the result, and 230(f)(3) makes anyone responsible "in whole or in
part" for creating information its content provider (`research/2026-usc-section-230-text.md`).
High confidence, and tonight changes nothing about it.

**The merits move.** Arizona asks whether the average reader would take a statement to state or
imply a provably false fact, judged on its whole context, and leaves that to a jury except in the
clearest cases (`research/1991-az-yetman-v-english-opinion-privilege.md`; Turner v. Devlin, in
`research/1990-scotus-milkovich-v-lorain-journal.md`). A shape nobody could decode implied nothing
provable and sat close to art. Tonight the same shape decoded into the right person at 90 to 100
percent, and into sentences about how a person argues and when. Three things fix what it means, and
all three are the platform's own:

1. **The legend.** The page tells a reader what each channel means, so the implication a stranger
   draws is the one the platform taught.
2. **The stated purpose.** Dan: "I want a person to get a feel for this users communication style
   quickly if they look at it and understand the basics about fingerprint mechanics." A plaintiff
   would open with that sentence, because it says the mark exists to be read as a description of a
   person by someone the platform has taught to read it.
3. **The framing.** "Fingerprint", "no two are the same", "cannot be edited". Each tells a reader to
   take the mark as a record of fact rather than as a view.
   `positions/2026-09-20-ctdpa-sensitive-data.md` already flagged "unfakeable" as the first word a
   demand letter would quote.

**The basis is withheld from exactly the people who can now read the mark.** The badge survives
Milkovich because its basis ships beside it: the comment and the AI Classification Card. The
fingerprint has no card. `comments`, `classifications` and `axis_events` are all closed to the
anonymous key (baseline schema lines 525, 563 and 616). The most a logged-out stranger can reach is
up to five Forum-tier comments on the recovered contributor page (`_recovered/api/contributor.js`
line 133), which is the basis for the flattering channels and for none of the others. A mark whose
negative channels imply facts the reader cannot see is the statement Milkovich describes, one that
"implies a knowledge of facts which lead to the conclusion."

**So consent carries the weight, and consent does not reach an error.** With the disclosed basis
this thin, the platform's defence is that the contributor agreed to be described, in the Pact and
at first render (ADR-004). Consent runs to a publication, and agreeing that the platform may
describe you is not agreeing that it may describe you wrongly
(`research/1977-restatement-583-consent-to-defamation.md`). Anselm agreed to a fingerprint. He did
not agree to heat he never had, and no Pact wording can make him.

**False light fits these defects better than defamation does.** Arizona adopts the Restatement:
publicity placing a person in a false light that "would be highly offensive to a reasonable
person," by someone with "knowledge of or ... reckless disregard as to the falsity" (Godbehere v.
Phoenix Newspapers, 162 Ariz. 335 (1989); Hart v. Seven Resorts, 947 P.2d 846 (1997);
`research/1989-az-godbehere-false-light.md`). It needs no harm to reputation, and it reaches a true
record presented so that it "creates a false implication." Dolores Vance is that case twice: real
heat drawn as recent, and 22 real graduations on Discourse drawn at 70 percent, so that her stranger
wrote "they don't tend to stay for a long back-and-forth."

**Where it lands.** "Heated" is mild. It probably fails both torts: it is not clearly defamatory,
and it is unlikely to be "a major misrepresentation of [the plaintiff's] character, history,
activities or beliefs," the bar Godbehere sets. Nobody wins a judgment over a heat reading. Tonight
changed two other things.

- **The knowledge element is on the record.** Before tonight a heat false positive was an unknown
  artifact. Tonight's log measures it and traces it to the code, dated, in the platform's own
  repository, and every legible mark published after that date without the fix is published with
  that knowledge. The readers were one vision model, which weakens the test as evidence of what a
  reader understands and leaves it whole as evidence of notice, because the mechanism comes from the
  code and not from the readers.
- **Heat is the rehearsal for the Breach residual.** A false Breach reading, that a person targets
  people rather than ideas, plausibly clears both of the bars Heat does not. `designer` now draws
  the residual by thinning the record's density at the radius of its date, with a floor of 0.07
  that never leaves (`council/designer/positions/2026-09-20-colour-and-the-breach-curve.md`). The
  test's ledger legend reads density as "how fast the person writes", and a quiet month thins the
  same variable. Nobody has tested whether a stranger reads a quiet month as a Breach, and tonight
  says to assume one will until it is measured.

## 2. Fidelity before strangers

**Every channel the page teaches has to be true to the record, for every person, before the mark
is shown to anyone but its owner.** Three rules follow, and each is cheap.

| Rule | What it closes | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No channel shows what the record does not hold | Anselm: the noise floor and heat share a grammar | `designer`'s ruling, already owed |
| The page teaches no reading the render cannot carry | Timing. `tierMix` holds no time, and line 266 says "Old Heat persists at the center; outer rings calm", the opposite of what the engine draws | Copy. `spec-reader` has the full list |
| Every channel that can shame passes a false-positive bar set before the study | Heat now, and Breach before it ships | One more column in a study already planned |

**The bar.** The frame asks for a heat false-positive rate with its bar set before anything runs.
This seat's floor, which `philosopher` may raise: for a record holding no Heat and no Stance, fewer
than one reader in five reports heat in the single-mark task, because that is the stranger's
condition. A smooth control mark in the same study shows how much of the rate comes from the legend
rather than the render. Tonight's score is three readers of three.

**The Breach residual stays with its owner.** `designer` hands its visibility to `philosopher` and
this seat. This seat's answer is owner-only until three things exist: the same false-positive test
run for Breach on records that hold a quiet month and no Breach, the render-level contest path in
section 3, and the Arizona opinion-privilege hour ADR-004 already points at the residual first.
Dan's user control stands for the rest of the mark. `designer` calls the floor "the difference
between a record and a reputation". In law the line runs the other way: a record is what the
platform keeps, a reputation is what strangers read, and a floor strangers can read is a reputation
that never fades.

**Self-visible can ship today.** Every defamation claim needs publication to someone other than the
person described (Turner, in `research/1990-scotus-milkovich-v-lorain-journal.md`). A mark only its
owner can see carries no exposure, and it is where the owner's basis view in section 3 lives.
ADR-004 already ships every level but self-visible disabled until the predicate policy exists. This
adds two conditions to the same switch, fidelity and the contest path. Connections-only is still
publication and waits with public.

**Keep a model from writing the stranger's note.** Tonight previews a feature someone will propose:
a model that writes what a fingerprint says about a person. It wrote a confident, unflattering,
false sentence about three people in seven. That is the commenter message problem at the scale of a
person, the least consentable thing the platform can publish
(`positions/2026-09-20-consent-waiver-and-the-pact.md`). If the page ever sets words beside a mark,
they come from fixed templates filled from counts, which a person can be shown in advance and a
stranger can check.

## 3. The contest path

ADR-004's amendment left the contest path open: "Neither blocks." Tonight it blocks, for every
level beyond self-visible. This seat's charter already vetoes "a public profile of a person's
thinking, without a stated basis a contributor can see and contest," and legibility is what makes
the fingerprint that profile.

**The contest path on the table would not have helped Father Anselm.** Community re-review, A-7 and
A-8, contests a classification, and every one of his classifications is right. The false statement
is in the drawing, and nobody can nominate a noise floor for reclassification. A contest path that
stops at the classification layer catches the engine's mistakes and misses the renderer's, and
tonight the renderer made the only mistake anyone measured.

What reaches the render, cheapest first:

| Piece | What it does | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The owner's basis view** | Channel by channel, what the page teaches it means beside what the record holds: a rippling line, read as heat; this record, no Heat and no Stance | The data is already there. `axis_scores.tier_mix` is readable by the anonymous key today (baseline schema line 653), which is `security`'s to weigh as an exposure of its own |
| **A way to say "this reads wrong"** | Reaches Dan, naming the channel | `feedback_items` exists |
| **A rule for which side gets fixed** | Where the drawing and the record disagree, the drawing changes, for everyone, and the change is logged | A sentence added to ADR-004 |
| Classification re-review, A-7 and A-8 | Contests the engine | Already owed, on the session-verified auth timeline |

The basis view doubles as the fingerprint's own Classification Card, if strangers ever see it. The
more of the basis a stranger can reach, the stronger the disclosed-basis defence: printed counts
would have told Anselm's reader "no Heat on record" beside a mark that said otherwise. Whether
strangers see counts is `philosopher`'s call, because counts beside a name make a scoreboard. This
seat's half of that answer: every channel a stranger can read and cannot check is exposure the
platform carries.

## 4. The share card

**A card carries a characterisation of a person only when that person is the one sharing it,
having seen that exact card.** Every other card carries the person's own words and name, or the
site's mark, and nothing the platform concluded about them.

Tonight extends this seat's answer on `circulation-01` from words to the image. The image decodes
into sentences, so a fingerprint on a card is a fingerprint-derived descriptor, drawn instead of
written. It stays off for three reasons, heaviest first.

1. **A cached card is the one copy the visibility setting cannot reach.** Facebook and X keep the
   image they fetched. A render defect shipped on a card is permanent, the one place where "can
   never be changed" is exactly true, and the worst place for it.
2. **Consent does not travel.** Section 583 consent runs to a specific publication. A Pact
   visibility choice covers a profile on Dialecta, beside its legend. A stranger's feed has
   neither.
3. **The basis does not travel.** The badge's reasoning, one step further: nothing on Facebook lets
   anyone check the mark.

The self-share exception rests on Section 583 at its strongest, a person choosing that publication
after seeing it. It still waits on section 2, because nobody can consent to an error they cannot
see.

`circulation`'s brief says the card already excludes the fingerprint in shipped code. The article
card does, both in `apps/web` and on the live sidecar, where it is a photo. Two other routes on that
sidecar do not. `dialecta-next` is a live Vercel project last deployed 2026-05-06
(`docs/SITE-INVENTORY.md`), and `docs/Dialecta_Project_Index.md` line 240 says all five of its card
routes "stay live".

| Route on `dialecta-next` | Carries | Linked from anything |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `contributor/[handle]/opengraph-image` | The archetype, and "Strong on X and Y" | No. The profile page's `og:image` is the avatar (`_recovered/api/contributor.js` line 115) |
| `comment/[id]/opengraph-image` | The tier word beside the member's name | No. Its own header says comment-sharing UI does not exist yet, and suppressed comments are excluded |

**Accept both for now.** Nothing links to either, the only archetypes on record belong to seed
fixtures, and the contributor card's words are flattering. The rule attaches the day any UI links
to either route, and a comment card that prints a tier word without its Classification Card is the
bare badge this seat already vetoed for A-5.

## 5. "Can never be changed"

Dan's property holds for one layer of the fingerprint and fails for two, and the law treats the
three differently.

| Layer | Can it be "never changed"? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The record**: hardened comments, the append-only ledger | Yes, for US adults | Built, and disclosed by affirmative act. Below |
| **The drawing**: what the engine makes of the record | No | Every ruling tonight redraws every mark, and should. A drawing that can never be corrected is a permanent false statement from the day it is wrong |
| **The display**: who can see it | No | Consent runs to a publication and can be narrowed. The visibility setting is what keeps a permanent record lawful to show |

**The record is already permanent, and lawfully so.** A comment hardens an hour after posting
(baseline schema line 518), and after that the recovered API refuses deletion: "The comment has
hardened into the public record and cannot be removed" (`_recovered/api/comment/[id].js` line 313).
The Pact discloses it by affirmative act: "Once your post hardens, it is yours permanently" (§ V).
Arizona gives an adult no right to delete what they wrote
(`research/2026-az-no-comprehensive-privacy-law.md`), and no statute this seat has read gives one
that reaches a fourteen-member site; the twenty-state question stays open. One mismatch to fix: the
Pact gives the reflection window as 30 minutes (`components/dialecta-pact.html` line 1202) and the
code enforces 60. More time than promised harms nobody, but the document a person signs should
state the rule the code runs.

**Where the record stops being permanent.** Four places, none of them live today:

| Who | What the law requires | Reaches the mark? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **EU residents** | Profiling behaviour is a plausible GDPR Article 3(2)(b) trigger on its own (`council/security/research/2016-eu-gdpr-territorial-scope.md`). Then Article 17 erasure, and Article 21's right to object "at any time" to profiling, stated "clearly and separately" at first contact (`research/2016-gdpr-art-17-21-erasure-and-objection.md`). The UK's version is not read in this tree | Yes. Article 21 names profiling. Article 17(3)(a) may keep a comment in a public thread; this seat does not read it as protecting a profile the platform wrote about the person |
| **California minors** | An operator with actual knowledge that a minor is using the site must let a registered minor remove what they posted, operative 2015-01-01, and hiding it from others satisfies the duty (`research/2013-ca-bpc-22581-minor-content-removal.md`). The Pact states no age requirement | No. The section reaches content "posted ... by the user", which is the comment and not the platform's reading of it |
| **Any author, on copyright** | "Your words are yours here" is the Pact's closing line (line 1318), and no terms exist yet. What licence Dialecta holds to keep showing a hardened comment its author wants gone is open (unsourced) | No. A drawing derived from a comment is not a copy of it (unsourced) |
| **Anyone promised deletion** | A privacy notice binds under FTC Act Section 5, which A.R.S. 44-1522(C) imports (`research/2026-az-consumer-fraud-act-44-1521.md`) | As far as the promise goes |

**Trace and evidence travel together.** Keeping a mark's trace after deleting the comment behind it
keeps an assertion and destroys its proof, the Breach notice's problem made permanent. The recovered
delete path gets this right inside the window: the classification and its events go, and then
`recomputeAxisScores` replays the ledger (`_recovered/api/comment/[id].js` line 343). Two copies sit
outside it. The baseline schema's own identity note says nine tables key on a bare `member_id` with
no foreign key (line 88); `axis_scores` and `archetypes` are two of them, both readable by the
anonymous key, so an account deletion would leave the public aggregate behind. None is built, so
none should be promised. And `fp_snapshots` uploads rendered PNGs to "public Supabase storage"
(`_recovered/api/_fp-snapshot.js` line 290), a copy that no table policy, no visibility setting and
no deletion reaches. A person who chooses self-visible needs those copies to follow, or the setting
tells them their restriction worked when it did not, the failure ADR-004 adopted this seat's rule to
prevent.

What survives, as a line Dan could put on the page, with `voice-editor` owning the wording: *No one
can edit the record behind it, including us. When the drawing gets the record wrong, we fix the
drawing.*

## 6. What Dan is missing

"I want the fingerprint to tell the story" makes the platform the narrator, and a narrator answers
for every sentence the story lets a reader write. The good, bad and ugly are fine. What the law
punishes on this surface is the untrue, and a true mark the person agreed to is the best-defended
thing on the site. The test shows that legibility and accuracy are one requirement. A beautiful mark
nobody could read carried almost no exposure. A mark strangers can read carries the full weight of a
statement, and today it tells a stranger that every calm person on the site runs hot.

## Where this needs a lawyer, and why

1. **The Arizona hour, widened.** It already examines the Breach residual first. It should now also
   ask whether a legible mark, read through a legend the platform wrote, is provable as false under
   Yetman and Turner, because that decides whether the whole fingerprint is opinion or assertion.
2. **Whether a documented render defect is reckless disregard.** Godbehere and Hart put knowledge in
   the fault element. Whether an operator's own dated log of a mechanism counts, for marks published
   after it, turns on dates, and a lawyer should answer it before the public level ships.
3. **The licence behind a hardened comment when no terms exist.** Asset Marketing Systems v.
   Gagnon (9th Cir. 2008) is the lead on whether an implied licence can be revoked. Unread.
4. **Which GDPR lawful basis would carry the public display**, and so whether Article 21 or consent
   withdrawal is the route out. The trigger is the first EU-resident member. Articles 6 and 7 are
   not read in this tree.
5. **Whether a media liability policy covers an image the insured's own code drew.** The broker
   question in `positions/2026-09-20-tier-label-first-party-speech.md` asks about statements a
   model wrote. It should now ask about the drawing too.

## Rebuttal

**`designer`'s rim fix is the strongest point against my gate, and meets half of it.** I reran
`rim_floor_fix.py`: tying the floor to turbulence orders it by heat for all seven and cuts
Anselm's from 1.31px to 0.25px. That fixes the amount and worsens the timing. Dolores Vance's heat
is all early. The ratio cap calms her core, one of two accidents that let tonight's readers place
it early, and the rim fix lifts her heat clear of its rim floor, 1.80 to 1, where the legend puts
recent heat. By the legend, a reformed person now runs hot lately: true heat, false implication,
Godbehere's pattern. The gate stays, with a cheap exit: stop teaching when heat happened until the
renderer reads dated events, rerun the single-mark task against the one-in-five bar, and print
`philosopher`'s "Heat, none" beside a calm mark.

**Conceded to `security`.** My section 5 called the ledger append-only and hardening built. The
ledger cascades on delete, and hardening is one check in the delete handler
(`_recovered/api/comment/[id].js` lines 289-343), keyed to a `member_uuid` from the request body
that `convener-05` shows anyone can harvest. The line I offered Dan, "No one can edit the record
behind it, including us," would be false today: inside the hour a stranger can delete a member's
comment and redraw their mark. Hardening comes off my accepted risks until the database refuses
the delete and takes the author from a verified session.

**`architect`'s append-only record is how the database would hold it.** A tombstoned comment
leaves nothing on the public mark: a trace outliving its comment is an assertion without its
proof. Strangers see the current `FINGERPRINT_VERSION`; the frozen SVG stays with its owner, since
in public it keeps whatever the renderer got wrong that day.
