# Moral Emotions and Moral Behavior

**Source:** Tangney, J. P., Stuewig, J., and Mashek, D. J. (2007). "Moral Emotions and Moral Behavior." Annual Review of Psychology, 58, 345-372. DOI 10.1146/annurev.psych.56.091103.070145 https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev.psych.56.091103.070145 (full text read from the copy at https://www.its.caltech.edu/~squartz/Tangney.pdf)

## Summary

The review organises the self-conscious emotions around one distinction, first proposed by Helen Block Lewis (1971): shame is a negative evaluation of the global self, guilt a negative evaluation of a specific behaviour (p. 349). The difference in object produces different emotions and different conduct afterwards.

- **Hiding against amending** (p. 350). Shame corresponds with attempts to deny, hide or escape; guilt with confession, apology and undoing the consequences. The review's summary: guilt promotes constructive, proactive pursuits, shame promotes defensiveness, interpersonal separation and distance.
- **The attribution decides it** (p. 350, reporting Tracy and Robins 2006). Internal, stable, uncontrollable attributions for failure were related to shame; internal, unstable, controllable ones to guilt.
- **Exposure** (p. 349). Shamed people feel exposed, often with imagery of how the defective self would appear to others, even with no audience present.
- **Anger** (pp. 351-352). Shame-proneness correlates with anger, hostility and blaming factors outside the self. Across four samples, externalisation of blame mediated the link between shame-proneness and verbal and physical aggression. Guilt-proneness ran the other way.

## Limits

Most of the evidence concerns shame-proneness as a disposition, measured with scenario instruments such as the TOSCA, and that measurement tradition has published critics (for one, PMC4114200 on TOSCA-based shame research). The state-level evidence is thinner. Nothing here tested a visual representation of a person's record. What transfers to Dialecta is the appraisal mechanism, self against act and stable against unstable, and no effect size.
