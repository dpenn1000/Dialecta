# The Loss of Loss Aversion: Will It Loom Larger Than Its Gain?

**Source:** Gal, D., and Rucker, D. D. (2018). "The Loss of Loss Aversion: Will It Loom Larger Than Its Gain?" Journal of Consumer Psychology, 28(3), 497-516. DOI 10.1002/jcpy.1047 https://doi.org/10.1002/jcpy.1047

## Summary

A review of the evidence for loss aversion, the principle that losses loom larger than equivalent gains, concluding that current evidence does not support it as a general principle. Loss aversion is central to prospect theory (Kahneman and Tversky 1979), and Kahneman has called it psychology's most significant contribution to behavioral economics. Gal and Rucker polled roughly 80 researchers at the 2016 Society for Judgment and Decision Making conference; all but three endorsed the claim that losses loom larger, at a session advertised as challenging it. Their review finds the standard evidence, including the endowment effect and status quo bias, admits alternative explanations that do not require loss aversion: inertia when a person is indifferent between options, and the different reference prices buyers and sellers attend to (Weaver and Frederick 2012; Isoni 2011). They argue the endowment effect is a case of status quo bias and inherits its confounds, and that vividly felt losses reflect attachment to a particular object rather than a general asymmetry around a neutral reference point. They call instead for a contextual account, citing Walasek and Stewart (2015), who show that the range of losses and gains a person has been sampling determines whether losses, gains, or neither loom larger. Their conclusion is that all three patterns occur and which one holds is an empirical question about context.

## Implies for Dialecta

- The charter names "loss aversion around tier" as a bias the classification card triggers. This source says that cannot be assumed. Of the four candidates the charter lists, this is the weakest, and it should be carried as contextual rather than settled.
- Walasek and Stewart's range effect gives a concrete prediction for the seven tier ladder: how a drop from Forum to Spark lands depends on the range of tiers a contributor has been placed in recently, not on the size of the drop. A contributor who has only ever seen Forum reads an Echo differently from one who has seen Forum, Spark and Echo.
- The mechanism that does have support is attachment, which attaches to a specific owned thing rather than to a reference point. That points at the contributor profile and the Thinking Fingerprint, which accumulate, rather than at any single comment's tier, which does not.
- Practical consequence for the reclassification flow and the profile in `apps/web`: do not build loss-softening affordances for tier downgrades on the assumption that they are felt as losses. Measure whether they are first.
