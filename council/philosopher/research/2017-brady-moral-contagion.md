# Emotion shapes the diffusion of moralized content in social networks

**Source:** Brady, W. J., Wills, J. A., Jost, J. T., Tucker, J. A., and Van Bavel, J. J. (2017). "Emotion shapes the diffusion of moralized content in social networks." Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 114(28), 7313-7318. DOI 10.1073/pnas.1618923114 https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1618923114

## Summary

An analysis of 563,312 social media messages about three polarizing moral and political issues. Each additional moral-emotional word in a message raised its diffusion by about 20 percent, an effect the authors name moral contagion. The effect was bounded by group membership: moral-emotional language increased spread more strongly within liberal networks and within conservative networks than it did between them. The authors present this as evidence that emotional expression is central to how moral and political ideas travel, and as a mechanism feeding group polarization.
