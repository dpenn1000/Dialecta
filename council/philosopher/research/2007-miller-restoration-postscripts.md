# Psychological Reactance and Promotional Health Messages: The Effects of Controlling Language, Lexical Concreteness, and the Restoration of Freedom

**Source:** Miller, C. H., Lane, L. T., Deatrick, L. M., Young, A. M., and Potts, K. A. (2007). "Psychological Reactance and Promotional Health Messages: The Effects of Controlling Language, Lexical Concreteness, and the Restoration of Freedom." Human Communication Research, 33(2), 219-240. DOI 10.1111/j.1468-2958.2007.00297.x https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-2958.2007.00297.x

## Summary

An experimental study manipulating two properties of a persuasive health message: how controlling its language is, and how concrete rather than abstract its wording is. It also tested restoration postscripts, a closing line that hands the choice explicitly back to the reader. Reactance theory had proposed restoration since Brehm (1966), but the authors note it had received little or no empirical scrutiny before this study. Controlling language produced a set of negative outcomes, including message rejection and derogation of the source. Restoration postscripts produced more positive outcomes. Separately, concrete language beat abstract language on three measures at once: it drew more attention, was judged more important, and produced more positive assessments of the source.

## Implies for Dialecta

- Dialecta's rule that every commenter message below Breach ends with the door open (Founding Philosophy Article 4, Editorial Voice v1.2) is a restoration postscript. This is the study behind it. The rule is not only an ethical preference, it is the tested intervention.
- The concreteness result supports the Editorial Voice rule requiring one concrete suggestion, and raises what is at stake in it. Concreteness bought attention, perceived importance, and source credibility together. A vague commenter message costs trust in the classifier itself, not just persuasion on that one comment.
- Two surfaces to check against this: the system prompt in `api/classify.js`, and the Stage 1 nudge bar in the Discourse Layer, which currently reads as a bare instruction and carries no restoration clause.
- Limit worth naming before leaning on it: the postscript was tested once, in a health message, on young adults, as a single exposure. Dialecta would apply one on every comment, repeatedly, to the same person. Nothing here shows a restoration postscript survives repetition, and a door held open a hundred times may stop reading as a door.
