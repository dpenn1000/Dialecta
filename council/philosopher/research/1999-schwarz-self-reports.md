# How the questions shape the answers

**Source:** Schwarz, N. (1999). "Self-reports: How the questions shape the answers." American Psychologist, 54(2), 93-105. DOI 10.1037/0003-066X.54.2.93 https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.54.2.93 (abstract and secondary summaries read; the two copies of the full text found online are image scans that would not extract, so no figure from the body is cited)

## Summary

A review of the survey-methodology program. Self-reports of behaviors and attitudes are strongly influenced by features of the instrument, including question wording, format and context, through cognitive and communicative processes that are systematic and increasingly well understood. The central mechanism: respondents treat the question and its response alternatives as a communication from the asker, and use them to infer what is meant and what answers are expected. The response scale is information, not only a container for the answer.

## Limits

Survey respondents answering a researcher, not readers placing themselves on a map an engine drew. Abstract-level claim only; the review's specific experiments are not cited from this filing.
