# How long people stay on a page

**Source:** Weinreich, H., Obendorf, H., Herder, E., and Mayer, M. (2008). "Not quite the average: An empirical study of Web use." ACM Transactions on the Web, 2(1), Article 5. DOI 10.1145/1326561.1326566 (full text read, section 4.4, from the authors' copy at vsis-www.informatik.uni-hamburg.de)

## Summary

A naturalistic client-side log of 25 participants over two to four months. On nearly 60,000 first visits to a page, nearly half lasted under 12 seconds (median 12.4 seconds). Pages visited that briefly averaged 430 words, which the authors note no one can read that quickly; participants "regularly just seemed to glimpse over most of the information offered" before navigating on. Stay time rose with page length, but less than expected.

## Limits

Desktop browsing in the mid 2000s, general web pages, 25 people. A Declare overlay opened on purpose after an eight-minute essay is a more committed visit than the median page view. The widely quoted "users read at most 28 percent of the words" is Jakob Nielsen's derivation from this data set, not a figure in the paper, and is not cited here.

## Implies for Dialecta

- **Order decides what is read.** Scanning is the default even on pages people chose to open, so the first field of the Declare overlay is the one a scanning reader takes away. Today that field is the Core Claim, restating the argument the reader just finished. The field with a debiasing literature behind it, the Strongest Objection (`1984-lord-considering-the-opposite.md`), comes third.
