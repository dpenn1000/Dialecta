# Considering the opposite

**Source:** Lord, C. G., Lepper, M. R., and Preston, E. (1984). "Considering the opposite: A corrective strategy for social judgment." Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 47(6), 1231-1243. DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.47.6.1231 PMID 6527215 (abstract read verbatim through Europe PMC)

## Summary

Several biases in social judgment come from failing to consider possibilities at odds with current belief. Two parallel experiments, one on biased assimilation of new evidence on social issues and one on biased hypothesis testing of personality impressions. Subjects were induced to consider the opposite either by explicit instruction or by stimulus materials that made opposite possibilities more salient. In both, the consider-the-opposite induction corrected more bias than the more demand-laden instruction to be as fair and unbiased as possible.

## Limits

Undergraduates, laboratory tasks, 1984. The corrective was measured on judgments made right after the induction, not on later behavior.
