# Algorithm aversion

**Source:** Dietvorst, B. J., Simmons, J. P., and Massey, C. (2015). "Algorithm aversion: People erroneously avoid algorithms after seeing them err." Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 144(1), 114-126. DOI 10.1037/xge0000033 PMID 25401381 (abstract read through Europe PMC and OpenAlex)

## Summary

Five studies. Participants saw an algorithm forecast, a human forecast, both, or neither, then chose whose future forecasts to tie their incentives to. People are especially averse to algorithmic forecasters after seeing them perform, even when they see them outperform a human forecaster, because they lose confidence in an algorithm faster than in a human after watching the same mistake.

## Limits

Forecasting tasks with money on the choice. A Dialecta reader is not choosing between the engine and a human reviewer; the transfer is to trust in the engine's reading, and it is a transfer.
