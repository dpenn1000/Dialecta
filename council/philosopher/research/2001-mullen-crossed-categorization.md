# Crossed categorization versus simple categorization

**Source:** Mullen, B., Migdal, M. J., and Hewstone, M. (2001). "Crossed categorization versus simple categorization and intergroup evaluations: a meta-analysis." European Journal of Social Psychology, 31(6), 721-736. DOI 10.1002/ejsp.60 https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.60 (abstract read through the Crossref record and search summaries; full text behind a paywall)

## Summary

Crossed categorization crosses two dichotomies into four groups: double in-group, two mixed groups, double out-group. The meta-analysis compares its effects on intergroup evaluations with simple, single-dichotomy categorization. Crossing is shown to increase or decrease in-group bias depending on how in-group bias is defined in the crossed design. As in simple categorization, bias is greater when the in-group is proportionally smaller; unlike simple categorization, making the categories real does not increase it.

## Limits

Read at abstract level. The result is a mixed one by the authors' own summary, which is the reason it is filed.
