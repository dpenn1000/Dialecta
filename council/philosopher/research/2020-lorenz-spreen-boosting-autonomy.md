# How behavioural sciences can promote truth, autonomy and democratic discourse online

**Source:** Lorenz-Spreen, P., Lewandowsky, S., Sunstein, C. R., and Hertwig, R. (2020). "How behavioural sciences can promote truth, autonomy and democratic discourse online." Nature Human Behaviour, 4(11), 1102-1109. DOI 10.1038/s41562-020-0889-7 https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-020-0889-7

## Summary

Argues that the online environment was built to capture attention rather than to support deliberate thought and autonomous choice, and that information overload, fine grained personalization and distorted social cues together open the way to manipulation and to false information. The authors identify cues that are technically available but largely unused: signals of the epistemic quality of content, signals of what drove an algorithmic decision, and signals of how much consensus a debate actually holds. They then map two families of behavioral intervention onto those cues. Nudging changes the choice environment so that behavior shifts. Boosting builds a competence the person keeps and can apply elsewhere, which is what makes it the autonomy preserving option of the two.

## Implies for Dialecta

- The nudge and boost distinction is the sharpest tool this council has for testing a Dialecta feature against Article 2 of the Founding Philosophy, which says the AI must reflect, describe and invite rather than gatekeep, judge or manipulate. A nudge steers the next comment. A boost leaves the contributor better at writing comments anywhere, including off Dialecta. Article 2 is a commitment to boosting, stated in other words.
- Held to that line, Dialecta's surfaces split cleanly. The Stage 1 nudge bar is a nudge. The commenter message on the card is a nudge. The structured analysis grid, which separates specificity, emotional register, article engagement and opposing view into distinct readings, is a boost, because it teaches a distinction the contributor can carry away. The seven tier descriptions in the nomination picker are a boost, and the Discourse Layer UX says as much when it calls them the platform's educational mechanism.
- That yields a design rule with a source behind it: where a surface could be built either way, build the boost. It also gives the council a test to apply to the Growth Layer, which is the part of the platform most exposed to the charge of manipulating the person it claims to serve.
- Bears on P0-D2 and the sign-up question. The third cue the authors name, the degree of consensus in a debate, is what the topology bar already shows. Showing it to a reader who has no account yet is a boost offered before anything is asked for, which is the autonomy preserving way to open the door.
