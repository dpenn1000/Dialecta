# The ambivalence-indifference problem

**Source:** Kaplan, K. J. (1972). "On the ambivalence-indifference problem in attitude theory and measurement: A suggested modification of the semantic differential technique." Psychological Bulletin, 77(5), 361-372. DOI 10.1037/h0032590 https://doi.org/10.1037/h0032590 (abstract read through OpenAlex, 818 citations there; full text not read)

## Summary

Attitude had been measured as one bipolar dimension, favorable against unfavorable. Kaplan examines what the neutral point of such a scale means and argues it confounds two different states: indifference (little feeling either way) and ambivalence (strong feeling both ways). He proposes measuring the positive and negative components separately, on two unipolar scales, and derives from them total affect, ambivalence and polarization as distinct variables. The later evaluative-space literature builds on the same separation.

## Limits

About evaluative attitudes toward one object, not positions between two named stances. The transfer to an opinion map rests on the structure, which is the same: one line whose ends are assumed to trade off.
