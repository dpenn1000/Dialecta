# The minimal group paradigm: categorization into two versus three groups

**Source:** Hartstone, M., and Augoustinos, M. (1995). "The minimal group paradigm: Categorization into two versus three groups." European Journal of Social Psychology, 25(2), 179-193. DOI 10.1002/ejsp.2420250205 https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.2420250205 (abstract read verbatim through the Crossref record; full text behind a paywall. Corroborated by Pechar, E., and Kranton, R. (2017), "Moderators of Intergroup Discrimination in the Minimal Group Paradigm: A Meta-Analysis," Duke working paper, section 9, which reads it the same way.)

## Summary

The minimal group paradigm (Tajfel, Billig, Bundy and Flament 1971) sorts strangers by a trivial criterion and measures how they allocate rewards between their own group and the other. Almost every such experiment used two groups, or two dichotomies crossed. This one ran the procedure with three distinct groups against a two-group baseline and found that "with a three-group structure there is no significant ingroup bias." The authors propose that a two-way split lets subjects access a dichotomous categorization, which "primes a competitive orientation", and that three groups make the us-and-them contrast less salient.

## Limits

The authors call the study "only preliminary research." Minimal groups are assigned and carry no content; real intergroup discrimination toward several outgroups at once is well documented, as the abstract itself says. One study, abstract-level reading. See `2000-spielman-dichotomous-categorization.md` for the replication.

## Implies for Dialecta

- **The binary map.** A binary labels two ends of one line and asks a reader to stand relative to them. That is the two-way split this study found sufficient for in-group favoritism, where a three-way split was not. The Project Brief's case for the ternary ("No sides") gets an evidentiary leg here, and the binary loses its claim to be a harmless last resort.
- **Size unknown.** A map position is chosen and about something real, so the direction should transfer and the size is not known. A placement study comparing warmth toward the far side after binary and ternary placement on the same question would measure it.
