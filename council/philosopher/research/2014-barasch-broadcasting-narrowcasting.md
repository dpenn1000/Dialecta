# Broadcasting and Narrowcasting: How Audience Size Affects What People Share

**Source:** Barasch, A., and Berger, J. (2014). "Broadcasting and Narrowcasting: How Audience Size Affects What People Share." Journal of Marketing Research, 51(3), 286-299. DOI 10.1509/jmr.13.0238 https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.13.0238

## Summary

Six studies on how the size of the audience changes what a person chooses to communicate. Broadcasting, meaning communicating with several people at once, led people to avoid sharing content that made them look bad. Narrowcasting, meaning communicating with a single person, led them to share content useful to that recipient. The authors attribute the difference to where attention is focused: people default to self focus, and addressing one person raises other focus, which lowers self presenting content and raises useful content. The effect is about audience size rather than about identity or anonymity.

## Implies for Dialecta

- The charter names "the audience effect" as a bias the classification card triggers. On this evidence it does not, and the effect belongs somewhere else. The card is a narrowcast: the engine addresses one person, privately, before anything is published. That is the condition this study associates with less self presentation, not more.
- Where the audience effect does bite is the Contrast Strip in the Discourse Layer UX, which publishes the mismatch between declared tier and engine tier on every comment that has one, permanently. That turns self declaration from a private judgment into a broadcast act, which is precisely the condition under which people work to avoid looking bad.
- The predicted failure is specific and it damages a locked number. Self declaration carries 15 percent of classification weight. A contributor who expects a permanent public mismatch has an easy way to avoid one, which is to declare low. Systematic under declaration would make that 15 percent measure fear of the strip rather than calibration, and Calibration is one of the six pillars in the Contributor Identity spec.
- The Discourse Layer UX calls the strip "not a warning" but "a statement of fact about how the system works." On this evidence that description is incomplete. The strip is factual, and it is also an audience, and an audience changes the behavior it records.
- Cheapest test before any redesign: compare the distribution of self declarations from contributors who have previously received a contrast strip against those who have not.
