# Reviewer bias in single- versus double-blind peer review

**Source:** Tomkins, A., Zhang, M., and Heavlin, W. D. (2017). "Reviewer bias in single- versus double-blind peer review." Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 114(48), 12708-12713. DOI 10.1073/pnas.1707323114 https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1707323114 (abstract read through Europe PMC; preprint arXiv:1702.00502)

## Summary

A controlled experiment at WSDM 2017, a conference with a 15.6 percent acceptance rate. Every full-length submission went to four committee members: two drawn from a pool that could see author names and affiliations, two from a disjoint pool that could not. The asymmetry held through bidding, reviewing and scoring. Single-blind reviewers were significantly more likely than their double-blind counterparts to recommend acceptance for papers from famous authors, top universities and top companies, at estimated odds multipliers of 1.63, 1.58 and 2.10.

The preprint reports different figures (1.76 and 1.67 for famous authors and top institutions, and bidding on 26 percent more papers where the published version says 22 percent fewer). Cite the published numbers only.

## Limits

The judges were experts assessing full papers at a selective venue, and the standing they could see was earned outside it. Dialecta's voters are community members judging comments, and the standing a fingerprint shows is earned inside the platform, partly from earlier votes. Experts are the population least expected to be swayed, so the direction should transfer; the size is not known here.

## Implies for Dialecta

- **The byline mark.** Community voting is 35 percent of a comment's classification (root `CLAUDE.md`). The comment card already carries the author's archetype tag in its header and the vote controls in its footer (`Dialecta_Discourse_Layer_UX.md`). A legible fingerprint beside the vote makes it a single-blind review of the author, and the vote then feeds the tier that grows the mark. That is the loop by which a description becomes a scoreboard.
- **The unit.** The vote is part of a comment's classification. Tier Psychology's first principle, describe the comment and not the person, says the author's record is the wrong unit for it, whatever that record predicts.
- **What survives.** Recognition needs identity and does not need rank. A mark shown at one size for everyone, carrying a nominal channel such as subject hue, lets a reader know who is speaking without knowing who outranks whom.
- **Bears on P-6** (`2017-brady-moral-contagion`, `2022-wojcik-birdwatch-bridging`): a bridging aggregator corrects for which faction voted. It does nothing about a voter who judged the author before the comment.
