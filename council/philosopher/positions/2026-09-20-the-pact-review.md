# Fourteen promises, three clauses, and a job the count doesn't capture

*philosopher review, 2026-09-20. Answers `council/log/2026-09-20-the-pact-review.md`, question
one, folding in the convener's mid-review note on the Pact's third job.*

## Brief

Dan scoped the Pact to three jobs: buy-in, legal concerns, and making sure this is the right
place for someone. The third job reframes question one. Counted, the Pact holds roughly fourteen
platform commitments against three first-person clauses from the signer. That imbalance matters
less than the self-selection test: a document meant to let someone say no must disclose what they
would be walking into. This one doesn't. The fingerprint and the Breach residual, exactly what a
considered no would weigh, appear nowhere. "I welcome the mirror" is consent to a category with no
preview. Section IV claims contestability it does not have. Brevity is not the obstacle. The fix
is a link, the form Dan asked for.

## Position

**1. The count, against buy-in.** Roughly fourteen falsifiable platform lines (never hidden,
patterns tracked "not as a score, not as a judgment") against three signer clauses, one a
disposition, not an action. The mutual-agreement claim survives that, a vow can be short. What the
count actually shows: heavy platform self-description, one conduct promise back, no stated cost if
the platform's side breaks.

**2. The mirror, welcomed sight unseen.** Broad consent to a description that does not exist yet
can be legitimate, but only with safeguards for the specificity the signer lacks. ADR-004's "first
render" moment is that safeguard, and it is not in the Pact. Until it is, "I welcome the mirror" is
ceremony, not an informed yes, for exactly the person the fingerprint might tell to leave.

**3. The Pact already overclaims, which now costs a fair no.** Section IV says "every decision is
contestable," written before any fingerprint existed. A comment's tier is contestable; the
fingerprint is not, yet. Someone deciding whether this is their place is owed that distinction
stated, not implied.

**4. Section VIII is the right seam, if it is the one read before signup.** The prototype's Path A
/ Path B chooser proves the interaction: a gated card choice unlocking the commit button, live in
`dialecta-pact.html`. The live snapshot's Section VIII shows no chooser, only a sign-in gate.
Confirm which one the visibility question joins before wording it.

**5. The omission is a function defect, not a completeness gap.** Section VI promises
pattern-tracking is private, "not as a score, not as a judgment." The fingerprint is a second,
public-by-default tracking of related history, marked for Breach, diluting rather than decaying. A
person who would opt out on sight of that cannot, since the Pact never says it exists. ADR-004's
rationale, proportionate, no shame, everyone gets a bad day, belongs in front of them, not buried
in a decision doc.

**6. Brevity helps the third job; silence does not.** A shorter Pact gets read in full; one
abandoned at word 900 selects on stamina, not fit, exactly Dan's worry. But neither gap above was
cut for length. The fingerprint postdates the text; the contest sentence predates the need to
scope it. Dan's fix covers both: a link to the mechanism, not a training module. Short and
complete are not in tension here. Only short and silent are.

## Recommendation

None of this needs a rewrite, the prose is good throughout. It needs a narrowed sentence in
Section IV, a linked disclosure that the fingerprint and Breach residual exist, and a confirmed
answer on which Section VIII a visibility choice joins. All three are a sentence or a link, Dan's
standard, not a bigger document.
