# The phase order is a sequence of decisions about a person

*`philosopher` position, 2026-09-20, path to launch. `docs/plans/launch.md` is under attack here,
not assumed. Angle: a phase plan is a sequence of decisions about what the platform does to
people, and every "done when" test in the convener's plan checks the system, never the person on
the other end of it.*

## Brief

A phase plan is a sequence of decisions about what the platform does to a person, not just to a
codebase, and this plan's six "done when" tests never check that. The clearest proof: Phase 2's
own done-when is a stranger seeing their fingerprint, which means the public Archetype ships
before Phase 5 ever invites anyone, and P-11 already established that the Archetype has no
contest mechanism at all, not even the weaker version community re-review gives a comment. Order
in this plan protects the codebase. It does not protect the first person the codebase gets wrong.

## Position

**On P-9, and phase order.** It matters, but not how the plan assumes. Heyman and Ariely tested a
price's presence and size, never timing: presence alone flips social norms to market norms
regardless of size. That it flips regardless of timing too is my extension, not theirs.
On that reading, deferring Charge to Phase 6 doesn't remove the switch, only decides who's
standing there when it lands. Charge early and the market frame predates any social norm to
overwrite. Charge last, and Phases 4 and 5 spend months building the unpaid behavior, generous
reading, honest calibration, community re-review, before Phase 6 sits a price beside it untouched:
a frame arriving after relationships already formed under a different one, not obviously the
smaller risk. The real fix is the wall P-9 names, payment status never reaching the classifier,
vote weight, or nomination panel, and it has no phase number. Give it one, in Phase 1,
before Phase 4 builds the community it protects.

**On P-11, whether it is a launch gate.** Yes, narrowly, not "block Phase 2." The Growth Layer's
Self-Snapshot already governs the self-facing view honestly: three voices, the contributor's own
words canonical, nothing offered as verdict. That ships on schedule. What can't is a public
Archetype visible to anyone but its subject: Phase 2 bundles it with the fingerprint on one
profile surface (`Dialecta_Contributor_Identity.md`: "above the fingerprint sits a second
representation, the archetype"), and A-7/A-8, the platform's only contest mechanism, resolves one
comment, not a history. Legal named the pattern: exposure grows "as the surface moves from
comment level toward person level," and the Archetype is that surface. The fix is a visibility
default, not new engineering: self-visible at Phase 2, visible to others only once an
aggregate-level contest path exists. Put that in Phase 2's own done-when, not left for its first
mischaracterized subject to discover.

**On zero published comments.** Circulation said the load-bearing part: 269 visitors met a
blog with a dead widget, not Dialecta. Worse than a conversion problem: the platform's core claim,
that the AI reflects rather than gatekeeps, is unfalsifiable by anyone who has never watched a
card, a badge, a strip operate. Trusting that on Dan's word alone is the exact deference the
founding essay indicts elsewhere. Publish the three pending comments, or decide on the record why
not, before Phase 5 sends strangers to look at nothing.

**Opening the growth question now, not at Phase 5.** Circulation's own open record asks legal and
designer what a share card may carry. It should have asked me too: a share card is the
largest-audience broadcast this platform will ever produce, off-platform, permanent, to people who
never saw the Pact. P-4's mechanism, self-presentation rising with audience size, is maximized
exactly there. No tier badge, no fingerprint-derived descriptor, on any share card, not pending
legal's answer but because the psychology needs no permission. The standing test for every
later circulation tactic: would it read, in the founding essay's own terms, as the thing it
indicts.

## Rebuttal

Security's correction lands. "A visibility default, not new engineering" was wrong: no live
session exists to key a predicate to, so self-visible-only isn't a flag, it's a migration against
auth that doesn't exist yet. I called it cheap. It isn't.

**Resized, not defeated.** The migration needs the same session-verified auth `builder` is already
building as shared foundation. The fix rides there, named inside it, not a separate Phase-2 gate.
Security made the identical move on the comment hole: retracted "ahead of phase one" for a fix
riding inside the feature itself, the same task, not an addition. The Archetype policy takes that
seat too.

**Sharper, not softer.** I wrote "self-visible" as if a default already held. Security measured it
doesn't: `USING (true)`, full anon SELECT, no column narrowing. Any Archetype row that exists is
already readable by anyone holding the anon key, the key every browser tab loads. That's
`ghost_member_id`'s own sentence again: live now, not at launch. Six real people, three arm's
length: a present condition, not a future risk.

**Cuts both ways, and I take the cut.** My own objection to trusting classification off one thread
applies harder to the artifact itself: an Archetype built on a handful of comments is a worse
characterization than one built on hundreds, before anyone asks who can see it. Thin data and open
visibility compound into one problem.

**The other three, one line each.** Payment wall, Phase 1: still pressing, unanswered, cheap, a
rule about what not to build. Phase 6 order: still pressing; treasurer priced the decision, never
the risk of charging last after two unpaid-norm phases. Discourse volume: letting it go as a gate
since no seat can name the number, kept as a standing constraint.
