# A visibility setting controls the audience, not the verdict

*philosopher position, 2026-09-20. Answers ADR-004's extension of the fingerprint visibility
decision to the Archetype-gating disagreement with legal, at Dan's direct request.*

## Brief

Dan's decision answers audience, not truth. My objection was that an uncontestable
characterization is a thing done to a person; a visibility setting decides who watches it happen,
not whether it happened or can be corrected. Default-effect research says a buried toggle will not
be used by most contributors, so public-by-default stays public for nearly everyone it exists to
protect, and opting out becomes its own tell. The contest path I argued for is not replaced by
this. It is more necessary now, sitting behind a control most people will never touch.

## Position

**1. Two problems, one touched.** My objection had two parts: the platform durably characterizes
someone from categories it chose, and the person has no way to say it is wrong. Visibility is
control over who looks. It leaves whether the claim is true or correctable untouched, and solves
the first only for contributors who act.

**2. A buried default is not a choice.** Johnson and Goldstein's organ-donation studies found
opt-out countries running participation well above 80%, opt-in countries well under 20%, same
populations, only the pre-selection differing. A toggle a contributor has to find will move about
as few. A real choice shows the actual rendered Archetype, offered the moment one first exists to
be seen rather than at signup, and says plainly that public means anyone with the anon key today,
and that nothing on the card can be contested. Short of that, it is notice, not consent.

**3. Yes, and it should be said plainly.** Because most people never touch a default, opting out
will be rare, which makes it informative: on a platform built around Breach residuals, going
private reads as having something to hide, precisely where that reading costs the most. The
control meant to let contributors manage their own shame becomes a second, more legible marker of
it. Not a reason to drop the control. A reason to expect it underperforms unless opting out gets
common for ordinary reasons, not just shame.

**4. Still live, mostly more urgent.** A contributor who finds the setting gets real, immediate
mitigation today, before any contest path exists, and I should credit that. But it does not touch
A-7/A-8 resolving one comment, not a history, and it does not reach the majority who never touch a
default. My earlier fix used platform-enforced invisibility as a stand-in for the missing contest
path; Dan's decision swaps that stand-in for an opt-in most will not exercise, so the group exposed
to an uncorrectable claim grows. Connections-only does not close the gap: the opinions that cost a
contributor most usually belong to connections, not strangers.

## Recommendation

Keep the visibility control; it is more humane than a platform gate and fits the ADR's own logic.
Ship it as a real choice: surface it against the actual rendered Archetype the first time one
exists, not a settings footnote, and state plainly that public means anyone with the anon key
today and nothing on the card can be contested yet. Expect the tell effect in point 3. None of this
replaces a contest path; build one, at aggregate grain, on the same timeline as the
session-verified auth already underway.
