# What early commitment can be asked to earn

*`philosopher` position, 2026-09-20, on Dan's two questions about founding-cohort terms.*

## Brief

Both are acceptable with conditions, and my shame objection is soft where the convener aimed it. A
badge nobody held is not a badge taken away, and the first 25 authors already get lifetime comp, a
larger earned distinction than a ring. What decides the badge is whether the condition is announced
while the person writes. Deci, Koestner and Ryan (1999), 128 studies: expected completion-contingent
rewards undermine free-choice motivation at d = -0.36, unexpected ones do not, and positive feedback
enhances it at d = +0.33. Same badge, opposite signs. On the ladder, P-9 raises no objection, because
the switch to market norms happened at the first dollar.

## 1. What the shame argument was built for

Tier Psychology's Breach section argues against shame for a label applied to a person's thinking
against their will, and its working sentence is "you broke an agreement we both signed," which reads
a mind without being asked. A mark for having written articles is voluntary, countable, and about
output rather than character. Importing the Breach argument here borrows weight it did not earn.
Charter Writers already hands the first 25 published authors lifetime comp, and nobody is shaming the
26th. The convener's third objection is the weakest of them.

## 2. Where the objection does hold

Not on shame, on sequence. Migration `032_profiles_is_charter.sql` says the flag is "Locked at
upgrade time, never revoked." A condition on a badge already granted is a revocation, and a mark you
never had cannot be lost. The distinction is temporal, not psychological, and it is the crux.

Second, the denominator. P-4 puts the audience effect on broadcast surfaces, and Barasch and Berger
(2014) found broadcasting is the condition under which people work to avoid looking bad. An ordinary
missing badge is invisible. One missing from a publicly enumerated cohort of a hundred is legible,
because a reader can subtract.

## 3. What a year of counting does

Anderson, Huttenlocher, Kleinberg and Leskovec (2013), several million Stack Overflow users, badges
with visible thresholds: activity on the targeted action rises sharply before the boundary and
"almost immediately returns to near-baseline levels" after. For the Electorate badge, answer-votes
*fell* as the line approached. Effort was steered off one contribution onto the countable one.

A member pays in October, writes one strong piece in March because she had something to say, and in
September faces two of three with three weeks left. She writes the third, the worst of the three, and
the commenting she skipped that month paid for it. Article 8 predicts this: whatever a system
rewards, people adapt toward. A count rewards a count.

## 4. The moderator that decides the badge

Deci et al. found unexpected and task-noncontingent rewards did no damage, and said why:
participants "were not doing the task in order to get those rewards, so they did not feel controlled
by them." The convener's alternative, a marker for underwriters who have published, is that form,
and the difference between the two is evidence rather than politeness. One caveat the authors carry
and Dan inherits: an unexpected reward handed out routinely becomes expected.

## 5. The ladder against P-9

P-9 has no objection, and I will say so rather than stretch it. Heyman and Ariely (2004) is precise
that a price switches the frame and its size does not. A member who paid $50 already holds market
norms, and $50 to $100 is movement inside that frame. The harder version gets the same answer: a
locked discount does not convert a patron into a customer, because the conversion happened at the
first dollar. It hands a number to a frame already installed.

Honest urgency, then, and specific. The half the house test hides is that it also has to be true: a
published ladder is a promise to raise the price, and it binds Dan rather than the members.

## 6. The date and the milestone

Both. The date binds; the milestone names the reason. A slip costs the first hundred nothing, since
they are locked either way; it lands on the 101st, who pays $100 for a Growth Layer that did not
ship. So hold the price and publish the hold. A platform arguing for contestable
reasoning can say the date moved and why.

## Recommendation

**Acceptable, with conditions.**

On the badge:

1. Nothing conditioned after the money. Migration 032's "never revoked" stands. If Dan wants a
   marker for underwriters who also write, render it on `is_charter` plus a count of that member's
   rows in `articles` at `status = 'published'`, threshold one, granted after the writing and taking
   nothing from anyone. `is_author` is the wrong flag and the correction below says why.
2. No progress counter, anywhere in the product. No "2 of 3," no remaining count, no year-end
   reminder. Visible progress is what installs Anderson's gradient.
3. No announcement in advance. The recognition can exist in the spec and in Dan's head; the moment a
   member knows the threshold while writing, it becomes the expected reward that measured at -0.36.
   This is the one thing here I am asking to go unpublished, against the transparency principle, and
   it holds only because the mark carries no standing and no capability. Nobody is worse off for not
   knowing. If it ever gates anything, publish it and accept the cost.
4. Once, for the founding year, and never an annual practice.
5. Never publish the cohort's size or roster beside the mark, or the absence becomes arithmetic.
6. If Dan wants a real condition anyway, attach it to something new, named and priced at the moment
   of sale. Never to the thing already bought.

On the ladder:

1. Publish the date and the milestone together before the first $50 is taken, and treat the date as
   binding on Dan.
2. State on the same page what happens if the milestone slips: the price holds, and the hold is
   published with a new date.
3. Tell the first hundred plainly that the lock is worth exactly what the increase is worth, and that
   the increase is a commitment Dan is making, not a forecast.
4. Extend the P-9 wall to the lock. A locked founding price is a louder market signal than a price,
   and it reaches the classifier, the vote weight and the nomination panel on the same terms: not at
   all.
5. Price it, state it once, and let the patronage pitch carry the sale. A lock sold as the reason to
   join is the one version that earns the manufactured-scarcity charge.

## Rebuttal to legal

**The deadlock is false, because my version drops Dan's question.** ROSCA reaches material terms of
the transaction. In my version nothing about the transaction is conditioned: `is_charter` is granted
at checkout unconditionally, and a member who never writes gets everything they paid for. A rendering
conferred later takes nothing and promises nothing, so there is no term to disclose and no bargain
for Douglas to modify. Legal's screen and my silence never meet. They miss only because my version
rewards where Dan asked to require, which legal says too. If he wants the requirement, the seats are
incompatible and it should not be built.

**Conceded, to legal.** The grant-only structure in section 2 beats my sequence objection. Setting
the flag at condition-met time never creates the thing to be lost.

**Conceded, with a size.** Deci measured people doing a task for an experimenter, and I applied it to
people buying a membership. Two stretches. The undermining reaches the member who would have written
anyway, a subset rather than the cohort, since one who would never write has no motivation to
undermine. And -0.36 comes from a tangible-reward corpus where a badge is symbolic and should land
softer. What holds at measured range is the expected-against-unexpected moderator. Anderson carries
the magnitude, on badges, at scale.

**Legal is right on the milestone and I over-reached.** "$100 on 2027-10-01" is specific and true,
the whole house test. The milestone answered whether the $100 is earned, a fairness question rather
than an honesty one, and publishing it buys no honesty the date did not supply.

**The capability matrix strengthens the patronage framing.** A feature ladder hands a member a list
to total against what they paid, the market-norm behaviour P-9 predicts. Patronage leaves nothing to
total. Section 5 and P-9 want the same page.

## Correction to my own badge condition

My recommendation said to render the marker on `is_charter` plus `is_author`. That is wrong, and the
framing it came from is wrong with it. `is_author` records permission rather than work: migration 006
calls it the gate on `/api/article/submit`, meaning may write, and migration 010 flips it inside the
same update that writes `pact_agreed_at` and `pact_signed_name`, so signing the Pact sets it. Some
profiles carry it from manual seeding. The predicate I should have named is the one legal wrote
against, `articles.author_member_id` joined to profiles on `status = 'published'`.

**Set the threshold at one, and nowhere higher.** Anderson's acceleration needs a gap to close, and a
threshold of one has no gradient: a member is at zero or finished. Three articles is a quota. One
published article is a categorical act, and the distinction between them is the whole of my section
3. The 25 lifetime-comp authors sit in a separate cohort awarded for being first rather than for
volume, so they add no second denominator inside the hundred.

**The error is evidence for the position.** A mark rendered on a flag that trips at Pact signature
would have appeared on every participating underwriter, a distinction that distinguishes nobody. That
is the mirror of section 2. There, a missing mark becomes legible because the denominator is small;
here, the mark becomes meaningless because the denominator is everyone. Same mistake either way, a
mark whose meaning nobody checked against the population carrying it.
