# Opinion map ideas

## Ink on commit

The reader's mark flips from a hollow tag to a filled one the instant it's committed. Paper takes ink slower than that. Hold the outline a beat, then spread the fill from the center out over roughly 200 milliseconds, no bounce, no overshoot. The overlay already calls this a Declaration on paper. A mark that visibly settles reads as a considered act. An instant color swap reads as an accident.

## The pull mid-drag

The corner washes are static, set once from the map's stored data. While a reader is still dragging, the pole nearest the live cursor could brighten, its label a touch heavier, its wash a touch stronger, while the far poles recede. The three-way tension a ternary exists to hold would then be something a reader feels while the thumb is still moving.

## Contour lines

The dashed grid inside the triangle cuts it into four sub-triangles that carry no meaning: nothing names them, nothing checks them. Concentric rings out from the centroid would carry a real one instead: near the middle reads as torn, near an edge reads as certain. That's an axis, conviction, the map never currently draws.

## Corner pulls

A square with a crosshair through the middle is a political compass, and most readers already know how to play one: pick a quadrant, defend it. Drop the center lines. Keep only the four corner pulls, the same radial washes the ternary already uses, and let the answer be how far each way.

## A smudge for torn

Both marks are a single point, and a reader split between two poles has to round that down to one pixel. That rounding might be exactly what stops some readers from placing at all. A second gesture, drag to stretch the mark into a short smudge toward a second pole instead of a dot, would let "mostly this, some of that" live on the map without a text box.

## The pill

The binary's track, a rounded bar with a mark sliding along it, is a good shape on its own terms. Once it's gone as a full map, it's still the right form for something smaller: a yes or no, a how sure are you. Worth keeping the shape even after the map it was built for is retired.
