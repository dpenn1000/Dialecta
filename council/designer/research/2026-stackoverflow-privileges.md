# Privileges

**Source:** Stack Overflow, "Privileges" help centre index, with the "create posts" and "comment everywhere" privilege pages. Read 19 September 2026. https://stackoverflow.com/help/privileges

## Summary

Reputation on Stack Overflow unlocks tools, not the right to speak. Both "create posts" and "comment everywhere" are awarded at 1 reputation, which every account holds on arrival. The site describes the first of these as the most basic privilege of all, the right to ask a question and the right to contribute an answer, and says it is "generally available to everyone, regardless of reputation level".

The whole ladder above that point governs power over other people's content and over the site itself: flag posts at 15, vote up at 15, vote down at 125, create chat rooms at 100, set bounties at 75, view close votes at 250, access review queues at 500, create tags at 1500, edit other people's posts without review at 2000, cast close and reopen votes at 3000, approve tag wiki edits at 5000, access moderator tools at 10000, protect questions at 15000, trusted user at 20000, site analytics at 25000.

The largest reputation system on the web spends none of its friction on the first contribution and all of it on moderation authority.

## Implies for Dialecta

- Backlog A-1: this is the direct counter-evidence to the intuition behind the 12 character gate. A system built entirely around earned standing still lets a brand new account post immediately.
- Contributor Identity: the Thinking Fingerprint should unlock tools rather than display status, which is how it avoids becoming the scoreboard the philosopher's charter guards against. Nomination weight, re-review access and Steward tooling are the Dialecta analogues of flag, review queue and close vote.
- Profile page (B-3): show what a contributor may now do, not only what they have scored. The privileges page is readable as a roadmap, which is most of why it motivates.
- Reclassification (A-7): nomination is power over someone else's comment. That is the surface where a threshold belongs, and it currently has none.
