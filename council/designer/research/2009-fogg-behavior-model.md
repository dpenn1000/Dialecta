# A behavior model for persuasive design

**Source:** B. J. Fogg, "A behavior model for persuasive design", Proceedings of the 4th International Conference on Persuasive Technology (Persuasive 2009), Claremont, California, ACM, pages 1 to 7. DOI 10.1145/1541948.1541999. Citation verified against Crossref 19 September 2026; the ACM full text is paywalled and was not read. Model content read from the Stanford Behavior Design Lab page at behaviordesign.stanford.edu. https://doi.org/10.1145/1541948.1541999

## Summary

Behaviour is the product of three factors that must converge at the same moment: motivation, ability, and a prompt. Fogg's lab states that when a behaviour does not occur, at least one of those three elements was missing. Ability is decomposed into simplicity factors, which are read in the context of the prompt rather than in the abstract. Motivation and ability are compensatory: very high motivation tolerates low ability, and very high simplicity tolerates low motivation. The working conclusion designers take from it is that raising simplicity is cheaper and more reliable than raising motivation.

On confidence: this is a widely adopted design heuristic with a thin empirical base, and the paper itself is a model statement rather than a study. Use it to name a mechanism, never to claim a measured effect.

## Implies for Dialecta

- Backlog A-1: names exactly what the composer must do. Dialecta cannot raise a first-time commenter's motivation by much, so it has to raise ability. The nudge bar raises ability, because it says what good looks like at the moment it is relevant.
- The disabled "Analyze my comment" button removes the prompt at the moment motivation is at its peak, which is the one combination the model says yields no behaviour. Whatever the character threshold is, the prompt should stay present.
- The classification card (A-3) is the second prompt in the flow, and Stage 2.5 is the third. Each is a place where ability, not motivation, is the variable worth spending on.
- Holds the charter line explicitly. This same model is the ancestor of the hook patterns the founding essay indicts. Dialecta takes the ability half and refuses the variable reward half, and saying so out loud is part of using it honestly.
