# Understanding Discourse Trust Levels

**Source:** Jeff Atwood, "Understanding Discourse Trust Levels", Discourse blog, 25 June 2018 (page updated December 2025). Read 19 September 2026. https://blog.discourse.org/2018/06/understanding-discourse-trust-levels/

## Summary

Every new Discourse account starts at trust level 0. Promotion to TL1 requires entering at least 5 topics, reading at least 30 posts, and spending a total of 10 minutes reading posts. Nothing in that ladder stops a TL0 account from posting on day one. What TL0 restricts is reach: one image per post, no attachments, at most 2 hyperlinks, at most 2 user mentions, at most 3 new topics, at most 10 replies, no personal messages, no flagging, and no editing after 24 hours. TL1 removes all of those restrictions at once and grants access to every core function. Movement from TL0 through TL3 is automatic, with no moderator in the loop. The reading requirement is therefore a condition of promotion, never a condition of speaking.

This is the closest living precedent for the Stewards ladder, and it spends its entire friction budget after the first post rather than before it.

## Implies for Dialecta

- Backlog A-1: Discourse, the system Dialecta's trust model most resembles, puts no gate in front of a first comment. The 12 character gate sits earlier in the flow than any gate Discourse uses.
- Stewards: promote on counts of reading and participation, resolved automatically. A human approval step is not needed and Discourse has run without one for over a decade.
- A-1 has no per-account rate limit. TL0's caps, 3 topics and 10 replies, are the shape to copy, and they answer the spam worry that a character minimum does not.
- The Pact: "read before you post" survives as a promotion criterion. It does not survive as a wall in front of the composer.
