# Deliberate Barriers to User Participation on MetaFilter

**Source:** Hannah Pileggi, Briana B. Morrison, and Amy Bruckman, "Deliberate Barriers to User Participation on MetaFilter", Georgia Institute of Technology Technical Report GT-IC-14-01, 2014. Abstract and repository record read 19 September 2026; full text not retrieved. https://digitalcommons.unomaha.edu/compscifacpub/63/

## Summary

A descriptive study of MetaFilter, in continuous operation since 1999 and carrying around 12,000 active users at the time of writing. The site runs two deliberate barriers: a $5 fee to join, and a mandatory one week waiting period before a new member is allowed to post. The paper's stated question is both why the barriers were imposed and why some users chose to surmount them, which is the half of the question a design argument usually skips. Data sources were historical documents posted on the site, interviews with eleven members, an informal user survey, and an interview with founder Matt Haughey. The fee dates to 18 November 2004, introduced after repeated closures of new member signup, to hold growth to what moderation could absorb.

Caveat on confidence: only the abstract and the repository record were read. The study is descriptive, single site, and has no control. Nothing in the findings should be quoted from this note until the full text is filed.

## Implies for Dialecta

- P0-D2 (open versus invite-only): the closest documented case of paid signup working. It bears on the decision, and it should be read in full before the council argues it.
- The barrier that held at MetaFilter is on identity and on time, not on the text. A member pays, waits a week, then writes at whatever length they like. This is the pattern in every precedent filed so far.
- The Pact: a read-first waiting period is a live alternative to a composer gate, and it is aimed at what Dialecta wants, which is a person who has seen what good looks like here.
- A-1: reinforces that a 12 character minimum is friction in the wrong place. It taxes the text and leaves identity unpriced.
- Reading list: promoted to a full-text lead, because the "why did people surmount it" finding is the part that would change a position.
