Membership

Underwriters keep Dialecta free to read

Every part of Dialecta that says who you are is free, including the parts still being built. The record of what you have argued, and the tools for reflecting on it, will never sit behind a payment.

Underwriting pays to keep the place running and to finish it. Two limits lift the day you join, and eight more capabilities are funded and not yet built, four of them free to everyone.

The price

$50 now, $100 after the first hundred

The founding rate belongs to the first hundred members rather than to a launch window. A Charter Underwriter keeps $50 for as long as the membership runs.

First hundred members $50 a year

Held at that number permanently. Ten years of membership costs $500.

Everyone after $100 a year

The same ten years costs $1,000.

No date is attached to the change. The rate moves when the hundredth membership is taken, which may be next month or next year.

Included and excluded

What a membership buys, and when

Ten capabilities sit in the tier matrix and two of them work today. The other eight wait on the Growth Layer engine, which is the Self-Snapshot Engine and the History Scroll, and neither is built.

Four of those eight are free to everyone the day they land. A membership pays for them to exist rather than buying access to them, which is what underwriting means.

Excluded by design

What a membership does not buy

Paying changes two limits in the editor and nothing about how your writing is read. Payment status never reaches the classifier, the vote weight or the nomination panel, and holding that wall up is part of what the money is for.

  • The classifierA comment's classification is read from the comment. Your subscription is not an input.
  • Vote weightAn Underwriter's vote counts once, the same as everyone else's.
  • The nomination panelSeats come from the record you have built. Paying does not put you on it.
  • ReachNo boost, no priority placement, no paid visibility of any kind.

Heyman and Ariely, 2004: a price of any size moves a relationship from social norms to market norms, and a small price does not escape it. Filed by philosopher, with the wall as the answer.

Roles

Founding cohorts

Three groups are seated at the founding. None of them is a rank, and a member can belong to more than one.

Charter Writers

First 25 published authors

Lifetime, free. Underwriter access for as long as the account exists, and a Charter ring that stays with it.

Charter Underwriters

First 100 paying members

$50 a year, held permanently. The Charter ring stays even if the membership stops.

Founding Voices

First 50 active commenters

One year, free. The year runs from the day the seat is granted, and the account returns to Free when it lapses with nothing lost.

A memberEveryone carries a seat ring from the day they join.

An UnderwriterThe dot fills when the seat carries Underwriter access.

HonoredA peer bought the year. The seal records the gift and never the price.

CharterThe ring turns to brass and thickens. Set once, never removed.

Peer gifting

A year for someone else

A member can buy a year for another member, and the record keeps both names. The gift stays recorded as a gift, because being given a year by a peer is a different fact from buying one.

A gifted year carries the Honored seal. It shows how the seat arrived and never what it cost. When the year ends the account returns to Free and every word written during it stays exactly where it was.

Gift a year

The recipient didn't pay themselves but is honored by a peer's gift.
