# The 90-9-1 Rule for Participation Inequality in Social Media and Online Communities

**Source:** Jakob Nielsen, "The 90-9-1 Rule for Participation Inequality in Social Media and Online Communities", Nielsen Norman Group, 8 October 2006. Read 19 September 2026. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/participation-inequality/

## Summary

In most online communities 90% of users lurk and never contribute, 9% contribute occasionally, and 1% account for almost all the activity. Nielsen traces the effect to Will Hill's work at Bell Communications Research in the early 1990s and reports it as a Zipf curve, straight on a log-log plot.

The number that matters most for a composer is from the Usenet study Nielsen cites: of more than 2 million messages, 27% of postings came from people who posted only a single message, while the most active 3% of posters contributed 25% of the messages. The ratios get worse in other formats. Blogs run closer to 95-5-0.1. Wikipedia runs at roughly 99.8-0.2-0.003, with about 1,000 people, 0.003% of users, producing two thirds of edits.

Nielsen's own concern is representational: a community read off its posters gives a biased picture of what the community thinks, because the silent majority never appears in the record.

## Implies for Dialecta

- Backlog A-1: this sizes the prize. If nine readers in ten never comment at all, the composer's whole job is converting the 9%, and any friction lands hardest on the person who was going to comment exactly once.
- The 27% single-post figure is the one to hold against the 12 character gate. In a healthy forum the most common contribution is one post, ever. A gate tuned to filter low-effort comments is tuned against the modal contributor.
- Topology bar and Quality sort (A-6): both are features for the 90% who read and never post. They are the largest audience the comment section has and the easiest to forget.
- Opinion map and the Phase 3 lightweight reactions (Social UX Architecture, Option 3): a one-tap position is the only contribution most readers will ever make. That is an argument for building it earlier than Phase 3.
- Dialecta has 14 members, so these ratios are not yet measurable here. First-comment completion has to be instrumented before any gate can be defended with numbers, which is this advisor's standing ask.
