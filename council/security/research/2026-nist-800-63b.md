# NIST SP 800-63B-4

**Source:** NIST, "Digital Identity Guidelines: Authentication and Authenticator Management", NIST Special Publication 800-63B-4, read 2026-09-20. https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/63/b/4/final

## Summary

SP 800-63B-4 is Final, published 07/31/25, and supersedes the prior SP 800-63B of 03/02/2020. It is one of three companion volumes in the SP 800-63-4 suite, alongside 63A-4 and 63C-4, and its title drops "Lifecycle Management" from the prior edition's name in favor of "Authenticator Management." The normative text, read via NIST's own HTML rendition of the same publication at pages.nist.gov, uses SHALL and SHALL NOT throughout.

On composition: "Verifiers and CSPs SHALL NOT impose other composition rules (e.g., requiring mixtures of different character types) for passwords." On rotation: "Verifiers and CSPs SHALL NOT require subscribers to change passwords periodically. However, verifiers SHALL force a change if there is evidence that the authenticator has been compromised." On length: "Verifiers and CSPs SHALL require passwords that are used as a single-factor authentication mechanism to be a minimum of 15 characters in length. Verifiers and CSPs MAY allow passwords that are only used as part of multi-factor authentication processes to be shorter but SHALL require them to be a minimum of eight characters in length," with a further "SHOULD permit a maximum password length of at least 64 characters."

The three Authenticator Assurance Levels are defined by confidence, not by a specific factor list: AAL1 "provides basic confidence that the claimant controls an authenticator that is bound to the subscriber account"; AAL2 "provides high confidence" of the same; AAL3 "provides very high confidence" of the same. This is the standard Supabase's own `aal1`/`aal2` JWT claim vocabulary is drawn from. Supabase implements AAL1 and AAL2; it has no AAL3 concept.

A companion supplement, SP 800-63Bsup1, surfaced during search but was not read this sprint. It reportedly addresses syncable authenticators (passkeys) specifically. It is named as a lead in the report below rather than summarized here, since nothing in it has been verified firsthand.

## Implies for Dialecta

- The 15-character password floor is the one to build to if P0-D2 includes a password option without mandatory MFA. Eight characters is permitted only when a second factor is also required on that account, matching the ASVS and cheat sheet notes filed this sprint.
- No composition regex, no forced rotation job. Both are explicit SHALL NOTs, not style advice.
- Supabase's `aal1`/`aal2` JWT claim (`2026-supabase-auth-mfa.md`) is this document's AAL1/AAL2 language, not a Supabase invention. An RLS policy gating on `aal2` implements this NIST requirement directly, worth citing alongside Supabase's own docs in an advice record.
- SP 800-63Bsup1's passkey guidance is unread. Add it to `reading-list.md` before Dialecta finalizes a passkey rollout, rather than relying on the Supabase passkey docs alone for the security properties.
