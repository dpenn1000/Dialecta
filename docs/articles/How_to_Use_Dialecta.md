# How to Use Dialecta

*A short guide for your first evening here, and the long road after*

By Daniel Pennington

---

Most platforms are built to be obvious in the first thirty seconds and exhausting forever after. Dialecta is built the other way around. It will reveal itself slowly, the way a city you have moved to does, until one evening you realize you know your way around without having tried to learn. This guide is a small head start. It is not exhaustive. It is the part of the city I want to show you first.

---

## Begin at the Pact

Everyone who joins Dialecta starts by reading the Pact. It is one page. It is the only thing on this site I would ask you not to skim. The Pact is the agreement we all make with each other about what kind of room this is. It is short on rules and long on intention, because the intention is the whole point. When you commit to the Pact, you are not promising to be civil or quiet or cautious. You are promising to engage with ideas instead of attacking people. Everything else flows from that one promise.

Read the Pact. Sit with it for a minute. Sign it when you mean it. The site is designed to feel different to you the moment you do, and it should.

---

## Then read an article

You do not have to comment. You do not have to react. You do not have to do anything yet. Read an article on a topic you actually care about and notice, while you are reading, that nothing is screaming at you. There is no flashing red banner. No popup begging for your email. No autoplay video chasing you down the page. The site is built to let you think while you read it. That alone is a strange feeling on the modern internet. Let yourself feel it.

While you read, notice the comments below the article. Each one carries a small badge. Those badges are the platform's tier system, and you will hear me describe them more than once because they are the heart of how this place stays this place.

---

## What the badges mean, simply

When somebody posts a comment, the system reads it before it goes public and quietly suggests which tier the comment fits into. There are seven tiers. Most of what you will read sits in three of them.

**The Forum** is the top tier. A Forum comment makes a specific claim, engages the article, and carries its weight honestly. Strong disagreement is welcome here. The Forum does not require you to be polite. It requires you to be about something.

**The Spark** is a comment that has the beginning of a real idea but stops short of finishing it. Sparks are not failures. Sparks are invitations. The platform notices when you almost said something larger, and gently asks if you would like to.

**The Echo, The Fog, The Heat, The Stance,** and **The Breach** are the tiers the system uses, in increasing order of seriousness, when a comment does not yet do what a Forum comment does. None of these tiers are punishments. All of them are observations. All of them, except the last, leave the door open for you to revise and try again, or to post anyway.

You do not need to memorize any of this. You will absorb it the same way you absorb the social rules of any place worth being in. By being there.

---

## When you are ready to comment

When you write a comment, the system will quietly read it before it posts and reflect what it sees. If your comment reads as Forum, it will tell you. If it reads as something else, it will tell you that too, and offer one small suggestion for how it might land differently.

This is the moment that matters most on Dialecta. It is the moment of friction. It is also the moment where the platform earns or loses your trust, depending on how you receive it. Take the reflection seriously, but do not take it as judgment. The system is not your boss. It is a mirror. Some days you will look in it and revise your comment. Some days you will look in it and post as is. Both are fine. The point is that you looked.

The reflection takes about eight seconds. Eight seconds is long enough for a thought you are about to put into the world to be a thought you actually want in the world. That is the entire trick.

---

## Your profile is a portrait, not a scoreboard

After you have been around for a little while, you will notice your profile starting to take a shape. It is not a follower count. It is not a karma score. It is something we call a Fingerprint: a quiet, visual portrait of the patterns that have shown up across the comments and articles you have written. There are six pillars of intellectual character that the platform observes over time, and the Fingerprint reflects the balance among them as it is, not as you wish it were.

You do not have to look at it. Many people will not. But it is there, and it grows with you, and one day you will glance at it and see something about yourself you had not quite named before. That moment, when it comes, is what the Fingerprint is for.

---

## The Stewards

A few of the writers here are recognized as Stewards. They are not staff. They are not appointed. The community recognizes them, over time, by the quality and consistency of the work they bring. You will see their names attached to articles you find yourself returning to, and you will, slowly, learn whose voice you trust and whose voice challenges you in ways you find generative. Following Stewards is one of the natural ways the site organizes itself for you over time. You do not need to do anything to start that process. Reading, with attention, is enough.

---

## The deeper rooms

There are more rooms in this house than this guide names. A growing library of philosophical and literary quotations chosen to keep company with the work being done here. Opinion mapping tools that let you place yourself inside the landscape of an article rather than just up- or down-voting it. A Living Guidebook that explains in detail anything you want to understand more thoroughly about how the platform works.

You do not need any of these on your first evening. They will be there when you want them. The guide most worth following on the first evening is your own curiosity.

---

## A few small habits that will make your time here richer

**Read before you write.** The first time you feel the urge to post a comment, sit with the urge for a moment and read three more comments before you do. You will be surprised how often the thing you almost posted is already being said better by somebody else, and how that lets you find the thing only you can say.

**Disagree with care, and then disagree.** This place is not built for soft agreement. Bring the position you actually hold. Anchor it to something specific. The community will meet you there.

**Trust the slowness.** The first week here will feel quieter than what you are used to. That quiet is the design working, not failing. The rewards on Dialecta arrive a beat later than the rewards on platforms you are leaving behind. They are also, when they arrive, considerably more nourishing. Stay for the second week.

**Come back tomorrow.** Almost everything good about being on Dialecta is cumulative. Patterns emerge over weeks. Friendships emerge over months. The Fingerprint emerges over a season. None of it happens in a single sitting. Come back tomorrow, and the next day, and the day after that, and the place will start to feel like yours.

---

## A welcome, again

You have been generous to give this place your attention. I do not take it for granted. The internet has trained all of us to expect to be sold to, mined, and managed. Dialecta is trying, with everything in its design, to do none of those things to you. What it is trying to do is offer you back something you already had, that the rest of your life online has been quietly stripping away. Time to think. Room to disagree. The company of people who are also tired of pretending.

Take your time. Read an article. Sit with the Pact. Find a comment that makes you want to argue. Argue well. The rest of the site is waiting for you, but only as patiently as you want it to be.

Welcome.

— Dan
