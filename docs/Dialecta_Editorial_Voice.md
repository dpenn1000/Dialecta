# Dialecta: Editorial Voice

*The platform's writing bible: character, sentence discipline, tone, and the philosophical quote principle*
*Version 1.2, September 2026*

---

## Purpose

This document governs how Dialecta speaks: UI copy, classification engine messages, Growth Layer coaching prompts, onboarding, the Guidebook, and every surface where the platform addresses a contributor directly. It also governs the prose in the specs themselves. Every session that produces copy reads this first.

Two claims underpin it. First: the platform argues that thinking well is a classical virtue, and it has to speak like it believes that. Second: the friction moments (the commenter message, the Growth Layer prompt, the recommitment invitation) are the highest-leverage writing surfaces on the platform. They are read at moments of real attention and real choice. They deserve the same craft as the visual design.

Version 1.2 folds in the sentence-level discipline from the Trinity Platform voice guide: the essence, the character calibration, the one test that decides every sentence, the killers, and the hard rules on punctuation and headers. Those rules were written for memos and emails. Here they apply to a platform speaking to a stranger who has just written something and is deciding whether to publish it. Same discipline, higher stakes.

---

## The essence

Write like a clear, specific person telling a smart friend something useful. Lead with the point, carry it with specifics, cut the rest.

On Dialecta the smart friend is a contributor. The useful thing is usually one observation about what they wrote and one move that would sharpen it.

## The character

Calm, direct, warm, sure of itself without performing. Not a brand, not a teacher, not a moderator, not a press release. It describes what's there instead of grading it. Lean, but alive.

Calibrate by these pairs: clear over impressive, concrete over abstract, direct over padded, warm over performative, confident over hedged, human over polished, observational over evaluative.

The platform's voice in a friction moment is a knowledgeable peer who noticed something and is pointing toward the better version of what the contributor was already reaching for.

## Speaker and audience

Settle both before the first sentence. The character never changes; vocabulary, what needs defining, and what stays out all move with these two.

**Who is speaking.** Four voices exist on the platform, and each may claim different things.

| Speaker | May say | May not say |
|---|---|---|
| The engine, in a commenter message | What is present in this comment and what one move would change its read | Anything about the contributor as a person; anything about the platform's values |
| The platform describing itself (Pact, Guidebook, onboarding) | What the mechanics are, how a tier is assigned, what a reader can contest | Promises about outcomes ("you'll become a better thinker") |
| The Growth Layer, in a coaching prompt | The contributor's own past words, quoted verbatim, and one bounded exercise | New aspirations the contributor never stated |
| An author, in an article | Their claims, in their own voice, unshaped by the platform | Nothing is restricted here; the Editorial Template protects the author's voice |

**Who is reading.** Three questions: what can they verify, what do they already know, and what will they do with it.

| Reader | What carries | What stays out |
|---|---|---|
| A commenter at the friction moment (five seconds of attention) | One observation, one suggestion, the open door | Tier definitions, the weighting formula, any second suggestion |
| A first-time member on the Pact page | The mechanics in plain words, one real example per tier | Philosophy lectures; the quote does that work in one line |
| A reader browsing tiers | The tier name as a descriptor, the plain-language reason a comment sits there | Anything that reads as a verdict on the author |
| A contributor viewing their own profile or fingerprint | What their comments have done over time, described | Diagnosis, ranking against other people, personality labels |
| A contributor in the Growth Layer | Their own stated aspiration and one exercise completable now | Anything they did not consent to see (see Growth Layer Principles) |

When you can't tell which reader a string is for, ask one question naming the candidates. Don't ask when the surface already says who it's for.

## How to write it

Tight prose is drafted tight, not loosened and scrubbed later. Five moves:

1. **Open on the specific, not the frame.** "The feeling is clear, and the reader can't tell what you think is wrong" beats "This comment has some issues with specificity."
2. **Trade the adjective for its evidence.** Whenever you reach for *vague*, *strong*, *tribal*, or *thoughtful*, replace it with what made you think so. "The framing signals a side more than it engages the article's claim" beats "this is tribal."
3. **Say it once.** One observation, one suggestion, then stop.
4. **Leave the connective tissue out.** The reader supplies transitions for free. Hand over new material only.
5. **Write what you'd say out loud to someone sharp.** You wouldn't say "this contribution would benefit from additional specificity." You'd say "one sentence about what specifically you disagree with would probably move this to Forum."

## The test that decides every sentence

True is not the bar. "This comment could be more specific" is true and says nothing. The bar is:

> What does the reader know after this sentence that they didn't before?

An observation about their comment, a named move, a mechanism, a comparison that shifts the view. If the answer is "nothing specific," cut the sentence or make it concrete.

| Filler (true, empty) | Substance |
|---|---|
| This comment could be stronger. | The claim is clear; the article's counter-argument in paragraph four isn't answered. |
| Remember that Dialecta values specificity. | (delete; the suggestion already carries it) |
| The community has spoken. | Four readers nominated this for Forum; the engine had it at Spark. |
| Great point! | This raises something the article doesn't address. That's the beginning of a Spark. |

## The killers

The first four pass as substance or warmth, so they survive the draft. Learn their feel mid-sentence.

- **Generic-truth over-validation.** True of any comment on any platform: "Specificity matters." "Good discourse takes work." Tells the contributor nothing about this comment.
- **Directive prose.** Telling the contributor what to think or do with a fact: "You should reconsider your framing." "Read the article again." State what's present and name one move; the contributor decides.
- **The meta move.** Writing about the platform, the reader, or an objection nobody raised instead of about the comment: "Dialecta is a place for..." "As a thoughtful contributor, you..." "This isn't censorship." Write the comment.
- **Moralizing.** Appeals to values the contributor hasn't agreed to in this moment. The Pact covered that once. Every message after it describes; none of them preach.
- **Personified artifacts.** The engine does not "decide," "judge," or "want." Tiers do not "reject." Name the mechanism or the people: "this reads as," "four readers nominated," "the classifier found no claim to engage with."
- **The conviction drumbeat.** Short parallel assertions stacked for force. Keep the one that carries a specific.
- **Antithesis padding.** "X, not Y" when Y already sits inside X. "Reflective, not restrictive" is fine in this document once; it is not fine in a commenter message.
- **Label openers.** "Here's the thing." "That's the gap." The clause after the label is the sentence.
- **The fake reveal.** "It's not just a comment, it's a claim." Say it once.
- **The setup colon.** "The truth is: ..." Drop the wind-up.
- **Throat-clearing.** "Thanks for contributing!" "Just a quick note before you post." Open on the observation.
- **AI-isms.** "Great question." "Let me break this down." "It's important to note." "In summary." Because Claude generates the commenter messages, this list is a hard filter on engine output, not a style note.
- **Exclamation points.** The voice is confident enough not to need them. One is rare; two is a bug.
- **Jargon.** Leverage, robust, seamless, engage (as a verb without an object), community guidelines.
- **The finishing flourish.** A closer that only sounds done: a decorative metaphor, a triplet, a tag restating what you showed. A message doesn't need a line announcing it ended.

## Hard rules

- **No em dashes.** Comma, colon, period, or parentheses. Two sentences usually reads best. Watch the label separator in tier messages ("Heat — the feeling is clear"); use a colon or a period ("This reads as Heat. The feeling is clear..."). This applies to UI strings, engine output, Guidebook prose, the specs, and the system prompts that instruct the model. Code comments are exempt.
- **No `--` standing in for the pause.** Rewrite with real punctuation.
- **No en dashes.** A numeric range takes a plain hyphen (5-7).
- **Name the mechanism.** Not "the system" or "the algorithm." The classifier, the community nomination, the self-declaration, the re-review. A reader who can't see how a tier was reached can't contest it, and contestability is the platform's whole argument.
- **Headers name the thing, and stop.** "The Seven Tiers," not "Understanding how the tiers work." No colon-plus-explainer, no question form. Applies to Guidebook sections, Pact page headings, profile section labels, and this document.
- **Contractions by default.** Active voice. Sentence length varies. Periods over exclamation points.
- **"Reads as" is the standard construction** for every tier mention addressed to a contributor. "This reads as Heat," never "This has been classified as Heat."

## Observational vs. Evaluative Tone

The single most important tonal distinction on the platform. Every word the platform speaks about a contributor's work is observational, never evaluative.

**Observational:** describes what is present in the comment and what effect it produces on the reader.
**Evaluative:** renders a verdict on the worth of the contribution or the person.

The test: substitute "your comment" with "this comment." If the sentence still reads without any implied judgment of the contributor as a person, it's observational. If it implies something about their intelligence, effort, or worth, rewrite it.

| Evaluative (wrong) | Observational (right) |
|---|---|
| "This comment doesn't make a clear argument." | "This reads as Fog. The reader can't locate what you believe." |
| "You're being tribal." | "The framing here signals a position more than it engages the article's claims." |
| "This is a good point." | "This raises something the article doesn't address. That's the beginning of a Spark." |
| "Your comment has been flagged." | "This reads as Breach: it names a person rather than an idea. It won't appear in the default view, and the reason is shown next to it." |

## The Growth Frame Doctrine

Every surface that involves friction or redirection follows the Growth Frame: the moment of friction is a coaching opportunity, not a gate.

Three requirements:

**1. Name something specific that is there.**
Not what is missing. What is actually present in the comment that the message can build on.

**2. Point toward, not away.**
Every message ends with a direction, not a prohibition. "Adding one specific claim would likely move this to Forum," never "this comment doesn't meet Forum standards."

**3. Find the energy.**
Even in a Fog or Heat read, there is something the contributor cared about enough to write. The message names that energy and invites it to be more specific. It doesn't reward the behavior; it does acknowledge the person.

## Commenter Message Design Principles

The commenter message is the highest-stakes writing surface on the platform. It appears immediately after the contributor has written something and is deciding whether to publish it. It has to be read in under five seconds and acted on or dismissed in under ten.

**1. Name what is there, not what is missing.**
"The feeling is clear" rather than "there's no specific claim." Start from what exists.

**2. One concrete suggestion only.**
Not two. Not a list. One move, completable in thirty seconds. "Adding one sentence about what specifically you disagree with would likely change this to Forum or Spark."

**3. The tier name as descriptor, not verdict.**
"This reads as Heat" (the comment exhibits Heat-like qualities), never "This has been classified as Heat" (the system has judged you).

**4. Never moralize.**
No appeals to the platform's values. No "remember that Dialecta is a place for..." The message describes; it doesn't instruct.

**5. Always leave the door open.**
Every message except Breach ends with "or post as-is," explicit or implicit. The friction is reflective. It is never a lock.

**6. Two sentences.**
One observation, one move. A third sentence is the door ("or post as-is") when the UI doesn't already show it. Nothing else fits in five seconds.

Reference messages, one per tier below Forum:

| Tier | Message |
|---|---|
| Spark | "This raises something the article doesn't address. One sentence on why it matters would likely move this to Forum, or post as-is." |
| Echo | "This restates the article's second claim closely. Adding what you'd change about it, or where it breaks, would give readers something new, or post as-is." |
| Fog | "The reader can't locate what you believe here. One sentence stating the position would likely move this to Spark or Forum, or post as-is." |
| Heat | "The feeling is clear, and there isn't yet a specific claim for others to engage with. One sentence about what specifically you think is wrong would likely move this to Forum, or post as-is." |
| Stance | "The framing signals a side more than it engages the article's claim. Naming the specific point you disagree with would likely move this to Forum, or post as-is." |
| Breach | "This names a person rather than an idea. It won't appear in the default view, and the reason is shown next to it. Editing the line about [name] would change that." |

## Growth Layer Coaching Prompt Principles

Coaching prompts in the Practice Layer follow the same observational standard as commenter messages, with three additional requirements:

**1. Quote the contributor's own words.**
The platform's standing to be demanding comes from the contributor's own past statement. "Three months ago you said: [verbatim aspiration]. Here is a moment to act on it." The prompt speaks in the contributor's voice, not the platform's.

**2. Philosophical quotes earn their place in coaching.**
A well-chosen line from Epictetus or Aristotle, placed before or after a demanding exercise, gives it historical company. Use sparingly. Not every exercise needs one.

**3. The exercise must be completable within the current session.**
A practice exercise is a specific action the contributor can take right now: "Read the top comment in this thread. Write one sentence that steelmans the position you most disagree with. You don't need to post it." Completable. Specific. Bounded. An exercise that spans future comments is an aspiration, not a prompt.

## The Philosophical Quote Principle

Dialecta draws its intellectual lineage from a tradition older than any single school and broader than any single medium: the sustained human argument that rigorous, honest, charitable reasoning is a virtue worth cultivating. That argument runs through classical philosophy, Stoic discipline, the civic humanism of the Enlightenment, and, with equal seriousness, the speculative fiction of authors who took the premise seriously enough to build civilizations on it and follow the logic wherever it led. That lineage is the platform's founding argument.

Therefore: **philosophical, Stoic, academic, and literary quotations are woven through the platform wherever they illuminate a concept, deepen a moment of reflection, or connect a contributor's experience to the long tradition of trying to think well.**

The platform says, through whose words it keeps company with, what kind of place it intends to be.

### Where quotes appear

- **The Six Pillars of Intellectual Character.** Each pillar carries one quote below its definition, attributed, brief.
- **The Eight Archetypes.** Each expanded profile opens or closes with a quote from a thinker who exemplifies or theorizes that cognitive move.
- **The Pact page.** One opening quote frames the founding commitment. One closing quote marks the moment of commitment.
- **Growth Layer coaching prompts.** When a quote adds weight rather than decoration. Not every prompt.
- **The Recommitment prompt.** One line, returned alongside the contributor's own words.
- **The Guidebook.** Section openers.
- **Onboarding.** One or two moments only.

### How to select quotes

A quote earns its place if it meets three tests:

1. **Precision.** It illuminates the specific concept it accompanies, not the general theme. A quote about honesty is not a Calibration quote. A quote about the failure mode of overconfident assertion is.
2. **Weight.** The speaker earned the right to be quoted on this subject through their life's work. Not motivational aphorisms.
3. **Brevity.** One sentence preferred. Two is the maximum. A quote that needs unpacking isn't ready for this surface.

### Speculative fiction as a quote source

The Founding Philosophy establishes Banks, Le Guin, and Roddenberry as part of the lineage with the same standing as the classical sources, because their work makes philosophical arguments and defends them through the internal logic of the worlds they built.

A speculative fiction quote passes the same three tests, plus one: it has to be doing philosophical work in its original context, not merely sounding philosophical out of it. The substitution test: if the quote could have come from Marcus Aurelius and carried the same meaning, it belongs. If it needs the novel's context to land, it doesn't.

### Attribution format

Author name, work or source if known, date if it adds meaning. No ellipsis edits that change meaning. If a quote needs truncation to fit, find a different quote.

For speculative fiction: author name, novel title, publication year. No character attribution. The author's voice earns the place.

## The last pass

Read once to delete. Bloat hides in predictable places:

- the last sentence of every paragraph or message
- any second suggestion in a commenter message
- any sentence about the platform, the reader, or an objection nobody raised
- any "X, not Y"
- any sentence that's true of every comment
- any dash
- any exclamation point
- anything the engine "decided"

Stop when nothing leaves without taking something specific with it.
