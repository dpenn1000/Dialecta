# Dialecta — Tier Psychology
*The Philosophy, Naming Rationale, and Behavioral Design of the Classification System*
*Version 1.1 — April 2026*

---

## Purpose

This document captures the psychological and philosophical reasoning behind Dialecta's seven-tier comment classification system. It is a companion to the Classification Engine Specification (which defines *how* the AI classifies), the Design Specification (which defines *how* the tiers look), and the Contributor Identity Specification (which defines the pattern-level layer of axes and archetypes that aggregates across many comments). This document answers a different question: *why* the tiers are what they are, *why* they are named what they are, and *what psychological effect* each tier is designed to produce on the person being classified.

This is a reference for editorial calibration, onboarding design, commenter message writing, and any future iteration of the tier system. Every naming decision, every tone choice, and every psychological nuance documented here was arrived at through deliberate analysis of the platform's behavioral science foundations.

---

## The Foundational Insight

Dialecta's source essay ("Social Media, Human Behavior, and the Rewiring of Society") establishes a thesis that governs every design decision on the platform:

**Human behavior is less influenced by stated values than by environmental incentives. Whatever a system rewards, humanity will adapt toward.**

The tier system *is* Dialecta's incentive structure. It is the mechanism by which the platform communicates what it values and what it doesn't. The tier names are the most visible expression of that mechanism — they are the words a person sees at the moment they've submitted a comment, the label that follows their contribution through its public life on the platform.

This means the tier names are not labels. They are behavioral nudges. They must be designed with the same care as any other incentive structure, because they will shape how people write, think, and engage on the platform over time.

---

## The Three Naming Principles

Every tier name was tested against three criteria:

### 1. Describe the Comment, Not the Person

The essay's core argument is that systems shape behavior — people are not inherently good or bad thinkers; they are adapted to the incentives around them. The tier names must reflect this. "Your comment is in The Heat" describes a behavior pattern. "You are being emotional" judges a person. The distinction is critical. A person classified in The Heat today may write a Forum-tier comment tomorrow. The name must leave that door open.

### 2. Observe, Don't Evaluate

The Classification Engine Spec defines the commenter message tone as "observational, not evaluative." The tier name is the first word of that observation. If the name itself is evaluative ("The Noise," "The Failure"), the observational tone of the message that follows is already undermined. The name must describe what is happening in the comment — not render a verdict on its worth.

### 3. Carry Its Own Definition

A new user should be able to roughly guess what a tier means without reading the guidebook. "The Forum" evokes civic discourse. "The Spark" evokes a beginning. "The Fog" evokes confusion. If the name requires a paragraph of explanation to convey its meaning, it fails at the moment it matters most — the first time a person sees it next to their own words.

## The Psychological Design of Each Tier

### Tier 1: 🥇 The Forum

**Classification criteria:** Specific claim (level 2–3), engaged with article content, reasoning present. Strong disagreement welcome — the claim is what elevates the comment, not the tone.

**What the name communicates:** Classical civic discourse. The Forum is a place — specifically, the place where serious people exchanged serious ideas in the public square. "Making it to The Forum" has a natural status resonance. The name implies that the person's comment has earned a seat at the most important table on the platform.

**Psychological intent:** Aspiration. The Forum is the tier everyone wants to reach. The name must feel genuinely earned, not merely assigned. It should trigger the same satisfaction as receiving recognition from peers you respect — not a gold star from an authority figure, but acknowledgment from a room of thinkers that you've contributed something worth engaging with.

**Why this name was chosen:** "Forum" carries dual resonance — the Roman Forum as a civic institution, and "forum" as a generic internet term. The classical connotation dominates because of the article ("The") and the tier's position at the top of the system. The generic internet association ("web forum") is weaker and fades once the user encounters the platform's editorial tone.

**Alternatives considered:** The Record (permanence, testimony — too formal), The Table (dinner-party warmth, "bringing something to the table" — strong but less gravity than Forum), The Floor (parliamentary, "having the floor" — too procedural).

**The critical edge case:** A comment can be angry, sharp, or contemptuous and still qualify for The Forum, provided it is anchored to a specific, substantive claim. The emotional register is never the disqualifier. The absence of a claimable proposition is. This is the single most important classification principle on the platform, and the name "Forum" supports it: forums were passionate, argumentative places. The name does not imply civility. It implies substance.

---

### Tier 2: 💡 The Spark

**Classification criteria:** Claim level 1–2, underdeveloped. The idea has potential but stops short. The comment invites "say more."

**What the name communicates:** The beginning of something. A spark is not a fire — but it could become one. The word is universally positive and inherently forward-looking.

**Psychological intent:** Encouragement. This is the most psychologically important tier in the system, because it's where the behavior-change moment lives. A person classified in The Spark has done something genuinely interesting — they just haven't finished the thought. The name must make them feel that the platform noticed their potential and is inviting them to develop it. Not "you failed to reach Forum" but "you're close, and the distance is one specific move."

**Why this name was chosen:** "Spark" is the strongest name in the system. It carries its own trajectory — sparks become fires, fires produce light. The metaphor naturally invites the response "say more," which is exactly what the classification engine's commenter message should prompt. It also pairs perfectly with The Forum: a spark is potential energy; the Forum is realized energy.

**The growth mechanic:** Over time, a person who regularly lands in The Spark tier should begin to internalize the pattern: "I had a good idea but didn't develop it." This self-awareness is the platform's actual product — not the classification itself, but the thinking habits the classification trains. The name "Spark" supports this because it frames underdevelopment as proximity to something good, not distance from a standard.

**Alternatives considered:** The Seed (organic growth — slightly too passive; seeds need someone else to water them, while sparks have their own energy), The Draft (writing process — accurate but nobody brags about having a draft), The Thread (something to pull on — contaminated by Twitter/X associations).

---

### Tier 3: 🪞 The Echo

**Classification criteria:** Claim level 0–1, restating. May contain a claim, but it mirrors what's already in the article or a prior comment without adding to it. Clear but unoriginal.

**What the name communicates:** Repetition without origination. An echo is the return of a sound that someone else made. It's not the person's own voice — it's a reflection of the article's voice.

**Psychological intent:** Neutrality. The Echo is the most emotionally neutral tier in the system. It is not a punishment, not an insult, and not an invitation. It is a description: your comment repeated what's already been said. The person classified here should feel observed, not judged. The goal is a mild "huh, I guess I did just restate the article" — a moment of self-awareness without emotional charge.

**Why this name was chosen:** Despite the "echo chamber" association (which exists at the macro/political level), at the individual comment level, "echo" is simply the most accurate word for what's happening. A commenter sees "This reads as The Echo" and immediately understands: I echoed something. The word does its job without requiring explanation. The echo chamber concern was weighed and ultimately judged to be a macro-level association that doesn't activate when the word appears as a classification on a specific comment.

**The distinction from Fog:** Echo is clear but unoriginal. Fog is unclear regardless of originality. Both lack substance, but for different reasons. The names support this distinction: an echo is perfectly audible (clear) but not new; fog is opaque (unclear) regardless of what's inside it.

**Alternatives considered:** The Refrain (musical repetition — beautiful but slightly too positive for a tier that's "not propulsive"), The Mirror (visual reflection — risk of "holding up a mirror" implying critique), The Chorus (communal agreement — too warm for a non-propulsive tier).

---

### Tier 4: 🌫️ The Fog

**Classification criteria:** Claim level 0, unclear. The reader cannot determine what the person believes or what point they're making.

**What the name communicates:** Opacity. You can't see through it. The word describes the reader's experience of the comment, not the writer's intent — which is important, because the writer may not realize they're being unclear.

**Psychological intent:** Gentle confusion. The person classified in The Fog should think "I guess my point didn't come through" rather than "I'm being told I'm stupid." Fog is a weather phenomenon — it's not anyone's fault, it just happens. The name avoids attributing the unclarity to the person's intelligence or effort. It attributes it to the comment's effect on the reader.

**Why this name was chosen:** Fog is the most immediately intuitive name for unclarity. It's a sensory metaphor — everyone has experienced not being able to see through fog. It also has a natural resolution: fog lifts. The implication is that clarity is available if the person revises.

**The commenter message opportunity:** Because Fog is about the reader's experience, the commenter message can be framed as "the reader can't tell what you believe" — which shifts the problem from "you wrote badly" to "the reader needs more." This is a subtle but important reframe that keeps the tone observational.

**Alternatives considered:** The Drift (untethered, moving without direction — more active and behavioral than Fog, but potentially more judgmental since "drifting" implies the person isn't trying), The Blur (visual, in motion — interesting but less universal than Fog), The Haze (warmer than Fog but too similar to justify the change).

---

### Tier 5: 🔥 The Heat

**Classification criteria:** Claim level 0–1, high emotion dominant. Emotion is present and dominant; claim is absent or buried. Passion without a point.

**What the name communicates:** Energy without direction. "More heat than light" is one of the most universally understood phrases in English. Heat is undeniably present — you can feel it — but it doesn't illuminate anything. The name acknowledges that the person cares (which is generous and true) while naming that the caring isn't focused on anything specific (which is accurate).

**Psychological intent:** Acknowledgment of intensity without reward for it. This is the tier that most directly challenges the engagement-maximization model the essay describes. On other platforms, emotional intensity *is* the signal of quality — the hotter the take, the more engagement it gets. Dialecta's tier system explicitly separates intensity from quality. The Heat says: "We see that you feel strongly. That's real. But feeling strongly is not the same as saying something specific." The name must carry both halves of that message.

**Why this name was chosen:** The phrase "more heat than light" is the tier's secret weapon. It's the most compact possible explanation of the classification, and it's already in common use. Keeping the name "Heat" makes that phrase available to every commenter message, every tooltip, every onboarding explanation. No alternative produces a comparably useful explanatory phrase.

**The Forum/Heat boundary — the hardest call in the system:** Both Forum and Heat comments can be passionate, angry, and sharp. The discriminator is whether the emotion is attached to a specific, arguable proposition. If it is: Forum. If the emotion *is* the content: Heat. The name "Heat" supports this distinction because heat is a physical property (temperature) that exists independently of any object. Forum-tier passion is heat *applied to something*. Heat-tier passion is heat *floating free*.

**Alternatives considered:** The Flare (sudden bright burst — loses the "more heat than light" phrase), The Vent (releasing pressure — too casual, too therapy-coded for classical aspirations), The Surge (powerful but undirected — more dramatic but doesn't produce a comparably useful explanatory phrase).

---

### Tier 6: ⚡ The Stance

**Classification criteria:** Any claim level — tribal, rhetorical, or identity-signaling markers overshadow whatever claim may exist. The primary function of the comment is declaring group membership, not engaging with ideas.

**What the name communicates:** A position taken, feet planted — but not moving, not engaging, not meeting anyone. A stance is rigid, public, and declarative. It's what the essay describes as "alignment over understanding." The word captures the root behavior of tribal rhetoric: the person has planted their flag, and the flag is the entire message.

**Psychological intent:** This is the most sensitive tier in the system. Being classified as "tribal" is a loaded judgment — it implies the person is performing group membership rather than thinking independently. The name must describe this behavior pattern without sounding like an accusation of bad faith.

"The Stance" achieves this by naming the behavior's structural feature (rigidity, declaration) rather than its motive (tribalism, signaling). A person classified here should think "I guess I stated my position without engaging with the other side" — which is an observation they can act on — rather than "I'm being called a tribal thinker" — which triggers defensiveness.

**Why this name was chosen over "Static":** The original name ("The Static") was conceptually precise — signal interference, noise blocking the real signal. But it was too abstract. "Your comment is static" doesn't intuitively convey "you're declaring a position rather than engaging with ideas." The word "static" in everyday use primarily means "unchanging," and the signal-interference meaning is secondary for many people. "Stance" is immediately understood and names the exact behavior the classification engine detects.

**Why this name was chosen over "Noise":** "Noise" was the most direct option from the signal-vs-noise philosophy, but it's the harshest name in the middle tiers. "Your comment is noise" is a judgment about value, not an observation about behavior. Heat says "you're feeling strongly"; Fog says "you're unclear"; Noise says "you're worthless to the conversation." That's a different register entirely, and it would push people away rather than inviting them to do better.

**The three behaviors this tier catches:**
1. **Tribal identity signaling** — "Anyone who thinks X has never Y." Declaring which team you're on.
2. **Rhetorical performance** — Grand language, absolute certainty, mic-drop phrasing that sounds devastating but isn't about anything specific.
3. **Coded language** — Dog whistles, in-group vocabulary, phrases designed for one audience's recognition.

All three share one root: the comment is performing for an implied audience that already agrees rather than engaging with the ideas in the article or the people who might disagree.

**The pairing with Forum:** Forum is dialogue. Stance is the refusal of dialogue. This pairing creates the clearest possible articulation of what the platform values: it's not about being right or wrong, agreeing or disagreeing. It's about whether you're engaging with the other side or just declaring your position to your own side.

**Alternatives considered:** The Static (too abstract, everyday meaning is "unchanging"), The Noise (too harsh — judges value, not behavior), The Banner (carrying a flag — most concrete visual metaphor but more pointed than other mid-tier names), The Jersey (wearing team colors — intuitive but too casual for classical aspirations), The Signal (broadcasting rather than conversing — strong but conflicts with "Signal" as a prior platform name candidate), The Megaphone (one-directional amplification — creative brief explicitly avoids megaphone imagery), The Rally (gathering energy — too politically loaded).

---

### Tier 7: 🚫 The Breach

**Classification criteria:** N/A — personal attack. Content targets a person, not an idea. The only tier where content is suppressed from the default view (but never deleted, and always accompanied by a transparent reason).

**What the name communicates:** A boundary has been crossed. Specifically, the boundary established in the Pact — the commitment every user makes when they join the platform. The Breach connects the classification directly to that foundational agreement: you agreed to engage with ideas, not attack people. This comment breaks that agreement.

**Psychological intent — the retention paradox:** This is the hardest tier to name because it must hold two truths simultaneously:

1. **This specific act crossed the line.** The person must feel the weight of where they are. A personal attack is the one behavior the platform considers a genuine failure mode — not a matter of degree (like Heat or Fog) but a matter of kind.

2. **You are not permanently defined by this act.** The essay's own logic applies: behavior is shaped by systems, not willpower. A person who attacked someone today may be capable of Forum-level thinking tomorrow. The name must leave that door open.

The word "breach" holds both truths. A breach is serious — breaches in walls, breaches in contracts, breaches in trust. But breaches are also repairable. A breached wall can be rebuilt. A breached contract can be renegotiated. The word carries consequence without permanence.

**Why this name was chosen:** Dialecta's Pact Page is the foundational onboarding experience — a mutual agreement between the user and the platform. When someone attacks a person rather than engaging with ideas, they have breached that pact. "The Breach" makes this connection explicit and structural. It doesn't say "you're a bad person." It says "you broke an agreement we both signed." This framing converts shame (which triggers defensiveness and disengagement) into accountability (which can trigger reflection and correction).

**Why shame doesn't work (even though it feels right):** Shame triggers defensiveness. Defensiveness triggers disengagement. Disengagement means the person either leaves the platform or doubles down. Neither outcome produces growth. The essay's own logic applies: if the system shames people, it will produce shame-driven behavior — withdrawal or escalation, never reflection. The goal is closer to what a firm, respected teacher does: "That's not what we do here. You know that. Try again." Disappointment, not punishment.

**Why this name was chosen over "Off the Air":** The original name used a broadcast metaphor — being disconnected from the frequency. While evocative, it framed the platform as a gatekeeper silencing speech, which contradicts Dialecta's transparency principle (nothing is deleted, everything is visible with explanation). "Off the Air" also broke the naming pattern — it was a phrase rather than "The [Noun]" — which, while intentionally signaling a categorical break, made the system feel less internally consistent.

**Why this name was chosen over "The Gutter":** The Gutter had the visceral weight that this tier needs, but "gutter" carries class-based connotations (gutter language, gutter press) that cross from observing behavior to judging character. This is the shame-trigger risk: the person hears "you're trashy" rather than "you crossed a line."

**Alternatives considered:** The Gutter (visceral but class-coded), The Margin (editorial, gentle — too gentle for the only tier where content is suppressed), The Foul (sports, temporary — strong "you can come back" energy but might feel too casual), The Line (simplest possible — "you crossed it" — but describes location rather than behavior, breaking the naming pattern), Off the Air (broadcast disconnection — frames platform as gatekeeper).

## The Tier Spectrum as a Psychological Gradient

The seven tiers form three psychological zones:

### Zone 1: Aspiration (Forum, Spark)
The person is contributing to the discourse. The question is degree of development.
- **Forum** = fully developed contribution
- **Spark** = partially developed contribution

Both tiers use positive metaphors. The person should feel good about being here. The movement from Spark to Forum is the platform's primary growth vector — the transition the system most wants to encourage.

### Zone 2: Description (Echo, Fog, Heat)
The person's comment has a structural characteristic that limits its usefulness. The names describe that characteristic without moral judgment.
- **Echo** = repetition (clear but unoriginal)
- **Fog** = opacity (unclear regardless of originality)
- **Heat** = emotional intensity without specificity

All three use sensory/atmospheric metaphors. The person should feel observed, not judged. Each tier's commenter message includes a single concrete suggestion for what would change the classification — this is where the behavior-change mechanism lives.

### Zone 3: Boundary (Stance, Breach)
The person's comment has crossed from individual expression into a pattern the platform considers structurally harmful.
- **Stance** = performing group identity rather than engaging with ideas
- **Breach** = targeting a person rather than engaging with ideas

These tiers are categorically different from Zone 2. The Zone 2 tiers describe *how* a person engages. The Zone 3 tiers describe *whether* a person is engaging at all — or doing something else instead (signaling, attacking). The names are firmer and more structural: a stance is a posture you've locked into; a breach is an agreement you've broken.

## The Role of Metaphor Families

The tier names draw from several metaphor domains, and the distribution is intentional:

| Domain | Tiers | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Place/civic** | The Forum | The aspirational tier is a *place* — somewhere you want to be. |
| **Energy/element** | The Spark, The Heat | Energy metaphors describe potential and intensity on a spectrum. Spark = focused potential. Heat = diffuse intensity. |
| **Sensory/atmospheric** | The Echo, The Fog | Sensory metaphors describe the reader's experience of the comment — what it sounds like (echo) or looks like (fog). |
| **Structural/positional** | The Stance | A structural metaphor for a comment that has taken a fixed position rather than engaging dynamically. |
| **Contractual/architectural** | The Breach | A structural metaphor for breaking an agreement — the Pact — that the user explicitly made. |

The metaphor domains roughly map to the three psychological zones: civic/aspirational for Zone 1, sensory/atmospheric for Zone 2, structural/contractual for Zone 3. This creates an intuitive gradient: the names shift from inviting places, through sensory descriptions, to structural violations — mirroring the shift from aspiration, through observation, to boundary enforcement.

## Commenter Message Design Principles

The commenter message is the behavioral intervention point — the moment of friction between submitting a comment and publishing it. Every message must follow these principles:

### 1. Name what is there, not what is missing.
"This reads as The Heat — the feeling is clear" (names what exists) rather than "This reads as The Heat — there's no specific claim" (names what's absent). The person should see their own comment reflected accurately before being told what would change the classification.

### 2. One concrete suggestion per message.
Not two. Not a list. One move. "Adding one sentence about what specifically you disagree with would likely change this to Forum or Spark." The person should be able to act on the suggestion in 30 seconds or less.

### 3. Use the tier name as a descriptor, not a verdict.
"This reads as The Fog" (the comment exhibits fog-like qualities) rather than "This has been classified as Fog" (the system has judged you). The verb "reads as" is the standard construction — it positions the classification as an observation, not a ruling.

### 4. Never moralize.
No "you should try to be more constructive." No "remember that this platform values..." No appeals to the person's better nature. The system works because it describes behavior accurately, not because it tells people how to behave. The essay's foundational insight applies: you don't change behavior by asking people to be better. You change behavior by designing environments where better behavior is the path of least resistance.

### 5. Always leave the door open.
Every message except The Breach should end with an implicit or explicit "or post as-is." The system is non-blocking. The friction is reflective, not restrictive. The Breach is the only tier where posting is blocked pending review, and even there, the message should communicate that the boundary exists to protect the discourse, not to punish the person.

## Visual Identity — The Brightness Ladder

The tier system's visual language is built on a single organizing principle: **lightness equals honor.** The highest tier (Forum) is the lightest — almost paper itself, barely a tint. Each tier steps down in luminosity until Breach arrives at a deep, near-black red. The eye reads the hierarchy instinctively before it reads any text. A user scanning a feed of badges can identify each tier by color alone, even before parsing the label.

This is not an arbitrary aesthetic choice. It is a behavioral nudge encoded in the visual system itself. The brightest badges are the ones the platform celebrates. The darkest are the ones it constrains. The gradient from light to dark literally maps the gradient from aspiration to boundary — making the value system visible at every level of attention, from a passing glance at a feed to a focused read of a single comment.

### The Brightness Ladder

| Tier | Color identity | Brightness | Hue rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Forum** | Almost-white cream → soft gold | Lightest | Gold has always meant "the standard." The lightest tint suggests it's almost paper itself — pure, unadorned, the baseline of honor. |
| **Spark** | Warm cream → amber | Light-warm | Warm yellow shifts toward amber. Still bright, still positive, but with the energy of something igniting. |
| **Echo** | Pale sage → muted green | Mid-light, cool | The first cooling. Sage is organic and natural — repetition is not negative, just not propulsive. The cool green creates the first visual pause. |
| **Fog** | Light slate → cool gray-blue | Mid, cool | Blue-gray is the temperature of unclarity. Cold, atmospheric, opaque. The neutral middle of the system. |
| **Heat** | Burnt orange → deep ember | Mid-dark, warm | The platform "heats back up" as we leave the calm middle. Orange signals temperature without yet signaling danger. |
| **Stance** | Rust → dark brick | Dark | Earthy, heavy, immovable. A position planted in the ground. Not a warning yet, but no longer in the calm zone. |
| **Breach** | Deep red → near-black wine | Darkest | The only tier where color carries moral weight. Red means a boundary was crossed. Deepened to wine so it reads as structural rather than alarming. |

### The Three-Zone Color Logic

The brightness ladder maps directly to the three psychological zones from earlier in this document:

- **Zone 1 (Aspiration: Forum, Spark)** — *warm and bright.* These tiers feel celebratory. The user wants to be here.
- **Zone 2 (Description: Echo, Fog, Heat)** — *cool and calm* in the middle, then *warming back up* as it approaches the boundary. These tiers feel observational. The user is being described, not judged.
- **Zone 3 (Boundary: Stance, Breach)** — *warm and dark.* These tiers feel structural. The user has done something the platform considers categorically different from the middle zone.

The cooling-then-warming temperature curve is intentional. It creates a visual rhythm: gold → amber → sage → slate → orange → rust → wine. The neutral middle is literally a calm pause between the warmth of aspiration and the warmth of warning. This rhythm reinforces the philosophy: the platform is not punishing as you descend the ladder. It is observing. Until it isn't — and then the color tells you.

## Visual Identity — The Tier Icons

Each tier has a custom SVG icon, not an emoji. This is a deliberate choice. Emojis render differently across platforms (iOS, Android, Windows, Linux), they cannot inherit the tier's color, and they do not scale crisply at small sizes. Custom SVG icons render identically everywhere, inherit the tier color automatically, and stay sharp at any size from 14px to 200px.

Each icon was chosen to be:
1. **Immediately recognizable** at the smallest badge size (14px)
2. **Conceptually tied** to the tier's meaning, not its name
3. **Visually distinct** from every other tier icon, so badges can be identified by shape alone

### The Seven Icons

| Tier | Icon | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Forum** | Roman columns under a triangular pediment | Classical civic architecture. Reads as "public square" instantly. Not a courthouse — Forum is a place for discourse, not judgment. |
| **Spark** | Lightning bolt | Electric, sudden, ignition. The moment of an idea catching. Spark is electrical — the flame belongs to Heat. |
| **Echo** | Two arrows chasing each other in a circular loop | The universal "repeat / cycle / loop back" symbol. Captures repetition without being negative — repetition is just what an echo does. |
| **Fog** | Fluffy scalloped cloud | A puffy cloud silhouette with rounded edges. Unmistakable at any size. The shape reads as "cloud" instantly. |
| **Heat** | Single asymmetric flame | Now that Spark uses lightning, the flame belongs entirely to Heat. Fire is thermal energy — the natural metaphor for emotional heat. |
| **Stance** | Pennant flag on a planted pole with ground line | Fixed, claimed territory. The flag is not waving or advancing — it is planted. Exactly what a stance is. |
| **Breach** | Two chain links pulled apart, with a diagonal snap line | Communicates "broken agreement" structurally and physically. The Pact has been broken; the chain shows it. |

### Why Spark is electric and Heat is fire (not the other way around)

This pairing matters. Both tiers use energy metaphors, but they describe categorically different things. Spark is the *beginning of an idea* — the ignition moment, brief and bright, with potential to become something more. Lightning captures this perfectly: a single instant of electrical energy that arcs from one place to another. Heat is *emotional intensity without focus* — sustained, ambient, present but undirected. Fire captures this: a continuous burn that produces warmth (and sometimes light) but isn't aimed at anything in particular.

If we reversed them — flame for Spark, lightning for Heat — the metaphors would break. A flame is sustained, not ignition. Lightning is sudden, not sustained. The electric/thermal split is the only assignment that fits both tiers' actual classification criteria.

### The "fire family" deliberately spans two tiers

Spark, Heat, and Breach all share warm color territory (amber, orange, deep red), and Spark and Heat share energy-metaphor icons. This is intentional: the platform wants to show that *energy is not the problem.* The problem is whether the energy is focused on a specific claim (Forum can be passionate too), is undirected emotion (Heat), or is signaling group identity (Stance). The shared visual family across these tiers communicates: *we see the energy, we're not punishing it, we're describing where it's pointed.*

## Open Questions for Future Sessions

- **Localization:** These tier names are deeply English-language. The metaphors (Forum, Spark, Echo, Fog, Heat, Stance, Breach) may not carry equivalent connotations in other languages. Multilingual deployment will require independent naming sessions per language, not direct translation. Icon meanings should also be tested cross-culturally — Roman columns may not read as "civic discourse" in every context.

- **User testing of tier names:** The Stance and The Breach are still provisional pending real-user feedback. Specifically, the test should measure: (a) Can users guess the tier's meaning from the name alone? (b) Does being classified in a given tier feel observational or judgmental? (c) Does the commenter message successfully prompt revision behavior?

- **Icon recognition at minimum size:** The 14px badge is the smallest size icons need to hold up at. The Echo loop and the Forum columns are the most detail-heavy icons in the set; they should be tested in real comment feeds to confirm they remain identifiable when scrolling quickly.

- **Accessibility and color blindness:** The brightness ladder is the system's primary signal, which means it should hold up for users with deuteranopia, protanopia, and tritanopia. Lightness-based hierarchies tend to perform well across color blindness types because lightness is preserved when hue information is lost — but this should be verified with simulator testing before launch.
