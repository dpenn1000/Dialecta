The Letterpress
Dialecta's Visual Language

Every color, typeface, spacing value, and component pattern used across the Dialecta platform originates in this document. Version 1.3 promotes three new color tokens to first-class status (parchment-cousin dark card, slate-graphite reading prose, deep warm brown lede), adds a Component Library section, and standardizes feature naming on the Advocate archetype across the spec. The aesthetic is editorial minimalism with classical undertones — warm cream surfaces, ink-tone text, gold as the semantic signal of quality, and a tier system whose visual hierarchy mirrors its psychological hierarchy.

Section 01

Color Tokens

The palette is built on warm neutrals — cream, not white; ink, not black. Gold is the only chromatic accent on surfaces, used semantically to mean "this is the standard." Tier colors follow a brightness ladder that maps the platform's psychological hierarchy directly into luminosity.

Section 02

Typography

Four typefaces, each with a distinct role. Cormorant Garamond carries editorial authority. Source Serif 4 is the reading face. DM Sans handles interface elements. DM Mono marks system output and data.

Cormorant Garamond · DisplayHeadlines, article titles, brand wordmark

The Rewiring of Society

Behavioral Conditioning at Scale

The question is not whether social media influences us.

Source Serif 4 · ReadingArticle body, comment bodies, long-form

Every interaction on social media is a form of conditioning. When a post receives attention, it reinforces the behavior that produced it. This creates a continuous feedback loop — the platform rewards certain behaviors, users unconsciously adapt, and the platform amplifies those behaviors further.

Source Serif 4 · LedeArticle subtitles, opening italics, intro paragraphs

A platform where ideas are the protagonist, not identities or political teams. The mechanics of engagement reoriented toward curiosity and honest disagreement.

Italic Decision RuleWhere each italic treatment lives

The hero gold-gradient italic (Cormorant + warm gold gradient + drop-shadow) is reserved for the top 250px gradient zone and dark surfaces only. The Section italic (terra → gold) is for editorial accent within cream reading territory. The lede italic (Source Serif 4, light weight, --text-lede) is for article subtitles and opening passages. Never mix them in the same eye-line.

Section 08

A two-bar system: the primary logo bar carries the cream-to-dark gradient with the wordmark at left (40px); a secondary sub-nav bar sits flush below with a warm metallic shimmer fill and white gradient rules at top and bottom. Section links live in the sub-nav in warm ink (#3a342c). A shared max-width inner container (1060px) aligns logo and links with the page content column. Section 08b

Page Background

This is the canonical Dialecta page background, primary for all platform builds. A diagonal gradient runs from the upper-left corner (warm stone) to the cream body. The gradient is lightened to 80% from earlier dark values, preserving atmospheric depth while keeping ink-tone text fully legible throughout the hero zone without color switching. A fixed SVG fractalNoise paper grain at opacity 0.035 overlays the entire body.

Hero Zone
Cards lift off the lightened backdrop.
The 80% lightened gradient maintains spatial depth without requiring on-dark text treatment — ink-tone headings and body copy remain fully readable throughout.

Section 11

Editorial Surfaces

Two surface treatments carry editorial weight. The Covenant surface — warm antique paper — is canonical for The Pact, where the moment should feel like a printed charter rather than a CTA. Dark editorial cards (--dark-card parchment cousin) are canonical for Advocate cards, Delta panels, and featured comments. The solid --bg-dark surface is retired — it has no canonical use on the platform.
