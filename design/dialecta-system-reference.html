Layer 1 — Per-Comment Analysis

Stage A: What the Engine Extracts

Before any tier is assigned, the AI extracts six structured observations from every comment. These fields are the raw data for both classification and the contributor identity layer.

Field 1 — Claim
What claim is the commenter making, if any?
Quote or paraphrase the core proposition. If none exists, state explicitly. This field drives everything else.
Field 2 — Specificity
How developed is the claim?
0 — None 1 — Vague 2 — Specific 3 — Developed
Field 3 — Emotional Register
What is the intensity of emotion present?
Low Medium High
High emotion alone does not determine tier. A passionate comment with a Level 3 claim is Forum.
Field 4 — Tribal Markers
Are identity-signaling or rhetorical patterns present?
Yes No
If Yes: cite a brief example from the comment. Three types: tribal identity signaling, rhetorical performance, coded in-group language.
Field 5 — Article Engagement
Does the comment engage something specific in the article?
Specific General
Requires author key claims (3–5 per article) as engine input. General engagement against a specific article raises the quality bar.
Field 6 — Opposing View Engaged
Does the commenter acknowledge the strongest counterargument?
Yes Partially No
Not required for Forum. Feeds the Magnanimity axis. The Advocate archetype requires consistent "Yes" here.
Layer 1 — Claim Threshold

The Claim Specificity Spectrum

Claim specificity is the primary discriminator between tiers. The engine assesses level 0–3 on every comment. Levels 2 and 3 are the threshold for Forum consideration.

Level Name Description Example Maps to Tiers
0
 No Claim Pure feeling, label, or tribal signal. Nothing to engage with on substance. "This is exactly the kind of thinking that's destroying discourse." Fog Heat Stance
1
 Vague Claim An assertion exists but is too general to engage specifically. You know which side they're on, not what they think. "The author way overstates how much algorithms control us." Spark Echo
2
 Specific Claim An identifiable proposition. Someone could directly agree or disagree with it on substance. "The author overstates algorithmic control because he ignores that heavy users actively seek out the content they receive." Forum Spark
3
 Developed Claim A specific proposition with supporting reasoning, evidence, or a named counter-argument. Full engagement. "...Pew Research (2024) shows the top 20% of social media users by time spent are also the heaviest active posters — suggesting the conditioning runs both ways." Forum (preferred)

The Seven Tiers

Each tier is assigned by the engine in Stage B, using Stage A observations. Tiers are descriptions of comment behavior, never verdicts on the person.

🥇
The Forum
Zone 1 — Aspiration · Lightest color (cream/gold)
Specific claim (Level 2 minimum, 3 preferred), engaged with article content, reasoning present. Strong disagreement fully welcome — the claim is what elevates the comment, not the tone.
Claim: 2–3 Emotion: Any Tribal: No Icon: Roman columns
💡
The Spark
Zone 1 — Aspiration · Warm amber
Claim Level 1–2, underdeveloped. The idea has potential but stops short. The comment invites "say more." Most psychologically important tier — the behavior-change moment lives here.
Claim: 1–2 Underdeveloped Tribal: No Icon: Lightning bolt
🪞
The Echo
Zone 2 — Description · Cool sage
Claim Level 0–1, restating. Mirrors what's already in the article or a prior comment without adding to it. Clear but unoriginal. Neutral tier — not harmful, not propulsive.
Claim: 0–1 Clear + Unoriginal Icon: Circular arrows
🌫️
The Fog
Zone 2 — Description · Cool slate-blue
Claim Level 0, unclear. The reader cannot determine what the person believes or what point they're making. Different from Echo: Fog is unclear; Echo is clear but unoriginal.
Claim: 0 Unclear intent Icon: Fluffy cloud
🔥
The Heat
Zone 2 — Description · Burnt orange
Claim Level 0–1, emotion dominant. Passion without a point. More heat than light. The emotion is the content. Distinguished from Forum: Forum has heat applied to something; Heat has heat floating free.
Claim: 0–1 Emotion: High Icon: Asymmetric flame
⚡
The Stance
Zone 3 — Boundary · Dark rust
Any claim level, but tribal/rhetorical/identity-signaling markers dominate. The comment is performing for an implied audience that already agrees rather than engaging with ideas. Three subtypes: tribal identity signaling, rhetorical performance, coded language.
Claim: Any level Tribal: Yes (dominant) Icon: Planted pennant flag
🚫
The Breach
Zone 3 — Boundary · Deepest color (wine/near-black red) · ONLY tier where content is suppressed from default view
Content targets a person rather than an idea. Name-calling, slander, or targeted personal attack. The Pact has been broken. Not deleted — suppressed from default view with transparent reason shown. The only tier where posting is blocked pending review.
Personal attack Pact broken Suppressed (not deleted) Icon: Broken chain links
Layer 2 — Edge Cases

The Hardest Tier Boundaries

These four pairs are the classification calls most likely to be inconsistent. Each has a single discriminating rule.

Boundary What they share The discriminator
Forum vs Heat
Both can be intense, passionate, and sharp. Is the emotion attached to a specific arguable proposition? If yes: Forum. If the emotion is the content: Heat.
Stance vs Heat
Both are emotionally charged. Primary function: expressing feeling = Heat. Signaling group membership = Stance. Assign to the dominant function. Comments can have elements of both.
Spark vs Echo
Both lack full development. Does the comment add something new? Echo restates; Spark gestures toward something not yet in the article or prior comments.
Fog vs Echo
Both lack substance. Clarity: Echo is clear but unoriginal. Fog is unclear regardless of originality.
Layer 3 — Contributor Identity

The Six Pillars of Intellectual Character

Aggregated across a contributor's full comment history. Each pillar is built from two pieces: graduation count (quality-gated) and tier-mix texture (the historical tier distribution behind those graduations). Together they measure not what someone believes, but how someone does the work of thinking in public.

Pair 1 — Substance
Acuity
Substance Pair
How precise and claimable the contributor's points tend to be. Driven primarily by Claim Specificity (Field 2) across all comments. The sharpness of intellectual vision.
Trades against Reach — going deep on one topic competes with going wide across many.
Reach
Substance Pair
How many distinct topic areas the contributor has engaged across. A wide Fingerprint here is rare when Acuity is also tall.
Trades against Acuity. High Reach + High Acuity is rare and visible.
Pair 2 — Intellectual Honesty
Calibration
Intellectual Honesty Pair
How well the contributor's confidence matches the strength of their evidence. Fed by Field 6 (Opposing View) and Stage A claim quality relative to evidence cited.
Trades against Discourse — high-volume back-and-forth tends to outrun careful position-holding.
Magnanimity
Intellectual Honesty Pair
How faithfully the contributor represents views they disagree with before engaging. The Principle of Charity enacted as behavior. Driven by Field 6 (Opposing View Engaged). The Advocate archetype is its fullest expression.
Rarest high-scoring pillar. Directly attacks the tribal default of strawmanning. From Aristotle: greatness of soul engaging with the strongest version of opposition.
Pair 3 — Engagement
Discourse
Engagement Pair
How often the contributor engages in sustained back-and-forth rather than hit-and-run. Measures the overall discussion, not a single exchange. Participation in threads, not comment volume.
Trades against Calibration and Magnanimity — replying to everything accumulates some Heat in Discourse history.
Consistency
Engagement Pair
How regularly the contributor shows up over time. The temporal pillar — the only one that can't be gamed by a single intense session. Pattern, not endurance.
Trades against quality pillars early on. Regular early-career posting produces noisier history.

The Eight Archetypes

Platform-assigned from observed pillar patterns. Never user-declared. A contributor can declare an aspirational archetype — the platform then surfaces the exact pillar behaviors required and tracks progress quietly. Three epistemic, three constructive, one magnanimous, one temporal.

🔎
The Skeptic
Epistemic stance
Questions premises before accepting conclusions. Tests the foundation of an argument before engaging with its superstructure.
Primary: High Calibration
Secondary: High Acuity
🔬
The Empiricist
Epistemic stance
Grounds every claim in evidence and data. Has the highest Acuity requirement of any archetype — claims must be claimable and evidenced.
Primary: Maxed Acuity
Secondary: High Calibration
🗺
The Contextualist
Epistemic stance
Situates ideas in their historical and cultural frame. Goes wide rather than deep — the only epistemic archetype that privileges Reach over depth.
Primary: High Reach
Secondary: High Calibration
🌀
The Synthesizer
Constructive move
Finds unexpected connections across domains. The constructive archetype most dependent on Reach — you can't synthesize what you haven't explored.
Primary: High Reach
Secondary: High Acuity
🏗
The Builder
Constructive move
Extends ideas into practical frameworks. The most discourse-intensive archetype — building requires sustained back-and-forth to stress-test the framework.
Primary: High Acuity
Secondary: High Discourse
💡
The Illuminator
Constructive move
Makes complex ideas accessible without losing nuance. The hardest constructive archetype to earn — requires all three non-temporal pairs at meaningful levels simultaneously.
Primary: High Acuity
+ High Magnanimity + High Discourse
⚖
The Advocate
Magnanimous engagement move
Argues the strongest version of views they disagree with. The fullest expression of the Magnanimity pillar. The rarest archetype. Directly attacks the tribal default of strawmanning opposition.
Primary: High Magnanimity
Secondary: High Acuity
Advocate Engine feature
↻
The Reviser
Temporal move (change)
Publicly updates their position when given good reasons. The only archetype defined by change rather than a static pattern. Requires visible wave texture in early rings resolving into Forum — history of growth made legible.
Primary: High Calibration
+ High Consistency
+ visible early-tier wave texture

Layer 4 — Growth Engine

Growth Engine Inputs

The Growth Layer operates on data from all prior layers plus user-authored aspiration inputs. These are the inputs that enable pillar-level coaching and fingerprint delta tracking.

Input Source What it enables
Stage A analysis fields (per comment) Classification Engine Raw material for all six pillar scores. Acuity (claim specificity), Emotion, Tribal Markers, Article Engagement, Opposing View (Magnanimity) feed directly into pillar computation.
Assigned tier (per comment) Classification Engine Drives tier-mix texture in each petal. High-Forum histories produce smooth curves; earlier Heat/Stance produces visible wave texture in inner rings.
AI suggestion vs. self-declaration delta Classification Engine + User Underused signal: tracks whether a contributor's self-assessment is aligned with the engine's read. Feeds Calibration pillar over time.
Six pillar scores + fingerprint shape Identity Layer Current state and trajectory. Stored at aspiration declaration to create a "from" baseline. Stored at each recommitment to show movement.
Aspiration statement (verbatim) User-authored Must be stored verbatim. Surfaced back to the user verbatim in Consent Renewal Loop prompts. Never paraphrased by the platform.
Pillar commitments (user-named) User-authored The critical specificity layer the aspiration alone lacks. "I want to deepen Acuity while growing Magnanimity" gives the engine a ranked pillar target list for coaching prioritization.
Target archetype (if aspiration-anchored) User-authored Translates to specific pillar movement requirements. Engine maps archetype signature to coaching priority order.
Declaration fingerprint (snapshot at declaration) Identity Layer + Growth Layer The "from" in the growth arc. Delta computation compares current fingerprint to this baseline — the only honest measure of whether growth happened.
Recommitment events + revised snapshots Growth Layer Each recommitment stores a new fingerprint snapshot. 90-day cycle or meaningful fingerprint shift, whichever comes first. Over time these become the visualization data — the arc of movement made visible.
Practice Layer engagement (opt-in) Growth Layer Sparse supplementary data. Which exercises were engaged, deferred, or declined. Treated as signal, not structure — many contributors grow without touching the Practice Layer.
