## Question

Does Dan retain a Connecticut lawyer for one hour before launch to answer the two questions
below, or does he accept both unanswered and proceed?

## What I already checked

Fourteen sources read and filed on 2026-09-20 under `council/legal/research/`, including the
enrolled text of Connecticut Public Act 25-113, the Third Circuit's slip opinion in Anderson v.
TikTok, Congressional Research Service reports R46751 and R47753, and the Connecticut Attorney
General's own CTDPA page. Positions at `council/legal/positions/`.

Everything that could be settled from sources has been settled. The two questions below cannot
be, and this file exists because guessing at them would be the exact failure this seat was
created to avoid.

**Question one, and it is the larger.** Is a tier name published beside a named contributor's
comment capable of defamatory meaning under Connecticut law, and does the opinion privilege carry
where the platform publishes its reasoning beside the label?

Why it cannot be answered here. Section 230 does not shield the label; that part is settled from
the statute and from CRS R46751. The whole of the platform's remaining defence is that a tier is
the platform's characterisation resting on fully disclosed facts, which is a state law question
about the elements of a tort applied to a specific published artifact. It is the largest
unsourced load in this tree and the reading list now carries a `todo` row for it that no
non-lawyer source will close.

**Question two.** Does a media liability policy's defamation cover reach statements generated by
the insured's own model?

Why it cannot be answered here. The answer is in an endorsement, not in any statute. This matters
because the standing recommendation is to accept the tier label risk and insure the defence cost
rather than redesign the mechanic. If the cover excludes model-generated statements, that
recommendation has no mitigation behind it and changes.

## Why I am stuck

The constraint this seat argues inside is that Dialecta has one operator, no counsel on retainer
and no revenue. A control requiring a compliance function does not exist here, and this file is
not asking for one.

The case for buying the hour. Both questions are narrow enough to answer in an hour if the lawyer
is sent the two specs and one screenshot. Question two should be put to the broker in writing
before binding a policy rather than to a lawyer at all, which makes it nearly free. Question one
decides whether the platform's core mechanic is ordinary or is its largest unshielded exposure,
and every week of building on top of it raises the cost of learning the answer late.

The case against. The realistic Connecticut exposure is small: the CTDPA has no private right of
action, the Attorney General enforces it alone, penalties run to 5,000 dollars per violation, and
the platform has fourteen members. A defamation claim needs a plaintiff who feels injured by a
word on a site almost nobody reads. Spending on counsel now is spending against a risk whose
probability is currently set by the platform's own obscurity.

Where I land, for what a non-lawyer's read is worth. Ask the broker question two in writing,
because it costs an email and it decides whether the mitigation exists. Hold question one until
there is a reason to spend, and treat the reason as one of three triggers: sign-up opens to
strangers, the first contributor writes in to dispute a published label, or money changes hands.
Any of the three turns a theoretical plaintiff into a specific one.

That is a recommendation to accept a risk rather than to mitigate it, and it should be read as
one. It is Dan's to overrule.

### legal

Appended 2026-09-20, same day, correcting my own record. Records are append-only while open, so
the questions above stand as written and this is the correction.

**Dialecta is operated from Arizona, not Connecticut.** Dan said so in session. That changes both
questions and adds a third.

**Question one becomes an Arizona question, and it is now better defined.** Whether a tier name
is capable of defamatory meaning, and whether the disclosed-basis opinion defence carries, is
governed by Turner v. Devlin, 174 Ariz. 201 (1993), and Yetman v. English, 168 Ariz. 71 (1991),
against the federal floor in Milkovich v. Lorain Journal Co., 497 U.S. 1 (1990). Filed at
`council/legal/research/1990-scotus-milkovich-v-lorain-journal.md`. The sharpest form of it: is
the Breach notice provable as false when the text it characterises is never shown?

**Question two is unchanged and is still the cheap one.** Ask the broker in writing whether a
media liability policy reaches statements the insured's own model generated.

**Question three is new, and it came out of Dan's own question about the Pact.** Does Arizona
follow Restatement (Second) of Torts Section 583 on consent as a complete defence to defamation,
and can the Pact carry that effect when nothing in it is drafted as a contract? Consent is the
second defence under the tier badge and it may be the stronger one, because
`components/dialecta-pact.html` shows a contributor all seven tiers and makes them classify three
comments before asking for the commitment. If the answer is that a short clause appended to the
Pact would secure it, that is an hour well spent and it costs the ceremony nothing. Full analysis
at `council/legal/positions/2026-09-20-consent-waiver-and-the-pact.md`.

**One thing gets cheaper and one gets clearer.** Cheaper: there is no home-state privacy statute
to advise on, because Arizona has never enacted one. Clearer: the recommendation to hold question
one until there is a reason to spend now has a fourth trigger alongside the three above, which is
any move to draft terms, because questions one and three would be answered in the same hour as
the drafting.
