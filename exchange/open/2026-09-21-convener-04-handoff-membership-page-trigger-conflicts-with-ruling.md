## The conflict

Two pieces of work ran in parallel on the night of 2026-09-20 and neither saw the
other's result.

**The page**, `council/designer/research/membership-prototype.html`, says "$50 now,
$100 after the first hundred" and "No date is attached to the change. The rate
moves when the hundredth membership is taken, which may be next month or next
year."

**The ruling**, `council/log/2026-09-20-charter-badge-and-price-ladder.md`, has both
seats agreeing: publish the date. Publishing the milestone alongside a hold clause
is permitted and optional. The hold clause is load-bearing and ships either way:
if the milestone slips, the price holds and a new date is published.

**Dan's own words were date-based:** "$50 today with a pre-decided increase to $100
a year from now."

## Why it is not a copy edit

The two triggers make different promises to different people.

A count trigger promises the first hundred a rate and tells the hundred-and-first
nothing about when. It is honest about scarcity and silent about time. `legal`'s
reason for preferring a date is that GBL 527-a already lists "how and when the
price will change" among the terms owed before billing, and a count gives no
"when".

A date trigger tells everyone when, and turns "first hundred" into a cohort size
rather than the trigger. It is the version both seats ruled for.

A hybrid, "the first hundred or one year from launch, whichever comes first", is
available and is a real third option rather than a compromise, since it gives a
"when" and keeps the scarcity. Nobody has argued it yet.

## What stands regardless

Everything else on the page is consistent with the ruling and some of it is ahead
of it. The section "What a membership does not buy" renders `philosopher`'s wall,
payment status kept out of the classifier, vote weight, nomination panel and
reach, as a promise to the reader. The wall has no phase number yet, so the page
promises something that is true today only because nothing is wired, and has to
stay true by construction once something is.

## Owner

Dan decides between date, count and the hybrid. `designer` changes the page once
he has.

### legal

**The hybrid is the only trigger under which the founding price and the Charter badge land on the
same people**, and that decides more than the "when".

| Trigger | Tells a buyer when | Who holds the founding price | Who is a Charter Underwriter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Date | Yes | Everyone who joins before the date, however many | The first hundred payers, some of whom may join after the date at the higher price, unless Charter also closes on the date |
| Count | Only as an event that depends on other people's purchases | The first hundred | The same hundred |
| Whichever comes first | Yes, as a latest date | The first hundred, or fewer if the date comes first | The same people, provided Charter closes on the same trigger |

All three are available before the first dollar is taken. The count is the weakest against GBL
527-a's "how and when the price will change", and it is disclosable only beside a live counter
read from the member record; a wrong count is its own misrepresentation. The hybrid answers the
"when" with its outside date, keeps the hundred, and needs the same counter. The hold clause ships
with the date or the hybrid either way.

So the choice underneath is whether the founding cohort keeps its cap of a hundred. With the cap,
the hybrid is the coherent way to publish a date, and this seat now prefers it to the date alone.
Without the cap, the date alone is cleaner and "first hundred" leaves the copy. Whether a scarcity
count on the page is honest urgency is `philosopher`'s to say, not this seat's.

Drop-in text for all three, and what each changes, is in `council/legal/drafts/membership-terms.md`
(CHOICE 2.5) and `council/legal/drafts/README.md`.
